**74 rows: 33 scored, 2 conditional, 12 consistency, 27 info. Scored: 30 OK, 3 FAIL** (D3.3, D3.4, D3.7), all shared with LambdaCDM. Of the 33 scored rows, 13 test GUID-specific content (the information scalar and Bridge B) and 20 test content GUID shares with general relativity, LambdaCDM or quantum mechanics. Conditional on the Starobinsky/Higgs plateau potential: OK D4.4; FAIL D4.3. The consistency rows were set from their own data and are never counted as successes. If the scored rows were independent, a correct theory would fail about 1.7 of them by chance at 95%; but D3.3 and D3.7 both use DESI DR2 BAO with the CMB, D3.4 rests on the CMB fit of LambdaCDM, and the conditional D4.3 uses the CMB with DESI DR1 BAO: the FAILs are correlated, and the chance expectation assumes an independence that does not hold. Method checks of the script's own numerics: 6 of 6.

Kinds: **scored** rows confront a prediction with a datum it was not set from: **OK** if GUID is consistent with the datum at 95% (|pull| < 1.96, or the GUID value inside the allowed region; rows at 90% CL say so), **FAIL** otherwise, also when general relativity, LambdaCDM or the Standard Model share it. **Conditional** rows apply the same test to a model Part I names but GUID does not derive. **Consistency** rows (CONS) hold by construction, because the prediction was set from the datum. **INFO** rows are superseded, derived, overlapping or without a usable uncertainty. The reason for every non-scored kind is given with the row.

Every measured number was checked on 2026-10-31 against the arXiv text or abstract of its source; rows read only through secondary sources (a web search of the journal record, or a review that quotes the value) are listed in the citations.

## Summary

- **Consistent and distinctive of GUID:** none yet has data that could tell GUID apart. The information scalar is consistent with every datum: free fall bounds its coupling to |lambda| < 3.1e-05 (95% CL; version 5.1's 2e-5 is the one-sigma-level value), and the drift of alpha is consistent with zero. The data say **nothing about the sign** of the drift: the newest clock value is (1.8 +- 2.5)e-19 /yr, and the joint fit of a slowly rolling scalar gives (1.8e-19 +- 2.5e-19) /yr, P(negative) = 0.24. Part I's action (no potential, the scalar driven by the electrostatic energy of baryons) predicts a drift that is negative, as GUID's forward statement says, but of size <~ 1e-23 /yr, four orders of magnitude below the clocks: the forward statement is derived in that model and untestable. If the scalar instead rolled with DESI's dark energy (1 + w0 ~ 0.16), the clocks would force |lambda| < 2e-08. Bridge B (the Diosi-Penrose rate) is consistent with every superposition made so far, by factors of 4e+12 and more, and with the radiation bounds once the smearing length is R0 >= 4e-10 m.
- **Consistent, not distinctive (shared with general relativity, quantum mechanics):** gamma = beta = 1, the Nordtvedt and strong-field equivalence tests, the gravitational redshift, the speed and mass of gravitational waves (D2, 10 scored rows, chi2 = 4.7 for 8 Gaussian pulls); Soltan efficiencies inside the Kerr range and the area theorem (D5); Tsirelson's bound (D9).
- **Consistent by construction, not counted (prediction set from the datum):** the smearing length of Bridge B, the black-hole window, monopoles and proton decay, alpha(M_Z) near 1/128, the electroweak crossover, the neutrinos and the gravitational-wave background as non-remnants, the Kerr bound on measured spins.
- **In tension (all shared with LambdaCDM, and correlated):** DESI DR2's evolving dark energy against the de Sitter floor (2.8 to 4.2 sigma; 3.2 sigma with recalibrated DES supernovae) [D3.3]; the Hubble tension against SH0ES (4.9 sigma) while the TRGB value agrees [D3.4, D3.5]; DESI DR2 + CMB curvature at 2.1 sigma [D3.7]. Conditional on the plateau potential Part I names: ACT DR6 + lensing + BAO puts n_s = 0.974 +- 0.003, which the leading-order predictions reach only for N >= 63 (2.4 sigma at N = 60) [D4.3].
- **No data yet:** gravitationally mediated entanglement (Bose-Marletto-Vedral), the LOCC test, the decoherence-diffusion trade-off at the needed sensitivity, Bridge B's decoherence, single-graviton clicks, primordial tensor modes, the Hawking glow at the light edge of the window, the return of information, proton decay (table below).

## Method

**D1, the information scalar.** Part I's action gives the scalar phi a kinetic term normalised to the reduced Planck mass and a coupling lambda = d ln alpha / d phi. A body whose mass has electrostatic share f carries the scalar charge lambda f, so a pair falling towards the Earth differs by eta = 2 lambda^2 f_Earth (f_1 - f_2) (Eq. fifth); shares from the Coulomb term of the semi-empirical mass formula as in checks.py [Q6] (Ti 2.022e-03, Pt 4.054e-03, Be 4.893e-04, Earth 1.708e-03; the MICROSCOPE alloys TA6V and PtRh10 change the difference by about 3%). The bound on lambda is a profile likelihood with lambda^2 >= 0 (Delta chi^2 = 3.84). Two cosmological models: **Model A** is Part I's action as written, with no potential: the scalar obeys phi'' + 3 H phi' = -lambda f_b rho_b, driven only by the electrostatic energy of baryons (dark matter, a gravitational remnant, carries none). It is solved in flat LambdaCDM (Planck 2018); the attractor gives phi'(today) = -0.141 lambda f_b, so d ln alpha/dt = -9.70e-12 lambda^2 f_b per year, negative for f_b > 0; f_b <= 1e-3 is used as a generous upper value (He-4 Coulomb energy alone gives 6e-5). This is BSBM-like evolution (Sandvik, Barrow and Magueijo) with a positive electrostatic share, which drives alpha down. **Model B** gives the scalar a linear potential and makes it the (thawing) dark energy: it is frozen by Hubble friction early and rolls late, with phi'^2 = 3 Omega_phi (1 + w) exactly. For small slopes the history has a universal shape: Delta alpha/alpha(z) = -(alpha-dot/alpha today / H0) G(z) with G(z_Oklo = 0.137) = 0.118, G(1.15) = 0.435, G(6.3) = 0.514, G(>=1100) = 0.516 (for 1 + w0 = 0.16 the same numbers change by less than 2%). Every alpha datum is then a measurement of the present drift; the joint fit is their weighted mean. In Model B the drift and lambda are independent parameters linked only by the field speed, drift = lambda H0 phi'_0, which the dark energy's 1 + w0 fixes; the allowed region is shown in `data_alpha_scalar.pdf`. Oklo's 1.8 Gyr is the elapsed time implied by Damour and Dyson's quoted bounds and rates. The clock value of Filzinger et al. 2023 includes the data of Lange et al. 2021, so only the newer value enters the fit.

**D2.** Pulls against general relativity's values. The LLR errors of Biskupek et al. are read as 1 sigma (their paper does not say; this is the stricter reading). The pulsar SEP bound is converted from 95% to 1 sigma. Of overlapping pairs one row is scored: Cassini (D2.1) for gamma, the ephemeris combination (D2.3) for beta. The combined chi2 is indicative only, since the lunar rows come from one fit.

**D3.** GUID's late universe is flat LambdaCDM with a de Sitter floor. The transition redshift follows from Omega_m. Its published determinations (cosmic chronometers; H(z) compilations, whose weighted mean depends mainly on the H0 prior; SNe Ia + H(z), whose three parametrisations are merged with their half-range as a systematic) disagree with each other, so D3.1 is reported as inconclusive and not scored (rows D3.1a to D3.1d give the individual pulls). Of the two flatness data, the newer (DESI DR2 with the CMB, D3.7) is scored. The DESI significances are the collaboration's (Delta chi^2 for two extra parameters, converted to Gaussian sigma); the check scores the least significant combination. H_inf is derived (H_inf^2 = H0^2 - (100 km/s/Mpc)^2 omega_m) and is reported, not scored: it exists only if w = -1.

**D4.** Leading-order plateau predictions n_s = 1 - 2/N, r = 12/N^2 for Starobinsky and Higgs inflation, N = 50 to 60. These rows are conditional on that potential, which Part I names but GUID does not derive. The n_s datum is ACT DR6 + Planck + lensing + DESI DR1 BAO (it includes the Planck data, so Planck alone and P-ACT are reported). A prediction band passes when some N in [50, 60] is within 95%; the pull quoted is the one at the best N.

**D5.** Novikov-Thorne efficiency 1 - E_isco from Bardeen, Press and Teukolsky, signed spin (retrograde a < 0): 3.8% (a = -1), 5.7% (a = 0), 42.3% (a -> 1). The Soltan efficiencies are read on that curve to give the typical spin. The spin tables are entered as 90% ranges (lower limits as [limit, 1]).

**D6.** Bridge B's rate for each experiment is computed two ways, and the larger is used: Part I's G M^2/(hbar R) and the full Diosi-Penrose self-energy difference E_Delta = (G/2) int int (rho_1 - rho_2)(rho_1' - rho_2')/|x - x'| for the actual separation, a uniform sphere for the bulk plus each nucleus smeared as a Gaussian of width R0 = 0.54e-10 m (the smallest allowed; larger R0 lowers the rate). Single atoms are their nucleus smeared over R0. The closed forms are checked against Fourier quadrature. An experiment is consistent if the predicted decoherence during its observed coherence time is below 0.1. Particle radii of the molecules are estimates from mass and density (1300 kg/m^3 for the porphyrins; 8 nm diameter stated for the Na clusters); they enter linearly and are far from mattering.

**D8.** Tree-level masses from alpha-hat(M_Z) = 1/127.950, sin^2 theta-hat = 0.23129 and v = (sqrt2 G_F)^(-1/2) = 246.22 GeV, Part I's route; two other standard tree-level routes show the scheme spread. Part I's claim, agreement within half a per cent, holds only in that route, so these rows are reported as tree-level Standard Model values (INFO).

## Corrections made to version 5.1 (applied in Part I of the v6 paper)

1. Version 5.1, Section 4.1, Eq. (lambdabound): |lambda| < 2e-5 is the one-sigma-level value (|eta| < 2.7e-15); the 95% CL profile-likelihood bound from MICROSCOPE and Eot-Wash is |lambda| < 3.1e-05.

6. Section 11.6 (3): DESI DR2 bounds sum m_nu < 0.064 eV, so the neutrinos are below 0.6% of the dark matter.
7. Section 11.5: the DESI DR2 range 2.8 to 4.2 sigma is correct; the DES supernova recalibration (Popovic et al. 2025/2026) lowers its end to 3.2 sigma. The Hubble tension (4.9 sigma against SH0ES) and the DESI curvature (2.1 sigma) are inherited LambdaCDM tensions and could be named alongside.
