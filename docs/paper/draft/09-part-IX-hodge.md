# Part IX. Hodge: the card, the positivity, and the one open step

*Draft 4. Prose, with every result marked. Lean paths relative to `docs/mass-gap/formal/ProofPhase/`; notes relative to
`docs/unified/`. Claim labels (IX.n) are listed in `../CLAIMS.md`.*

## IX.0 What is proved, by whom, and what this part does

**The Clay problem** (Deligne's description) **[classical]**. Let X be a projective non-singular algebraic variety over the complex
numbers. A **Hodge class** is a class in the rational cohomology `H^{2p}(X, Q)` whose image in `H^{2p}(X, C)` lies in the `(p, p)` part
of the Hodge decomposition. The class of an algebraic subvariety of codimension p is a Hodge class. **The Hodge conjecture says the
converse: every Hodge class is a rational linear combination of classes of algebraic subvarieties.**

**State of the problem (2026-11-24).** The conjecture is a theorem for p = 1 (Lefschetz 1924), and by hard Lefschetz for curves (degree
2n - 2). So it holds for every variety of dimension at most 3 **[classical]**. It is **open** for codimension p >= 2 in general; the first
open case is the fourth cohomology of fourfolds **[open]**. Beyond that, only special families are known, mostly abelian varieties
(IX.1). The notes record no claimed proof of the general case.

**This part proves no case of the Hodge conjecture that was not already known.** The Lean proves the linear algebra of the positivity
and the diagram chases of Lefschetz (1,1). The geometry (Hodge theory, the exponential sequence, hard Lefschetz, intersection theory on a
surface) enters as named `External` slots. The conjecture itself is the card's one `OpenStep`.

**What this part does.** It puts Hodge on the DMP API as a card (IX.2), proves what Mathlib can reach (IX.3), records what is
borrowed (IX.4), states the one open step (IX.5), and attaches the first author's readings, short (IX.6). Hodge is also the place
where the **same positivity** that the Riemann hypothesis over finite fields needs appears as a theorem over the complex numbers. IX.3.4
and IX.3.5 show the shape for curves in Lean.

## IX.1 The problem as stated, and what is known

**IX.1.1 What is proved** (recalled from the literature; not re-read for this draft).

| Case | Result | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| p = 1 (divisors) | **Lefschetz (1,1)** (Lefschetz 1924; sheaf proof Kodaira-Spencer 1953): an integral class in `H^2(X, Z)` is of type `(1,1)` exactly when it is the first Chern class of a holomorphic line bundle; on a projective X every line bundle is `O(D)` for a divisor D. So the conjecture holds for p = 1, even with integer coefficients | classical |
| p = n - 1 (curves) | hard Lefschetz `L^{n-2} : H^2 -> H^{2n-2}` is an isomorphism of Hodge structures and sends divisor classes to curve classes; so the rational conjecture holds in degree 2n - 2 | classical |
| dimension at most 3 | follows from the two rows above: the only degrees with `(p, p)` classes are p = 0, 1, n - 1, n | classical |
| abelian varieties whose Hodge ring is generated by divisor classes | the conjecture holds; this covers a very general abelian variety (Mattuck) and simple abelian varieties of prime dimension (Tankeev). Mumford's example and Weil's classes show that not every abelian variety is of this kind | classical / external, recalled |
| abelian fourfolds of Weil type | Weil classes are algebraic: Markman, arXiv 2502.03415 (February 2025: abelian sixfolds of discriminant -1, and by a degeneration argument all abelian fourfolds of Weil type) and arXiv 2509.23403 (September 2025, whose abstract reports the algebraicity on all abelian fourfolds of Weil type and that the conjecture for abelian varieties of dimension at most 5 then follows); other proofs of the discriminant-1 case by Floccari-Fu (arXiv 2504.13607) and van Geemen-Rapagnetta (arXiv 2607.18341) | external, abstracts and search records only, not checked |
| other families | cubic fourfolds (Zucker), uniruled fourfolds (Conte-Murre), some Fermat hypersurfaces (Shioda) | external, recalled |

**IX.1.2 What fails** **[external, recalled]**. Two natural strengthenings are false, so "described" must be read exactly.

* **Integral coefficients.** "Every integral class whose rational image is Hodge is algebraic" is false. Atiyah and Hirzebruch (1962)
  found torsion classes in `H^4(X, Z)` that are not algebraic (the degree is as recalled, the paper not read; Totaro 1997 gave the cobordism reading). Kollar (1992) found
  non-torsion failures: integral classes on hypersurfaces that are not algebraic though a multiple is.
* **Non-projective Kaehler manifolds.** On a general complex torus there are Hodge classes but too few analytic subvarieties (Zucker 1977).
  Voisin (2002) showed that even with Chern classes of coherent sheaves the statement fails on some complex tori. Projectivity is part
  of the hypothesis.

**IX.1.3 The standard conjectures and the positivity** **[classical; open where marked]**. Grothendieck's standard conjectures (1969) are
four statements for a Weil cohomology of a smooth projective variety.

* **B (Lefschetz type):** the inverse of hard Lefschetz is given by an algebraic correspondence. Known for abelian varieties
  (Lieberman); implied by the Hodge conjecture in characteristic 0. **[open in general]**
* **C (Kunneth type):** the Kunneth projectors are algebraic. Over finite fields it follows from the Weil conjectures (Katz-Messing 1974).
* **D:** numerical and homological equivalence agree. **[open in general]**
* **Hodge type:** on primitive algebraic classes of codimension `p <= n/2` the form `(-1)^p <x, L^{n-2p} x>` is positive definite.

The Hodge-type conjecture is a **positivity**. **Over the complex numbers it is a theorem**: the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations make the form
definite on primitive Hodge classes, and algebraic classes are Hodge classes. **In characteristic p it is open**, except for surfaces,
where it is the Hodge index theorem (Hodge 1937; Segre; Grothendieck 1958 in every characteristic), and a few further cases (for
example Ancona 2021 for abelian fourfolds) **[external, recalled]**.

**The link to the Weil conjectures.** Weil proved the Riemann hypothesis for curves over a finite field in 1948 through the
Castelnuovo-Severi inequality, which Mattuck-Tate and Grothendieck (both 1958) showed follows from the Hodge index theorem on the surface `C x C`:
`abs(N - q - 1) <= 2 g sqrt q`. Grothendieck's plan was that the standard conjectures give it for every smooth projective variety by
the same positivity. **Deligne proved the Riemann hypothesis over finite fields in 1974 by another route** (Lefschetz pencils,
monodromy, Rankin's squaring trick), without the standard conjectures, which remain open **[classical]**.

**IX.1.4 What is open** **[open]**.

* The Hodge conjecture for p >= 2 in general, including general abelian varieties beyond the cases above.
* The standard conjecture B in general, D, and the Hodge type in positive characteristic.
* Any positivity route to the Riemann hypothesis over finite fields beyond surfaces (Deligne's theorem is proved; the route is not), and its
  analogue over Z.

## IX.3 What is proved

declarations rest on the three standard axioms (two, `sq_add_smul` and `transfer`, on two of them); the source has no `sorry`, `admit`,

### IX.3.1 A polarised Hodge structure, and positivity on its Hodge classes (`HodgeLinear`)

Cauchy-Schwarz holds for a symmetric form that is nonnegative on a subspace, over any ordered field (`sq_add_smul`, `cs_of_nonneg`).

`PolHS` is a real polarised Hodge structure of weight 0 in Deligne's circle form. It is a real representation `h` of the circle, whose
characters `z -> z^{p-q}` are the Hodge types; a symmetric form `Q` invariant under the circle; and the Hodge-Riemann positivity
`Q(C x, x) > 0` for `x` not zero, where `C = h(i)` is the Weil operator. A Tate twist brings weight 2p to weight 0. The **Hodge classes** are the vectors
fixed by the whole circle (`PolHS.hodge`, `mem_hodge`): charge 0. Proved:

* `Q` is positive definite on the Hodge classes (`PolHS.hodge_pos`, `hodge_nonneg`): on a fixed vector the Weil operator is the identity;
* it satisfies Cauchy-Schwarz there (`hodge_cs`), the encoder bound;
* the Hodge classes split off orthogonally: the space is their direct sum with their `Q`-orthogonal (`hodge_isCompl`, finite dimension).

That the cohomology of a smooth projective variety, with `Q` built from the cup product, an ample class and the Lefschetz signs, **is** such a
structure is the Hodge-Riemann theorem of Hodge theory **[classical]**, not in Mathlib and not proved here.

### IX.3.2 The card, and the positivity on algebraic classes (`HodgeAPI`)

* **The card** (`Input`, `HodgeConjecture`, `problem`): from `alg <= hodge` (an `External`) and the open step `hodge <= alg`, the conclusion
  `hodge = alg` follows by antisymmetry. The card adds no mathematics. It shows the shape: the content is all in the slot.
* **Positivity** (`PosInput`): a symmetric rational form `Q` and the Hodge-Riemann relations on rational Hodge classes, as an `External`. Then:
  `Q` is positive definite on algebraic classes (`PosInput.alg_pos`, `alg_nonneg`), which is an instance of **Grothendieck's Hodge standard conjecture in
  characteristic 0** (given that the `External` identifies `Q` with the Lefschetz-sign form on primitive classes); an algebraic class orthogonal to all algebraic classes is zero (`num_eq_hom`), which is conjecture D in this piece;
  and `Q(x,y)^2 <= Q(x,x) Q(y,y)` on algebraic classes (`alg_cs`), the encoder bound. **[Lean, from Hodge-Riemann]**
* **Where the external input comes from** (`hr_of_polHS`): a real polarised Hodge structure whose rational points carry `Q`, and whose Hodge
  classes contain the rational Hodge classes, gives exactly the `hr` slot. **[Lean]** The one thing outside is the theorem of Hodge theory
  that the cohomology of X is such a structure.

### IX.3.3 The Hodge index theorem as linear algebra (`HodgeLinear`)

`HodgeIndex B H` says every class orthogonal to `H` has nonpositive square. For the intersection form on the Neron-Severi group of a surface and `H`
ample this is the Hodge index theorem **[classical]**. Proved:

* **Signature gives the index property** (`hodgeIndex_of_sigPos`): if the form has no positive plane (signature `(1, *)`) and `H` is positive, a class orthogonal to
  `H` cannot be positive. The signature is Mathlib's, in Sylvester's law of inertia form.
* **The reversed Cauchy-Schwarz inequality** (`reverse_cs`): under the index property, `B(D,D) B(H,H) <= B(D,H)^2`. This is the Lorentzian twin
  of the encoder bound. **[Lean]**

### IX.3.4 Weil's bound for curves from the Hodge index property (`HodgeLinear`, `HodgeAPI`)

On `C x C` let `f1`, `f2` be the fibre classes, `Gamma` the graph of Frobenius and `Delta` the diagonal. The classical intersection numbers are an
`External` slot (`CurveInput.geometry`). Then, all **[Lean]**:

* **Castelnuovo-Severi** (`castelnuovo_severi`): the index property at `f1 + f2` gives `D^2 <= 2 (D f1)(D f2)` for every class `D`.
* **The quadratic form** (`weil_form`): with the intersection numbers, `g q a^2 - a b (N - q - 1) + g b^2 >= 0` for all real `a`, `b`.
* **Weil's bound** (`weil_bound`, `weil_bound_abs`): choosing `a = N - q - 1`, `b = 2 g q` gives `(N - q - 1)^2 <= 4 g^2 q`, so
  `abs(N - (q + 1)) <= 2 g sqrt q`.
* **The card** (`curveCard`): the open step is the index property at `f1 + f2`; the conclusion is Weil's bound.
* **The slot is filled for curves** (`curve_open`): with the Hodge index theorem in its signature form (one positive direction; Hodge 1937,
  Grothendieck 1958) as an `External`, the open step holds and Weil's bound follows. **[Lean from the external index theorem]**

Positivity runs the system and certifies the line, for curves. The full Riemann hypothesis for a curve also needs the bound for every extension
`F_{q^n}` and the rationality of the zeta function **[classical, not in the Lean]**.

### IX.3.5 A positive form puts the eigenvalues on a circle (`HodgeLinear`, `HodgeAPI`)

* If Frobenius `F` scales a positive-definite norm by `c`, every eigenvalue `mu` has `abs(mu)^2 = c` (`eigen_on_circle`). The same holds with a
  positive-definite Hermitian form for which `F` is a similitude (`eigen_on_circle_form`). **[Lean]**
* **The card** (`weilCard`): the open step `StandardHodge` is a form `S` with positive real diagonal on which Frobenius is a similitude of ratio `q^w`
  (the Lean does not require `S` to be Hermitian; a positive Hermitian form of this kind would make Frobenius normal, so the slot
  contains semisimplicity of Frobenius). It is a positivity statement of the shape the Riemann hypothesis needs, in the spirit of
  Grothendieck's Hodge standard conjecture but not that conjecture, which concerns primitive algebraic classes. The conclusion is every eigenvalue on the circle
  `abs(mu)^2 = q^w`. **[Lean from the open step]** Deligne (1974) proves the conclusion for l-adic cohomology by another route
  **[classical]**, so the card's conclusion is a theorem. Its open step is not known to hold in general.

### IX.3.6 Lefschetz (1,1) and hard Lefschetz, as diagram chases (`HodgeLinear`, `HodgeAPI`)

* **The chase** (`lefschetz11`): if `Pic -> H^2(X, Z) -> H^{0,2}` is exact at `H^2` (the exponential sequence) and an integral class has zero `(0,2)` part, it is
  the first Chern class of a line bundle.
* **Rational descent** (`rational_descent`): a rational class with a positive multiple equal to the image of an element of a set lies in the rational span of
  the images.
* **Divisors** (`DivisorInput`, `DivisorInput.toInput`, `DivisorInput.holds`, `DivisorInput.card`): from three `External` facts (exactness of the exponential
  sequence; a rational `(1,1)` class has an integral multiple with no `(0,2)` part; divisor classes are `(1,1)` and every line bundle on a projective X is
  `O(D)`), the open step of the Hodge card **holds for p = 1**, and the described classes are the rational span of divisor classes. **[Lean from the three
  external facts]**
* **Transfer** (`transfer`): if an isomorphism of Hodge structures has an inverse that keeps Hodge classes and a map that sends algebraic classes to algebraic
  classes, the Hodge statement on the source gives it on the target.
* **Curves** (`CurveClassInput`, `CurveClassInput.toInput`, `CurveClassInput.holds`): hard Lefschetz as an `External` carries the statement from divisors to curve
  classes. **[Lean from hard Lefschetz and the divisor inputs]**

So the card's slot is **not empty**: it is filled for p = 1 and for p = n - 1, which is exactly the classical range.

### IX.3.7 Describable must mean rational: the integral version fails (`HodgeLinear`, `HodgeAPI`)

A torsion class maps to 0 in rational cohomology, and 0 is a Hodge class. So the integral statement makes every torsion class algebraic
(`integral_needs_torsion`). With a non-algebraic torsion class as an `External` (Atiyah-Hirzebruch 1962), the integral statement is false
(`IntegralInput`, `IntegralHodge`, `integral_fails`). **[Lean from the Atiyah-Hirzebruch class]** The non-projective failure (Zucker, Voisin) is text
only: Mathlib has no complex manifolds beyond holomorphic functions.

### IX.3.8 What Mathlib has and lacks

Mathlib (v4.34.0, as the notes read it) has no Kaehler metric, no de Rham theorem, no Hodge decomposition, no Chow ring, no cycle class map and no Lefschetz
theorems. It has algebraic cycles on a scheme (new in 2026), sheaf cohomology, the signature of a quadratic form, eigenvalues and the circle. So the
core is finite-dimensional linear algebra, with the geometry as slots. **[paper, from the notes' search of the library]**

## IX.5 The one open step

**The open step of the card.** The Hodge card has one open step: **`HodgeConjecture`. On a smooth complex projective variety, every rational `(p, p)`
class is a rational linear combination of classes of algebraic cycles of codimension p.** It is proved for p = 1 and p = n - 1 (IX.3.6) by classical
theorems, and so for dimension at most 3. **For p >= 2 in general it is open.** Everything else in the card is proved (IX.3) or is a named external
theorem (IX.4). **[open]**

**The care points, stated exactly.**

* **Rational, not integral.** The integral statement is false (Atiyah-Hirzebruch; Kollar). The Lean says why in one line: torsion classes are
  automatically Hodge (`integral_needs_torsion`).
* **Projective, not merely Kaehler.** The statement fails on some complex tori (Zucker; Voisin).
* **The card adds no content.** `problem.proved` is antisymmetry of inclusion. The mathematics is the slot.

**The second slot, a different problem.** Over a finite field a positivity of the same shape is the open step of `weilCard` (IX.3.5): a form on
which Frobenius is a similitude, which puts the Frobenius eigenvalues on the circle and makes Frobenius semisimple. It is not known in general
(the Hodge standard conjecture it is modelled on is known for surfaces, the Hodge index theorem, and in special cases) **[open]**. The Riemann hypothesis over finite fields is nevertheless a theorem (Deligne), by a route that does not use that positivity **[classical]**.
So this slot's conclusion is known; only its route is open. Over the integers the analogue would be a Hodge index theorem on "`Spec Z x Spec Z`",
which does not exist as an object **[open]**. That missing positivity is the GRH card's sign slot (Part V).

**What this does to the problem.** The API does not settle Hodge. It states the conjecture as one inclusion in one slot, shows the classical range
filled, and shows the positivity that is a theorem over the complex numbers. The mechanical answer is: **open for p >= 2, with the open step located.**

## IX.6 DMP readings attached to this part (all [reading])

* **Describable implies exists.** The Hodge conjecture is the cleanest instance of the first author's sentence among the seven problems. The described
  objects are the rational `(p, p)` classes (cohomology describes them). The existing objects are the algebraic cycles (geometry builds them). The
  conjecture is `describable <= exists`; the converse is classical. In the API it is literally `OpenStep (hodge <= alg)` with `alg <= hodge` as an
  `External`.
* **It is not a logical triviality.** The first author anchored "what is described exists" in Goedel's completeness theorem (a consistent first-order theory
  has a model) **[classical]**. Hodge is the geometric version. Here a description is a cohomology class with a Hodge-type condition and existence is
  an algebraic cycle. No completeness theorem bridges them, which is why it is open.
* **Describable must mean rational, on a projective variety.** Integral "descriptions" include torsion classes, which every `(p, p)` description admits
  (their rational image is 0) but which need not exist as cycles. On non-projective Kaehler manifolds descriptions outrun existence. A description
  counts only after tensoring with Q, and only on a space with an ample class, the polarisation that makes the positivity available. The sentence holds at most in that
  form, and in that form it is the open conjecture. (Lean for the torsion half: `integral_fails`.)
* **Hodge types as charges on the G0 circle.** In Deligne's definition the circle acts on `H^{p,q}` by `z^{p-q}`: a Hodge type is a charge. The Hodge
  classes are the neutral vectors (`PolHS.hodge`, fixed by the circle). Described = neutral and rational; exists = built from subvarieties.
  (Lean for the model: `PolHS`, `mem_hodge`; the identification with DMP's G0 circle is the reading.)
* **The Hodge index route is more efficiently correct than Deligne's.** There are two complete proofs of the Riemann hypothesis over finite fields.
  Weil's (curves, 1948) is one positivity statement, the Hodge index theorem on `C x C` (`weil_bound`). Deligne's (all smooth projective varieties, 1974) reaches the same
  bound without a positivity statement on cycles. The first author prefers the first because it originates directly from G0 and so simplifies further. Both
  proofs are complete and correct. The difference is that the positivity route has **one moving part**, Cauchy-Schwarz on a positive form
  (`cs_of_nonneg`), the same shape as the encoder bound of Yang-Mills and of `PosInput.alg_cs`. That is why it transfers between problems.
  **Reach:** the positivity route is proved for curves and, for the index theorem, for surfaces in every characteristic. In higher dimension it needs
  the standard conjecture of Hodge type in characteristic p **[open]**, where Deligne's route covers everything. For GRH over number fields the missing
  piece is this route's analogue for `Spec Z` **[open]**. So choosing the route names the open step precisely; it does not close it.
* **The shared positivity with the GRH column.** "Exists implies positive" is, for Hodge, the Hodge-Riemann relations: on the described classes the
  polarisation is positive (`hodge_pos`, in the model), so on the existing classes too (`alg_pos`). Over C that positivity is proved. Over `F_q` the same
  positivity is Grothendieck's Hodge standard conjecture, and it would put the Frobenius eigenvalues on the critical circle (`eigen_on_circle_form`).
  For curves the index theorem on `C x C` supplies it (`curve_open`, `weil_bound`). The two columns have one shape: **a positive form, a dynamics that is a
  similitude of it, and the line as the consequence.** In GRH and in Hodge over `F_q` the positive form is the open step; over C, and for curves over
  `F_q`, it is a theorem.

## IX.7 What would close it

If an independent proof of the Hodge conjecture for p >= 2 were found, the card's open step would be discharged by that work. This part would supply
the shape and the audit. The change to this paper would be one row of the ledger, one verdict line of this part and one item of the introduction's list of open steps.
Nothing in this round proves any part of that.

The formalisation items that would change the status of the **borrowed** parts, none done and all named in the notes as reachable or far:

1. **Reachable on Mathlib, not built:** hard Lefschetz as the linear algebra of an `sl_2`-representation (an `sl_2`-triple on a graded space gives
   `L^k : V_{-k} ~ V_k` and the primitive decomposition); the Hodge-Riemann signature formula for a polarised Hodge structure of any weight;
   Hodge structures of weight k with the Hodge filtration; finiteness of lattice Hodge classes of bounded norm. **[paper, an estimate]**
2. **Far, needs geometry Mathlib lacks:** Kaehler manifolds, harmonic forms, the Hodge decomposition itself, the cycle class map, the
   exponential sequence on a complex manifold. **[paper, an estimate]**

## IX.8 The box in the one-box test

**Gate factor and marker.** In the Lean the box of an input (one fixed codimension `p`) has the ticks `0, 1, 2, ...`, every tick carries
the card's one open step `hodge <= alg`, the gate reads inside at a tick exactly when that step holds, and the marker is the claim
"inside". The box of an input passes exactly when the Hodge conjecture holds for it **[Lean]** (`hodge_box_iff`, `ETM/CardBox.lean`;
claim IX.8a). For divisors, codimension one, the box passes, from Lefschetz (1,1) with its named inputs **[Lean]** (from `External`;
`hodge_box_divisor`). The box is the card's open step in the test's language (II.4): it restates the step and proves no new bound, and
the content stays in the step **[paper]**. **[reading]** The first author names the ticks the **+dimensions** (in plain terms, the
