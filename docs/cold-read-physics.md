## 6. What this pass proves, and what no pass can

Asked whether a DMP instantiated for this universe can be proven not to be quantum gravity: no, and the question has no proof either way.
Quantum gravity is not a fixed object, and an instantiated DMP is not one until its dictionary, which quantity is which observable, is
written (E22); a non-identity between two undefined objects is not a theorem. What is proven, each in Lean under the pinned toolchain
or on a checked published result: every dictionary entry offered so far fails (rows R1 to R20, D0 to D1 and E1 to E35), and four features
of the chain stand in the way of any entry until the model changes. Any two ancestors of a line are comparable, so the order has no
spacelike pair (`cr_ancestors_are_comparable`). The gate's correlations sit at the classical bound under a local encoding and at four
otherwise, never at the quantum value (`gate_obeys_chsh`, `cr_cross_gated_pair_reaches_four`). The field's quantities are monotones with
no exchange (`cr_total_mass_never_decreases_under_extension`, `k4_refresh_never_raises`). No dimensionful constant arises in it; every
one in the drafts is imported (E4, R15). What is not proven, and not provable, is that no dictionary exists. Under DMP's own rule the
burden lies with the claim: "DMP is quantum gravity" is a claim, a claim is not evidence (Axiom 2), and its status is undecided until an
entry carries a number named before the data, at which point it becomes decidable, one entry at a time. The absence of a disproof is
not a proof, and the owner's standard for a part applies to the claim: "a claim beyond its proof is a refusal-worthy claim" (T35).
