## Question

Workstream A found (`../c2a-adjoint/NOTE.md` §5.1) that Leg C's flowed path-product block variable satisfies the halves criterion
(adjoint half + centre half < 1, hence D′ by the Lean theorem `limitDprime_of_halves`) from L√σ ≈ 2.5 on, but that its adjoint
coarse plaquette is zero within two errors there: the noise-limit trap (a near-Haar variable makes D′ cheap and moves the gap into
`ObsTransfer`). The question here:

> For SU(2) blocked with **the good blocking** of `formal/ProofPhase/RouteA/Blocking.lean` (reflection positive, not a decimation),
> at L√σ between 2 and 4.2, **does the block variable keep content** (adjoint and fundamental coarse plaquettes resolved, coarse
> Creutz ratio reproducing σ), and **where it does, does the fitted blocked action satisfy the halves criterion** in sup (TV) or
> linearised W₁ form, with the photon excluded through the centre half, deconfined Z₂ excluded, and no "not excluded" below the
> deconfinement edge?

**Claim discipline (RULES.md, round 4).** A counted NOT_EXCLUDED is a numerical statement about a **fitted, truncated** blocked
action: that action satisfies D′ (Lean: `limitDprime_of_halves`, then `decay_of_flatW`). It is weaker than the mass gap and is not a
proof of `AdjointHalf ∧ CentreHalf` (blueprint P4.1′) for the true blocked theory; the distance is H1Local (true vs fitted action),
the truncation, and `ObsTransfer`. With content, `ObsTransfer` is the cheap direction (workstream 1 §2), which is why the content
check is mandatory. Yang–Mills is in the hypotheses of what is measured (SU(2) Wilson measure, the good blocking).

## The block variable

Exactly `smearBlock w` of `Blocking.lean`, iterated (`smearIter`): one step maps links of length l to links of length 2l. The
coarse link (y, μ) is the projection of

  `straight(y, μ) + w · Σ_{ν spatial, ν ≠ μ} Σ_{s = ±} staple_{ν,s}(y, μ)`,

straight = the path y → y + 2lμ; staple_{ν,±} = y → y ± lν → y ± lν + 2lμ → y + 2lμ (each step a level-(k−1) link); 4 staples for
a spatial link, 6 for a temporal one. Projection: SU(2) `A ↦ A/√det A` (= q/|q| for the quaternion sum; identity on the null set
q = 0), U(1) the angle of the sum, Z₂ its sign. Spatial coarse links use only their fine time slice, temporal coarse links only
their time strip (temporal decimation); direction 0 is time. Block size b = 2ⁿ. All even fine sites serve as coarse origins
(the blocking commutes with translations, so every origin gives a configuration of the same blocked law): (b/2)⁴ coarse lattices
per configuration, each a periodic (N/b)⁴ lattice.

**Checked (`goodblock.py selftest`, CPU, all PASS, `cpu-test/selftest.log`):** the array code equals an independent scalar path
recursion at levels 1–3 for spatial and temporal links (10⁻¹⁶); w = 0 equals Leg C's decimation (`smearBlock_zero`); gauge
covariance (SD sums, plaquettes and loops invariant, SU(2) and U(1)); **time reflection commutes with the blocking**
(`smearBlock_thetaConn`, numerically, 10⁻¹⁶); the SU(2) blocking restricted to the U(1) subgroup and to the centre equals the U(1)
and Z₂ blockings; the anisotropic SD sums add up to Leg C's isotropic sums; the anisotropic SD fit recovers Wilson β at b = 1 and
two known anisotropic mixed actions generated by an independent Metropolis (SU(2) (1.2, 0.7, 0.6, 0.2) → (1.202, 0.706, 0.608,
0.196) ± (0.006, 0.005, 0.010, 0.012)); the anisotropic Z₂ pseudo-likelihood recovers Wilson β; the class-resolved pair tables add
up to Leg C's table and, for isotropic couplings, the halves equal `evaluate_adjoint.quantities` (3·10⁻¹⁶). The GPU path is tested
by `selftest --gpu` as the queue's first step (GPU = CPU on blocking, sums and loops; chain extraction).

### The weight w (rule fixed before the one-loop scan was run)

`wscan.py` evaluates workstream 1's linearised abelian flux variance σ²(ℓ) of the coarse plaquette (`../ws1-routeA/selfenergy.py`,
imported unchanged) for w ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4} at ℓ = 4, 8, 16, both orientations (time–space, space–space).
Smaller σ² means a coarse plaquette closer to 1 at one loop (⟨W⟩ ≈ e^{−g²σ²/2}): more content, less noise. **Rule:** w is the
smallest grid weight (w > 0) such that (i) σ²(16) is within 5 % of the grid minimum for both orientations, (ii) σ²(16) ≤ σ²(8)
for both (no residual growth with ℓ), and (iii) w is not a Z₂ tie weight (1/6, 1/4, 1/2: at these a Z₂ path sum `s₀ + w·(even
integer)` can vanish, and the projection is undefined on a set of positive measure). If no grid weight passes, w = 1 (ws1's
largest tested weight) and the report says so.

**Result (`wscan.log`, `wscan.json`): w = 2.** σ²(16), time–space / space–space: w = 0.5: 0.276 / 0.239; 1: 0.220 / 0.178;
1.5: 0.206 / 0.165; **2: 0.201 / 0.161**; 3: 0.197 / 0.162; 4: 0.196 / 0.163 (grid minima 0.196, 0.161); decimation 15.1. At w = 2
the straight path carries 1/9 (spatial) and 1/13 (temporal) of the weight; Z₂ sums are odd integers, never zero. At w = 2 the
variance still falls with ℓ (time–space 0.242, 0.216, 0.201; space–space 0.207, 0.180, 0.161 at ℓ = 4, 8, 16): the blocking's own
smoothing grows a little faster than ℓ. This matters for the photon gate (item 2 there).

## Fit and quantities (`analyse_refit.py`; its docstring is the reference)

* **Anisotropic basis.** The good blocking is not hypercubic (spatial smearing, temporal decimation), so time–space (ts) and
  space–space (ss) plaquettes carry separate couplings. SU(2): `fund_c = ½tr V_p`, `adj_c = χ₁(V_p)/3`, `s32_c = χ_{3/2}(V_p)/4` for
  c ∈ {ts, ss}, and the 1×2 rectangle `½tr V_R` (pooled over orientations). U(1): `cos θ_p, cos 2θ_p, cos 3θ_p` per class, `cos θ_R`.
  Z₂: plaquette per class, rectangle; pseudo-likelihood. **Fits:** base {F_ts, F_ss, A_ts, A_ss}; extensions + spin-3/2 (both
  classes), + rect, + both. SD identities (Leg C's derivation, sums split by class). Errors: jackknife, ≥ 20 blocks within chains.
* **Centre split** (`../c2a-adjoint/NOTE.md` §1): per plaquette, centre-blind `β_A(c)(4t² − 1)/3`, centre-charged
  `β_F(c) t + β_T(c)(2t³ − t)`; rectangles centre-charged. U(1): `q2 cos 2θ` blind, `q1 cos θ + q3 cos 3θ` charged. Z₂: all charged.
* **TV halves** for a link z of each type (temporal, spatial): `a(z, λ) = Σ_{i≠z} tanh(S⁰_{iz}/2) e^{λd}` with the oscillations of
  the centre-blind parts of the factors shared by i and z (pair table enumerated with classes on 7⁴; its class-pooled form equals
  Leg C's 62-neighbour table, checked), `c(z, λ)` likewise with the centre-charged parts. **γ_A = max_z a, γ_C = max_z c,
  H_TV = γ_A + γ_C** (the hypothesis of `limitDprime_of_halves` is `γ_A + γ_C < 1` with each half uniform over links).
  `λ*_H = sup{λ : H_TV(λ) < 1}`; **Y/X_TV** = 1/s*, s* the factor on both adjoint couplings at which H_TV = 1 (∞ if γ_C ≥ 1).
* **W₁ halves (linearised, SU(2) sites, geodesic cost):** `a_W(z) = 3·(8/3)·k_A·Σ_{p∋z}|β_A(p)|`,
  `c_W(z) = 3·Σ_{p∋z}(k_F|β_F(p)| + k_T|β_T(p)|) + 90 k_F|β_R|`, max over link types per half; k_A ∈ [2√2/(15π), 1/(5π)]
  (workstream A, proved), k_F = 8/(9π), k_T ∈ [0, 0.144]; hi/lo from the brackets. Isotropic couplings give workstream A's formulas
  exactly. Per point also: whether both |β_A| + 2 err < 0.28 (the nonlinear W₁ ceiling, workstream A §2.3).
* **Dimension swap** (workstream A §4b): λ*_H ≤ 4.0·L√σ; a violation marks a fit artefact (flag).
* **Closure** of the base fit for each unfitted basis function (reported).

**Verdict per point, criterion (H_TV, a_TV, c_TV, H_W, a_W, c_W) and fit:** NOT_EXCLUDED if value_hi + 2·stat < 1; EXCLUDED if
value_lo − 2·stat > 1; else UNDECIDED. The point's verdict needs the base fit **and every extension fit** to agree, else UNDECIDED
(Leg C, workstream A). NOT_EXCLUDED is biased towards false positives (omitted terms add oscillation); EXCLUDED is robust.

## The content check (mandatory; a point counts only if it passes)

Per SU(2) point (block size b, L = b·a):

* **C1, fundamental:** the coarse plaquette `⟨½tr V_p⟩` of **both** plane classes (ts and ss) exceeds 3 jackknife errors.
* **C2, adjoint:** the adjoint coarse plaquette `⟨χ₁(V_p)/3⟩` of both classes exceeds 3 errors.
* **C3, σ:** the Creutz ratio of the **half-block lattices** (level n − 1, coarse spacing L/2, all of their sublattices),
  `χ = −ln(W(2,2)W(1,1)/(W(2,1)W(1,2)))` pooled over the six planes, divided by σ(L/2)² = (b/2)²(a√σ)², lies in [0.5, 1.5] with
  error ≤ 0.25 (a√σ error included). W(2,2) at level n − 1 is the loop of the same physical size L × L as the coarse plaquette,
  built from the half-block variable; this checks that the variable carries the string tension one level below, where loops of
  two sizes exist on the coarse lattice. Tolerance as Leg C's signal check (Coulomb and smearing corrections at R ≈ 1.5 coarse
  units are of order 10–30 %).

**Status:** PASS iff C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3; otherwise **NO CONTENT** (the point is reported, with what is missing, and does not count:
it cannot be a click, and no entry scale uses it). **Reported, not criteria:** the retention ratios `ρ_F = ⟨½tr V_p⟩ / W⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾(2,2)`
and `ρ_A` (the coarse plaquette against the same-size loop built one level down: how much a further blocking step keeps), and a
thermalisation drift (first against second half of the measurements, flagged above 3σ).

## Gates

* **Photon** (U(1) β = 1.1, Coulomb, 48⁴, b = 4, 8, 16). All of: (1) H_TV, H_W **and the centre half c_TV** EXCLUDED at every b
  (the photon must be excluded through the centre half; its adjoint half `q2 cos 2θ` is not required to be excluded: a centre-blind
  condition cannot see the photon); (2) the base-fit q1 of both classes **does not fall with b** (each larger b keeps at least 85 %
  of every smaller b's value, within 2 combined errors) and **does not run away** (largest/smallest ≤ 1.5); (3) mean q1 ≥ 0.5·β.
  Item 2 replaces Leg C's symmetric "flat within 15 %" rule, which is reported next to it; the reason, stated before data: at
  w = 2 the one-loop variance still falls with ℓ (above), so q1 ∝ 1/σ² is expected to **rise** by about 20 % (ts) and 28 % (ss)
  from b = 4 to 16, close to the symmetric rule's limit. A rising photon coupling is the opposite of the noise limit (which the
  photon gate is there to catch). **If the photon gate fails, the queue stops before SU(2)**, and the good blocking at w = 2 is not an
  instrument that can tell a massless theory from a gapped one.
* **Z₂ deconfined** (β = 0.6, 48⁴): H_TV and H_W EXCLUDED at every b (Z₂ has no centre-blind part: a = 0, H = c). Reported, not a
  queue stop. Note: if the blocked Z₂ configurations are perfectly ordered (pipeline test: all coarse plaquettes 1), the
  pseudo-likelihood has no maximum and the fit returns a large finite value; EXCLUDED is then the correct reading.
* **Deconfinement edge:** any NOT_EXCLUDED of H_TV or H_W at L√σ < 1.45 (the points 0.73, 1.03, 1.40) is flagged as a false
  positive of the method, whatever its content status; 1.46 is reported with the remark that it lies at the edge.

## Expectations, stated before data (not criteria)

* **Fundamental content.** A coarse plaquette is a smeared L × L Wilson loop; with good overlap it is roughly e^{−σL²}: 1.4·10⁻²
  (2.06), 4·10⁻⁴ (2.80), 2·10⁻⁴ (2.92), 4·10⁻⁸ (4.13), 2·10⁻¹⁴ (5.60). Expected statistical errors about 10⁻⁴ (Leg C reached 2·10⁻⁴
  at 3.15 with a quarter of this volume × configurations). So C1 is expected to pass at 2.06, to be marginal at 2.80 and 2.92, and to
  fail at 4.13 and 5.60 for any variable whose coarse plaquette follows the area law of an L × L loop (Leg C's flowed variable resolved 2.2·10⁻³ at 3.15, probably because its 4D flow at radius
  b/2 shrinks the effective area; the good blocking smears only spatially).
* **Adjoint content.** The adjoint L × L loop falls faster (Casimir 8/3 at intermediate L, then string breaking with a perimeter
  cost); Leg C's variable gave 8.6·10⁻⁴ at 2.10 and zero within errors from 3.15 on. Expected here: resolved at 2.06 at best,
  likely unresolved from 2.80 on. **So the most probable outcome is: content PASS at most at L√σ ≈ 2.06, NO CONTENT from about
  2.8 on**, i.e. at L√σ ≳ 3 the coarse-plaquette content check is limited by physics (area law, adjoint screening) rather than by
  the block variable. This is the coordinator's point to weigh (NOTE.md, "Decision for the coordinator").
* **Halves where content holds (2.06).** Leg C's variable at 2.10 had β_F = 0.19, centre half c_TV = 3.3 (EXCLUDED through the
  centre). A variable with more content keeps larger couplings, so EXCLUDED through the centre half is expected at 2.06.
* **Photon:** EXCLUDED at every b with q1 ≈ 1.3–1.7 (pipeline: 1.27–1.66 at 12⁴), rising with b. **Z₂:** EXCLUDED, perfectly
  ordered blocked configurations. **Edge points:** EXCLUDED (large couplings).

## What this measurement cannot show

1. Anything rigorous about the true blocked theory: verdicts are about fitted, truncated actions (H1Local, truncation; the closure
   residuals and extension fits probe the truncation only for the terms included).
2. The supremum in the W₁ form (linearised only).
3. At L√σ ≥ 4 almost surely, and probably from 2.8 on, whether the halves hold **with content**: the content check is expected to
   fail there by physics (above).
4. Volume dependence at b = 16 (3⁴ coarse tori; no 4⁴ partner within memory); one coarse volume per b.
5. The continuum limit: three β, one physical size compared across β (2.80/2.92).
6. Uniqueness of w: one weight (w = 2, chosen by the one-loop rule). A click is a statement about this variable.
7. Unit tests not run here: finite subgroups (no update code for them), triviality and Banks–Zaks (not applicable to a
   measurement on SU(2)).
