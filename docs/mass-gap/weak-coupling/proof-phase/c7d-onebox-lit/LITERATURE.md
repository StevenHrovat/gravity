# Cycle 7, workstream d (c7d-onebox-lit): what is proven about one-box (finite-volume, worst-case-boundary) criteria, and what changes for gauge theories (2026-11-22)

Scope: literature only. No computation, no Lean. Claim levels as in `c6c-floor/NOTE.md`: **read** (text of the source checked this session, at least the named statement), **abstract** (abstract, listing or a secondary description only), **recalled** (from memory, not re-checked). Roles, not names, for the project side.

## 0. In plain words

1. The one-box criterion exists as a theorem for classical spin systems: the **Dobrushin-Shlosman (DS) condition C_V**. Take ONE finite box V. For every boundary condition, change one boundary spin y and measure how far the Gibbs distribution inside V moves (Wasserstein distance, summed over V). Call the worst case over boundary conditions k_{V,y}. If the sum over boundary sites y of k_{V,y} is strictly less than |V|, there is a unique Gibbs state with exponentially decaying correlations. This is exactly "interior insensitive to the outside, uniformly over boundary conditions, margin beating the surface count" (form read in Shlosman 2025 and Fernandez et al. 2019; DS 1985/1987 themselves not read).
2. It is **constructive**: a finite computation, used for computer-assisted proofs (Dobrushin-Kolafa-Shlosman 1985, 2D Ising antiferromagnet, box 3 x 4 according to Shlosman 2025). For Ising it is **sharp**: Shlosman (2025) shows the supremum over boxes of the couplings satisfying C_V is the critical point.
3. For **compact continuous spins** the consequences are proven in dynamical form: for finite-range interactions on a compact continuous spin space, a uniform log-Sobolev inequality is equivalent to the DS mixing condition (Stroock-Zegarlinski 1992, abstract). The Wasserstein form of the uniqueness theorem is generalised to complete metric spin spaces (Dorlas-Savoie 2023, abstract). So a compact single-link space such as SU(2) is not excluded by the spin-system theory.
4. **For gauge theories no one-box criterion exists in the literature I found.** What exists: (a) DS-type decay under every boundary condition at strong coupling only (Chatterjee 2021 remark: provable "for essentially any lattice gauge theory" at small beta by the DS methods); (b) weak-coupling decay for **finite** gauge groups (Forsstroem 2022 abelian, Adhikari-Cao 2025 non-abelian finite), by other methods; (c) Chatterjee's implication "decay under arbitrary boundary conditions, for all cubes, uniformly, plus non-trivial centre, gives unbroken centre symmetry and the area law": the natural consumer of a one-box theorem, but its hypothesis is stated for all cubes, not one.
5. **SU(2) at beta around 2 to 2.5: nothing rigorous or computer-assisted found** that derives decay from a finite-volume check.

## 2. Answers to the five questions

**Q1. Dobrushin-Shlosman.** Exact form (read via later papers that restate it): a finite box V; two boundary conditions differing at one boundary site y; the Wasserstein distance (single-spin metric summed over V) between the two finite-volume Gibbs laws on V, maximised over the boundary condition, called k_{V,y}; condition C_V: the sum over boundary sites of k_{V,y} is strictly less than |V|. For one site it is Dobrushin's 1968 condition. Consequence: unique Gibbs state, exponential decay. Spin spaces: finite sets in the original; the Wasserstein formulation extends to complete metric spaces (Dorlas-Savoie) and the dynamical side to compact continuous spins (Stroock-Zegarlinski). Unbounded spins: only Gaussian-perturbation cases appear in the Dorlas-Savoie abstract; conditions not read. Open to my reading: whether ONE cube already gives full complete analyticity (all subvolume shapes) in d >= 3. The step from weak to strong mixing is what Martinelli-Olivieri-Schonmann address, confirmed only for d = 2 discrete spins and only at abstract level.

**Q2. Martinelli-Olivieri, Stroock-Zegarlinski.** Martinelli-Olivieri: strong mixing in one large enough cube gives log-Sobolev in all volumes by block decimation, but written for discrete spins; their introduction (as relayed) says DS complete analyticity and Stroock-Zegarlinski cannot be applied to the whole one-phase region, which is why they introduce strong mixing. Stroock-Zegarlinski: for finite-range interactions on a compact continuous spin space, log-Sobolev is equivalent to the DS mixing condition, so **compact continuous spins with finite-range interactions are covered on the equivalence side**. Caveat: the mixing condition there is the all-volumes one; the one-box content comes from the DS finite-volume theorem. Abstract-level only.

**Q3. Computer-assisted verifications.** Dobrushin-Kolafa-Shlosman 1985 (2D Ising antiferromagnet, 3 x 4 box); computer-assisted bounds on the hard-core critical activity on Z^2 (as cited); Fernandez et al. 2019 (cell-board Ising, DS on 2 x 2 and 3 x 3 boxes, floating point, not certified); Shlosman 2025 (theory: boxes of unbounded size exhaust the Ising region). Boxes used are tiny (side 3 to 4), because the worst case over boundary conditions of an optimal-transport distance on 2^|V| states grows exponentially. No continuous-spin or gauge certification found.

**Q4. Gauge theories.** No one-box criterion known to me. What exists: Chatterjee's Definition 2.3 (every cube, every boundary condition, uniform constants) with Theorem 2.4; his remark that it is provable at small beta by the DS methods (strong coupling); weak-coupling decay for finite groups; abelian Higgs by renormalisation group. Chatterjee also notes (read) that for finite groups, believed gapped but not confining at large beta, the known decay is under special boundary conditions, not arbitrary ones, which is why Theorem 2.4 is not contradicted. Warning for our route: the criterion must hold uniformly over ALL boundary conditions, or it says nothing about confinement.

How gauge invariance enters:
- *In the sources (read, Chatterjee):* boundary conditions assign group elements to the boundary EDGES of the cube, so they are gauge-variant; the observables in Definition 2.3 are local functions of edges, not required to be gauge invariant. Wilson loops enter only in the consequence.
- *Standard background (recalled):* Elitzur's theorem (1975): a local gauge-variant observable has zero expectation without gauge fixing; mass-gap statements are therefore about gauge-invariant observables. Usual remedies: restrict to gauge-invariant observables; fix a gauge (axial, temporal, maximal tree) so the variables are not redundant; use free or gauge-invariant boundary conditions.
- *My analysis, not a literature statement:* the DS distance compares interior laws in a metric on the spin variable. With the gauge-variant link metric, at weak coupling the interior links sit near a pure-gauge extension of the boundary data, so a boundary change by a gauge transformation moves the interior law by an amount of order the box volume while changing nothing physical. The literal link-metric C_V should therefore fail at weak coupling for every box, for gauge-trivial reasons. A meaningful gauge version needs the distance on gauge-orbit space (or between laws of a generating set of gauge-invariant observables) and boundary data modulo boundary gauge transformations, or a gauge fixing compatible with the boundary. No source found does this. It is checkable on a small example (SU(2), small box, gauge-invariant versus link metric). A second, milder point: a boundary link enters several plaquettes, so "change one boundary spin" becomes "change one boundary link"; bookkeeping, not an obstruction.

**Q5. SU(2) at beta around 2 to 2.5.** Nothing rigorous or computer-assisted found that gets decay from a finite-volume check. Rigorous coverage: strong coupling (Osterwalder-Seiler, Dobrushin-type small-beta arguments) and the weak-coupling program of Balaban (not re-read; only seen in search listings). The window between is open in the literature. The only large-beta claims in search are self-published and unrefereed (row 24); not read, not relied on.

## 3. Verdict

**Closest proven template: the Dobrushin-Shlosman finite-volume criterion C_V** (row 3), in the Wasserstein form for general metric spin spaces (row 11), with the **Stroock-Zegarlinski equivalence** (row 5) saying that for finite-range compact continuous spins the mixing property is the strong one (uniform log-Sobolev). Why DS rather than Martinelli-Olivieri: DS has the sharp one-box form "sum over boundary < volume", is tight for Ising (Shlosman 2025) and is computer-checkable (Dobrushin-Kolafa-Shlosman). Martinelli-Olivieri is the better template only for "one cube plus its sub-boxes implies all volumes", and is discrete-spin only. For the consequence we want, the second template is Chatterjee 2021: **one-box DS-type criterion (needed, unknown for gauge theories) implies decay under every boundary condition for all cubes (Definition 2.3), which implies by Theorem 2.4 unbroken centre symmetry and the area law.** The first arrow is the gap; the second is a theorem for compact gauge groups with non-trivial centre.

**The gauge-theory gap, in three parts.**
1. *No one-box theorem for gauge theories.* DS-type decay under all boundary conditions is known only at strong coupling; weak-coupling decay is known only for finite gauge groups, by cluster-expansion arguments that do not give uniformity over all boundary conditions and do not extend to continuous groups. Chatterjee's hypothesis is for all cubes and has not been derived from a finite check in any gauge theory.
2. *Gauge-variant boundary conditions.* The spin criterion uses a metric on the spin variable and arbitrary boundary values; for gauge fields both are gauge-variant, and the literal translation should fail at weak coupling for gauge-trivial reasons (my analysis, Q4). A gauge-invariant formulation of C_V (orbit-space distance, boundary data modulo gauge or in a fixed gauge) is not in the literature found.
3. *Continuous group at intermediate coupling.* No finite-volume or computer-assisted decay statement for SU(2) at beta around 2 to 2.5. Computer-assisted DS checks in the literature are for discrete spins in boxes of side 3 to 4, in floating point or by hand, without interval arithmetic. For a continuous group the exact Wasserstein distance is an infinite-dimensional transport problem, so a certified check would need a computable upper bound (a coupling construction or a dual bound) instead.

**What this means for the route (reading, not literature).** The one-box condition in the plan is a faithful transcription of DS C_V and the needed theorem is of known type for spin systems, but its gauge version is open and would be a new theorem, not an application. Two cheap steps follow: (i) formulate C_V for SU(2) with a gauge-invariant distance and boundary data modulo gauge, and test on a small box at strong coupling (must hold) and at weak coupling (to see whether it can hold at all); (ii) keep Chatterjee's Definition 2.3 as the target statement of the one-box theorem, since its consequence (Theorem 2.4) is already proven.
