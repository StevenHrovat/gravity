## 0. Result in plain words

1. **All five rows carry over, and trapping makes the constants simpler than `D-N.md` feared.** With deep cores trapped
   after a fixed delay, Balaban's own delay `N = R_j` and his own exponents work again; the retuning of `D-N.md` 1 was
   needed only for the random-shift device, which is gone.
2. **One new lemma is needed, and it is not trivial: the fibre-mass bound (Lemma E7.J).** Balaban bounds every group
   integral in a large-field region by 1 (Haar measure has mass 1). Under the block constraint the same integrals are
   over a fibre `{V̄ = W}`, whose mass is the density at `W` of the blocked Haar measure. The induction needs that density
   to be at most `C(L)` per coarse bond, uniformly in `W`. A sketch exists (each coarse bond owns the fine bonds crossing
   its block face, and averaging independent Haar elements has bounded density), but two points are open: the reference
   path couples those bonds, and the exponential map's Jacobian vanishes at the cut. **Open; the one non-routine item
   in E5-E9.**
3. **E9, the fundamental inequality** `sup_{W regular} N_X(W)/D_X(W) ≤ exp(-2(1+β0)^{-1} p0(g_k))`, follows from E5-E8 by
   Balaban's argument ((1.79), (1.80), (1.89)) with the fibre-mass factor `C(L)^{|X^{(k+1)}|}` absorbed into his per-step
   entropy `O(1) M^d R^{d+1}` per unit size, **given E7.J**.

## 2. Lemma E7.J (fibre mass), open

**Statement (target).** For the blocking map `M` (Balaban's average, or its twisted/excised version) restricted to the
fine bonds of a region `Λ` (bonds outside fixed), the pushforward of product Haar measure has a density `m_Λ(W)` with
respect to product Haar measure on the coarse bonds of `Λ`, and `sup_W m_Λ(W) ≤ C(L)^{|Λ^{(k+1)}|}`.

**Sketch.** Each coarse bond `c` of `Λ` owns the `L^{d-1}` fine bonds crossing the face between its two blocks: they
are read by `c`'s segments and by no other coarse bond. Condition on all other fine bonds. Then `Ū(c)` depends on its own
face bonds only, and different coarse bonds on disjoint sets, so the density factorises over `c`. For one `c`: each
segment uses one face bond, with weight `L^{1-d}`, so the logarithm in (0.4) is a weighted sum of `L^{d-1}` terms, each
a function of one independent Haar element; a sum of independent terms one of which has a bounded density has a bounded
density (convolution does not increase the sup norm), giving `C(L)` per coarse bond.

**What is missing.** (i) The reference `U(c)` is the straight path, which uses one face bond, and every logarithm is
taken relative to it; conditioning on that bond too removes the coupling but leaves `L^{d-1} - 1` owned bonds, whose
terms are then independent given the rest, so this point looks fine. (ii) The exponential map from the logarithm to
`SU(2)` has Jacobian `(sin|v|/|v|)²`, which vanishes at `|v| = π`: the density of `Ū(c)` can blow up where the averaged
logarithm reaches the cut. For `W` regular and the reference near `W` this does not happen, but the bound is needed for
all fine configurations in the fibre, including large fields. Either bound the probability of reaching the cut (the
averaged logarithm of many independent terms is concentrated, so its density near `|v| = π` is small) or restrict the
fibre integral by a characteristic function and treat the rest as a large field. Plausible, 2-3 pages; not done.
