\section{What the tests show}\label{sec:synthesis}

Taken together, Parts~II and~III sort GUID's statements into four kinds.

\emph{What reduces to established physics.} Free fall, light bending, the post-Newtonian limit, gravitational waves, Kerr black holes, the
late universe, the Higgs couplings and the running of the forces all come out as in general relativity, $\Lambda$CDM and the Standard
Model, and they agree with the data as those theories do (Sections~\ref{sec:data} and~\ref{sec:sm}). They show that the reading is
consistent, not that it is right. The tensions in this group, evolving dark energy, the Hubble constant and curvature, are $\Lambda$CDM's,
inherited unchanged.

\emph{What was refuted.} Bridge~A and the stochastic dark-matter remnant fail against galaxy rotation curves in every form computed
(Sections~\ref{sec:sparc} and~\ref{sec:remnant}). The window fixed in advance for the variation of the proton-to-electron mass ratio with
$\alpha$ failed as fixed (Section~\ref{sec:sm}). A standard model of the compact hadron trees fails for most measured exotic masses
(Section~\ref{sec:exotics}). The claim that an entanglement experiment with two spheres decides between classical and quantum gravity
fails if the Di\'osi--Penrose rate is real (Section~\ref{sec:bmv}).

\emph{What GUID adds that is not yet testable.} The classical frame itself, through Hypothesis~1$'$; the Di\'osi--Penrose rate through
Bridge~B, whose first decisive test needs a superposition five to eight orders of magnitude heavier than any made so far
(Section~\ref{sec:data}); primordial black holes as the dark matter, which this paper does not test; neutrinos without Majorana mass; and
the minting reading of pentaquarks, whose test lies in states not yet established (Section~\ref{sec:exotics}).

\emph{What to compute next.} Two calculations would give GUID a number of its own. DMP's two independent witnesses may imply two
independent noise sources with a statistical signature that a single source lacks (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}); and a relativistic form of
the stochastic remnant would say whether anything of it survives lensing, the acoustic peaks and merging clusters
(Section~\ref{sec:remnant}).

\section{Where we expect it to break}\label{sec:break}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{The hypotheses.} Everything physical rests on the steps of Table~\ref{tab:hyp}, above all on Hypothesis~1$'$, which places the
field that pulls laboratory masses in the frame. Another faithful reading changes the predictions; the dissent of
Section~\ref{sec:dissent} is one.
\item \emph{Experiment.} A positive test of gravitationally mediated entanglement, a violation of the LOCC inequality, or decoherence and
diffusion both measured below the trade-off~\eqref{eq:tradeoff} would each show that gravity is not GUID's classical frame, unless
classical gravity can entangle after all~\cite{azizhowl}. A null entanglement test is not decisive (Section~\ref{sec:bmv}).
\item \emph{Constants.} The axioms derive none of $G$, $\hbar$, $\Lambda$, the entropy per area or the strength of the channel. The bridges
fix the strength only by adding a principle. If a superposition of about $10^{-14}$\,kg over more than its own size keeps its coherence well
beyond $\hbar R/GM^2$, Bridge~B is refuted.
\item \emph{Dark matter.} The contained remnant now carries the dark matter alone. If evaporation and microlensing close the asteroid-mass
window, or if halos of primordial black holes cannot form the observed structures, the trapped-remnant hypothesis loses its last route.
\item \emph{The start.} GUID borrows its inflation model; if the scalar tilt settles where plateau models need many more e-folds, another
model replaces it, and GUID's reading of the start does not depend on which. If Axiom~1 forbids a coupling to curvature in every frame,
plateau inflation and GUID exclude each other.
\item \emph{Black holes.} A derivation of the Page curve that needs superposed geometries after the start would contradict
Lemma~\ref{lem:classical}.
\item \emph{Space.} A literal tree is not our space; the routing reading avoids the objection but has not been shown to give a Lorentzian
3+1 manifold in any limit.
\item \emph{The frame's place.} Hypothesis~1$'$ keeps the classical prediction only because the frame is a DMP structure shaped by the
counts. Read as lying outside DMP's structures, it would leave DMP silent on whether gravity is quantum.
\item \emph{Unification.} A proton decay found would show that a unification existed, as Hypothesis~6$'$ allows; continued silence cannot
refute one that lies before time.
\item \emph{Neutrinos and hadrons.} A confirmed neutrinoless double-beta decay would refute the Dirac statement; a confirmed open-flavour
pentaquark would refute H9.
\item \emph{The simulations and checks.} Classical hosting is exponential in the number of entangled qubits; the coherent field is simulated
in full only for small systems; every data confrontation depends on the likelihood and error model stated in its section; and the checks
of Part~I use round inputs that move numbers by tens of per cent, not the verdicts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Read as physics, the three axioms of the Dark Matter Protocol give a definite account of gravity and of what it contains. Gravity is a
classical frame, the tetrad of general relativity, shaped by the counts of energy and momentum, weighing all energy alike, and classical
because the axioms make each of its values a record that independent witnesses hold in full. Consistency with quantum mechanics then makes
it decohere matter and diffuse, and its own bridge hypotheses fix the rate at Di\'osi and Penrose's. The axioms host either reading of the
field that pulls laboratory masses; the classical one is GUID's choice, and an experiment can refute it.

Tested, the account holds wherever general relativity, $\Lambda$CDM and the Standard Model hold, inherits their open tensions, and loses
some of its ideas: Bridge~A, the stochastic dark-matter remnant, a numerical window it fixed in advance, and the masses a standard model
gives its compact hadron trees. What remains is a classical frame over quantum content, primordial black holes from the start as its
candidate dark matter, Dirac neutrinos, a pentaquark rule of its own, and one sharp number, the Di\'osi--Penrose rate. The simulations
showed that, if that rate is real, the experiment usually named as decisive cannot separate classical from quantum gravity; the first
experiment to decide is whether a single mesoscopic superposition decoheres at that rate. We claim that the construction exists,
reproduces and agrees with the data named. Whether it is right is for the experiments, and for the reader, to break.
