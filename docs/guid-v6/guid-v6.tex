\begin{abstract}
\noindent GUID reads an information-theoretic axiom system for trust, the Dark Matter Protocol (DMP~0.9.9), as physics. In DMP every
act needs two independent witnesses and leaves a record that its witnesses read in full, while every other reader sees only counts. Read
with stated hypotheses, gravity becomes a classical frame, the tetrad of general relativity, over quantum content. A structure that every
party reads in full cannot be quantum, so the frame cannot mediate entanglement; consistency with quantum mechanics then makes it
decohere matter and diffuse, and two bridge hypotheses fix the coupling at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate, with one length left to experiment.
This paper states the account and tests it. In simulation, a recorded frame and a coherent field behave as stated, and a calculation that
does not depend on GUID shows that if Di\'osi--Penrose decoherence is real, no entanglement experiment with two solid spheres can separate
classical from quantum gravity; the decoherence itself is the first test. Against data, galaxy rotation curves refute the theory's
stochastic dark-matter term and leave primordial black holes, which this paper does not test, as its candidate. Of 72 scored
confrontations with laboratory, astrophysical, cosmological and particle data, 58 hold. The 14 that fail are 11 inherited tensions of the
theories GUID adopts and all 3 GUID-specific statements that a datum or a derivation can already reach; 22 further GUID-specific
statements hold but are not yet sensitive.
Statements about the Standard Model, fixed before the analysis, hold for the Higgs couplings and predict no neutrinoless double-beta
decay if $B-L$ is read as a conserved count; one of them failed as fixed, and we report it. A reading of hadrons as trees of dualities is one
that QCD already allows, and a standard model of its compact states fails for most measured tetraquark and pentaquark masses. We claim
that the construction exists, reproduces and agrees with the data named, not that it is correct.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem}

General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of a classical spacetime metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ sourced by energy and momentum,
$G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=(8\pi G/c^4)T_{\mu\nu}$. Quantum theory describes every other interaction as a quantum field. The two
meet in three places, and in each the meeting is not understood.

\emph{The ultraviolet.} Quantising the metric perturbatively around flat space, $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu}$ with
$\kappa=\sqrt{32\pi G}/c^2$, gives a massless spin-2 particle, the graviton, whose coupling has the dimension of a length. The theory is
not renormalisable: pure gravity diverges at two loops (Goroff and Sagnotti~\cite{goroff}), and with matter already at one loop ('t~Hooft and
Veltman~\cite{thooftveltman}). Below the Planck energy $E_P=\sqrt{\hbar c^5/G}\approx1.22\times10^{19}$\,GeV the theory is nevertheless a
reliable effective field theory (Donoghue~\cite{donoghue}); its leading quantum correction to the Newtonian potential is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eft}
V(r)=-\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r}\left[1+3\frac{G(m_1+m_2)}{rc^2}+\frac{41}{10\pi}\frac{G\hbar}{r^2c^3}\right],
\end{equation}
where the middle term is classical and the last is quantum~\cite{bdh}. At a distance of one metre the quantum term is about
$3\times10^{-70}$ of the first \chk{Q1}. What completes the theory above $E_P$ is the subject of string theory, loop quantum gravity,
asymptotic safety, causal dynamical triangulations, causal sets and others (Section~\ref{sec:landscape}).

\emph{Black holes.} A black hole radiates thermally at the Hawking temperature $k_BT_H=\hbar c^3/8\pi GM$~\cite{hawking75} and carries the
Bekenstein--Hawking entropy $S=k_Bc^3A/4G\hbar$~\cite{bekenstein73}. Whether the radiation carries the information that fell in, and how,
is the black-hole information problem~\cite{page93,penington,aemm}.

\emph{Whether to quantise at all.} Nothing in experiment yet requires the gravitational field itself to be quantum. Feynman's argument for
quantising it, the Eppley--Hannah thought experiment~\cite{eppley} and the Page--Geilker experiment~\cite{pagegeilker} rule out specific
ways of coupling a classical metric to quantum matter, not all of them. A consistent classical-quantum dynamics exists and is testable
(Oppenheim~\cite{oppenheim}; Oppenheim, Sparaciari, \v{S}oda and Weller-Davies~\cite{osswd}), and the question has become experimental:
if gravity can entangle two masses through local interaction, it is not classical (Bose et al.~\cite{bose}; Marletto and Vedral~\cite{mv};
Galley, Giacomini and Selby~\cite{ggs}).

\subsection{The proposal in brief}

GUID, the General Unified Information Duality, is the reading of an information-theoretic axiom system, the Dark Matter Protocol
(DMP~0.9.9~\cite{dmp}), as physics. DMP was written to govern trust in computer systems. Its first axiom asks that every act be passed by
two independent witnesses, one owning trust and one owning information. Its second calls trust ``gravitational'', a field with sources,
depths and a freshness that decays. Its third asks that every act leave a \emph{record} that its witnesses read in full, while every other
reader sees only \emph{counts}, and that nothing about information be derivable from trust except that it exists. The resemblance to
physics is deliberate: a field that sources from mass, boundaries that contain, a floor at which only entropy leaves.

Taken literally, that resemblance gives one account of gravity and of what it contains (Part~I; Figure~\ref{fig:map}). The trust field
becomes \emph{the frame}: the tetrad of general relativity, shaped by the counts of energy and momentum but never identical to what it
contains. The information half carries the quantum \emph{content} of the world. The frame falls universally and does not mix with the
information half. Because the axioms make every one of its values a record that two witnesses hold in full, it is classical once the
start of the universe is complete, and it mediates no entanglement. A classical frame coupled to quantum matter must decohere that matter
and diffuse; the axioms' own architecture, record then recompute, reads as the measurement-and-feedback channel that does so, and two bridge
principles fix its one free constant. The same reading covers horizons, black holes, holographic codes, the start of the universe, the
dark matter, the Higgs vacuum, the fine-structure constant and the hadrons.

\subsection{The argument in eight steps}\label{sec:steps}

The main line of the paper is short (Figure~\ref{fig:steps}); the rest elaborates or tests it.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Axioms~3, 2 and~1 make every value of the trust field a record that at least two independent witnesses read in full
(Section~\ref{sec:fix}).
\item A family of values that can be read in full by independent readers commutes: the frame is classical (Lemma~\ref{lem:classical}).
\item A classical mediator acts by local operations and classical communication, so it creates no entanglement: $\mathcal N=0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:locc}). That the field which pulls laboratory masses belongs to the frame is Hypothesis~1$'$.
\item Consistency with quantum mechanics forces a classical frame coupled to quantum matter to decohere it and to diffuse, with a
trade-off between the two (Section~\ref{sec:cq}).
\item Record then recompute reads as a measurement followed by feedback (Proposition~\ref{pr:ktm}).
\item The balance rule and Bridge~B fix the coupling at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}).
\item At that rate the entangling phase between two solid spheres cannot outrun their decoherence, so an entanglement experiment cannot
discriminate (Section~\ref{sec:bmv}).
\item The first decisive test is therefore the decoherence of a single mesoscopic superposition (Sections~\ref{sec:bmv}
and~\ref{sec:data}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is new and what is not}\label{sec:new}

Every physical prediction of this paper already belongs to an existing programme: classical gravity coupled to quantum matter and its
decoherence-diffusion trade-off~\cite{oppenheim,osswd}, gravity as a measurement-and-feedback channel~\cite{ktm,tilloydiosi},
gravitationally mediated entanglement as the test~\cite{bose,mv,ggs}, horizon thermodynamics~\cite{unruh,hawking75,jacobson95},
holographic codes with a classical centre~\cite{harlow}, and primordial black holes as dark matter~\cite{gortongreen}. Fixed by its own
bridge hypotheses, GUID's coupling lands on the Di\'osi--Penrose rate or on a refuted value (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}). GUID is
therefore not a new theory of quantum gravity; it is a member of the classical-quantum family.

What it adds is a route. First, it makes explicit that DMP's demand that every party can audit the shared structure in full is the
redundancy condition of quantum Darwinism~\cite{zurek,bph}, so a frame built from that structure sits on the classical side of the open
question by construction (Lemma~\ref{lem:classical}). The axioms themselves host either outcome of the entanglement experiment; the
prediction $\mathcal N=0$ comes from Hypothesis~1$'$, which places the field that pulls laboratory masses in the frame
(Section~\ref{sec:dissent}). Second, it reads DMP's record-and-recompute architecture as measurement-and-feedback gravity
(Proposition~\ref{pr:ktm}). Third, its balance rule holds the decoherence-diffusion trade-off at equality, which
Section~\ref{sec:gravsim} shows to be a measurement and feedback with a complete record. Fourth, read the other way, the lemma is a design
statement for information systems: a control structure every party can audit in full is classical, while the data it governs need not be.

Two results of this paper hold independently of GUID, and Section~\ref{sec:bmv} compares them with the literature. The threshold itself,
entanglement only if the entangling rate exceeds twice the dephasing rate, was found numerically by Chevalier, Paige and
Kim~\cite{chevalier2020}; we prove it for every product input, and the statement for any two bodies at their self-rates is the two-branch
form of Tilloy and Di\'osi's least-decoherence principle~\cite{tilloydiosi2017}. As far as we can tell, what is new is the bound over
geometries: at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate the ratio of the entangling to the decoherence rate of two solid spheres is at most 1, below what
entanglement needs, so no such experiment can entangle them and the decisive measurement is the decoherence itself. The second is a
two-setting witness adapted to the state of that experiment, which detects every entangling phase, where the witness printed in the arXiv
version of the original proposal is reached by product states.

\subsection{Results in brief}\label{sec:brief}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{Simulation} (Part~II). A recorded frame keeps many gravitating masses in product states while reproducing Newton's force; a
coherent field entangles them. The holographic assembly holds its kinematics at scale, with an exact erasure threshold. Known quantum
configurations hosted on a classical computer return their known answers, which validates the host, not new physics.
\item \emph{The decisive experiment.} If Di\'osi--Penrose decoherence is real, an entanglement test with two spheres cannot decide; the
decoherence measurement can (Section~\ref{sec:bmv}).
\item \emph{Dark matter.} The theory's stochastic remnant fails against galaxy rotation curves in every form computed; GUID's remaining
candidate is primordial black holes from the start, which this paper does not test (Sections~\ref{sec:sparc} and~\ref{sec:remnant}).
\item \emph{The count.} Of 72 scored confrontations with laboratory, solar-system, astrophysical, cosmological and particle data, 58
hold. Eleven failures are tensions of $\Lambda$CDM, the Standard Model and a borrowed hadron model; the three GUID-specific statements that
the data already reach all fail; 22 more hold but are not yet sensitive (Sections~\ref{sec:data} and~\ref{sec:tally}).
\item \emph{The Standard Model.} Statements fixed before the analysis hold for the Higgs couplings, predict no neutrinoless double-beta
decay if $B-L$ is read as a conserved count, and include one failed prediction (Section~\ref{sec:sm}).
\item \emph{Hadrons.} Reading hadrons as trees of dualities is one that QCD already allows; a standard model of the compact trees fails for
most measured tetraquark and pentaquark masses. The minting reading of pentaquarks forbids open flavour, which no confirmed state has,
predicts hidden-bottom pentaquarks near 11.1\,GeV, and its splitting rule fails (Section~\ref{sec:exotics}).
\end{enumerate}

correct, and Section~\ref{sec:break} names where we expect it to break.

\textbf{Verdicts.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{derived}: follows from the axioms and the hypotheses named;

\item \emph{untested}: no measurement reaches it yet;
\item \emph{live}: testable, with a test possible or under way;

\item \emph{inherited}: a tension with data that GUID shares unchanged with $\Lambda$CDM or with a model it borrows.

overlapping or choice-dependent; \emph{not yet sensitive} rows are scored but lie far from where GUID differs from established physics.
