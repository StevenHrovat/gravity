\section{Dark matter I: rotation curves against the stochastic remnant}\label{sec:sparc}

We test the stochastic remnant of Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}, with its size fixed by Bridge~A (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridgeA}) or left free,
against the rotation curves of the SPARC galaxies; Primer~\ref{box:galaxy} gives the background.

\subsection{The model tested}\label{sec:sparc-model}

The linear term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:stochpot} adds $|\gamma_1|/2$ to the centripetal acceleration at every radius, Eq.~\eqref{eq:vcirc}.
In Ref.~\cite{oppenheimrusso}, $\gamma_1$ is a boundary condition of the path integral, not a field sourced by local matter. We therefore
take it to be the same at every radius of a galaxy, whatever the distribution of its baryons. At radius $R$ of a disc galaxy the
prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-model}
g_{\rm model}(R)=g_{\rm bar}(R)+\tfrac12|\gamma_1|-\gamma_2 R ,
\end{equation}
where $g_{\rm bar}$ is the Newtonian acceleration of the observed stars and gas \tagH.

\paragraph{The $\Lambda$ term and the turnover.} With the Planck 2018 value of $\Lambda$~\cite{planck18}, $\gamma_2=\Lambda c^2/3=
3.27\times10^{-36}$\,s$^{-2}$. The term $\gamma_2R$ stays below $7\times10^{-4}$ of the observed acceleration at every point. It moves
the fitted $|\gamma_1|$ by $7\times10^{-5}$ of its value \chk{sparc:S2a}. For a point mass the speed has its minimum $v_*$ at the turnover
radius $r_*$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:btf}; beyond it the curve rises as $\sqrt{|\gamma_1|r/2}$.

\paragraph{The values tested.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item Bridge~A, Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridgeA}: $|\gamma_1|=4.88\times10^{-10}$ ($\beta=0$) to $5.42\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$
($\beta\to-\infty$) \chk{Q19}, with no free parameter.
\item One universal $|\gamma_1|$, fitted.
\item The dwarf-galaxy scaling of Ref.~\cite{oppenheimrusso}, $|\gamma_1|(M)=g_0(1+M/10^{10}M_\odot)$, with $g_0\approx10^{-28}$\,m$^{-1}$
in units $c=1$, i.e.\ $8.99\times10^{-12}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$. The authors take it over from conformal-gravity fits; it is not derived.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Comparison models.} We compare with Newton, $g_{\rm model}=g_{\rm bar}$, and with the empirical radial acceleration
relation (RAR)~\cite{mcgaugh2016}, which has one acceleration scale $g_\dagger$ \tagP:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-rar}
g_{\rm model}=F(g_{\rm bar})=g_{\rm bar}\big/\big[1-\exp\big(-\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}/g_\dagger}\big)\big].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Data, error model and likelihood}\label{sec:sparc-data}

\paragraph{Data and provenance.} From SPARC~\cite{lelli2016} we use 175 disc galaxies and 3391 radii. At each radius we use the
rotation speed $V_{\rm obs}$ and its random error $\delta V$. We also use the speeds $V_{\rm gas}$, $V_{\rm disk}$ and $V_{\rm bul}$ of
the mass model at unit mass-to-light ratio. Per galaxy we use $D$, $i$ and their errors, $L_{[3.6]}$, $M_{\rm HI}$, $V_{\rm flat}$ and the
quality flag $Q$. The two public files are not in the repository. The script checks their SHA256 and stops on a mismatch
\chk{sparc:S0}:
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\texttt{MassModels\_Lelli2016c.mrt} & \texttt{9108994b12cc401b94a1768beca61c53ec354779385c9c9cc571049f3043244c}\\
\texttt{SPARC\_Lelli2016c.mrt} & \texttt{5aa0501f6b0d881fa579030e315e7b5b6ef561a5bd3a07472f9929c7e5728243}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
We apply the cuts of Ref.~\cite{mcgaugh2016}. $Q=3$ removes 12 galaxies and $i<30^\circ$ removes 10 more, which leaves 153. The cut
$\delta V/V_{\rm obs}<0.1$ keeps 2696 of their 3168 points, from 147 galaxies \chk{sparc:S1}.

\paragraph{Accelerations and errors.} With $\Upsilon_{\rm d}=0.5$ and $\Upsilon_{\rm b}=0.7$~\cite{mcgaugh2016},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-g}
g_{\rm obs}=\frac{V_{\rm obs}^2}{R},\qquad
g_{\rm bar}=\frac{V_{\rm gas}|V_{\rm gas}|+\Upsilon_{\rm d}V_{\rm disk}|V_{\rm disk}|+\Upsilon_{\rm b}V_{\rm bul}|V_{\rm bul}|}{R}.
\end{equation}
The errors follow Ref.~\cite[Eq.~(2), Sect.~2.1.2]{lelli2017}, with $g_{\rm gas}$ and $g_\star$ the gas and stellar parts of
$g_{\rm bar}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-err}
\frac{\delta g_{\rm obs}}{g_{\rm obs}}=\Big[\Big(\frac{2\delta V}{V_{\rm obs}}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{2\delta i}{\tan i}\Big)^2
+\Big(\frac{\delta D}{D}\Big)^2\Big]^{1/2},\qquad \delta g_{\rm bar}=\big[(0.1\,g_{\rm gas})^2+(0.25\,g_\star)^2\big]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The terms of $\delta g_{\rm bar}$ are a 10\% HI flux calibration and a 25\% scatter in $\Upsilon$. In dex the two errors average 0.104
and 0.089, against 0.1 and 0.08 in Ref.~\cite{lelli2017}. We work in $\log_{10}g$ because the dominant errors are multiplicative.

\paragraph{Likelihood.} The residual of point $i$ is $r_i=\log_{10}g_{{\rm obs},i}-\log_{10}g_{{\rm model},i}$. The primary likelihood,
L1, is the Gaussian likelihood of Section~\ref{sec:scoring} in $r_i$, with the effective variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-like}
\sigma_i^2=\sigma_{{\rm obs},i}^2+s_i^2\,\sigma_{{\rm bar},i}^2,\qquad s_i=\frac{\partial\log g_{\rm model}}{\partial\log g_{\rm bar}} .
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma_{\rm obs}$ and $\sigma_{\rm bar}$ are the errors of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-err} in dex; the error in $g_{\rm bar}$ thus enters
through the model's slope. The variant L2 uses the velocity errors plus an intrinsic floor $\sigma_{\rm int}$, fitted for each model. It
measures how much unexplained scatter each model needs. Models are compared by $\Delta$BIC, as defined in Section~\ref{sec:scoring}, with
$N=2696$ points.

Points of one galaxy share its distance, inclination and $\Upsilon$ errors. Point-wise BIC differences therefore overstate the evidence.
We take parameter ranges from a bootstrap over galaxies (1000 draws, fixed seed; brackets give the 16--84\% range), and we count galaxy by
galaxy which model fits better.

\subsection{The radial acceleration relation reproduced}\label{sec:sparc-rar}

We fit Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-rar} by orthogonal-distance regression in $(\log g_{\rm bar},\log g_{\rm obs})$, with errors in both
variables, as Refs.~\cite{mcgaugh2016,lelli2017} did. Our implementation reproduces \texttt{scipy.odr} to $10^{-3}$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The galaxy cuts reproduce exactly (153).
\item The public release has slightly more points than the 2016 version: 3168 and 2696, against 3149 and 2693.
\item The scatter reproduces: rms 0.133\,dex (published 0.13), Gaussian width 0.110 (0.11), and 0.130 expected from the errors (0.12)
\chk{sparc:S1}.
\end{itemize}
Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-rar-btfr}a shows the relation.

\paragraph{The scale $g_\dagger$.} We find $g_\dagger=(1.102\pm0.016)\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ ($1\sigma$, scaled by
$\sqrt{\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}}=\sqrt{1.55}$). The published value is $(1.20\pm0.02\,\mathrm{stat}\pm0.24\,\mathrm{sys})\times10^{-10}$. Ours
lies within the total uncertainty but not within three times the random error. Across six fitting choices it ranges from 1.02 to 1.27
\chk{sparc:S1}. The choices are orthogonal distance in log or linear space, with full or velocity-only errors on $g_{\rm obs}$, and least
squares in log space, weighted or unweighted. The papers do not state the space of their fit. The pipeline is right, and the quoted random error leaves out a
fitting-choice dependence of about $\pm0.12$. This orthogonal-distance value serves only the comparison with the published fit. The
model comparisons below use the L1 maximum-likelihood value, $g_\dagger=1.12\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (Table~\ref{tab:sparc-models}).

\subsection{Bridge A against the data}\label{sec:sparc-bridgea}

With Bridge~A's value, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-model} has no free parameter. It lies above the data at 98\% of the points. The median of
$\log(g_{\rm obs}/g_{\rm model})$ is:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $-0.59$ to $-0.63$\,dex overall;
\item $-0.97$ to $-1.02$\,dex at $g_{\rm bar}<10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$;
\item $-0.15$ to $-0.17$\,dex at $g_{\rm bar}>10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$.
\end{itemize}
The misfit grows where the remnant dominates (Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-residuals}a). The fit has $\chi^2/N=96$ to 107, and its BIC exceeds
the RAR's by 2.5 to $2.8\times10^5$ \chk{sparc:S2b}. Bridge~A's value is 11 times the best universal one and 8 times the per-galaxy
median. Section~\ref{sec:sparc-btfr} gives its Tully--Fisher consequences.

\subsection{One universal \texorpdfstring{$|\gamma_1|$}{|gamma1|} against the RAR}\label{sec:sparc-universal}

The best universal remnant is $|\gamma_1|=4.54\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ [4.15, 4.98] (Table~\ref{tab:sparc-models}). That is below
Bridge~A, and below the $a_0/2$ used in the estimate of Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}. The value is a compromise. It overshoots the lowest
accelerations and undershoots intermediate ones (Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-residuals}a). Against the one-parameter RAR its BIC is worse by
$+5066$ under L1. Under L2, where each model fits its own floor, it is worse by $+1542$. The constant then needs
$\sigma_{\rm int}=0.169$\,dex where the RAR needs 0.123. The RAR fits better in 109 of the 147 galaxies \chk{sparc:S2a}.

\subsection{The excess acceleration is not constant}\label{sec:sparc-slope}

Whatever the value of $|\gamma_1|$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-model} predicts the same excess $\Delta g=g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar}$ at every
$g_{\rm bar}$. The RAR instead tends to $\Delta g\simeq\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}g_\dagger}$ for $g_{\rm bar}\ll g_\dagger$, a logarithmic slope of
$1/2$. We take binned medians of $\Delta g$ in 0.3\,dex bins below $g_\dagger/10=1.12\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (914 points). Then we
fit their slope, resampling galaxies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-slope}
\dd\log\Delta g/\dd\log g_{\rm bar}=0.44\ [0.39,\ 0.49]\qquad\text{(a constant: 0; the RAR in the same bins: 0.45)}
\end{equation}
\chk{sparc:S2d}. The RAR's local slope is below $1/2$ because these bins are not deep in the low-acceleration regime.

\paragraph{A free exponent.} The law $g_{\rm bar}+K(g_{\rm bar}/10^{-10}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}})^p$ contains the constant at $p=0$. Fitted under
L1 with $K$ and $p$ free, it gives $p=0.436$ [0.396, 0.464] from all points. The same fit restricted to the 914 points below
$g_\dagger/10$ gives $p=0.543$. Against $p=0$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=-4983$; against the RAR it is still $+82$.

\paragraph{The binned excess.} Below $g_\dagger/10$ the binned excess rises from 1.4 to $3.0\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, with no
plateau. Further up it rises to $8.4\times10^{-11}$ and flattens in the bins at $\log g_{\rm bar}=-9.77$ and $-9.46$. The RAR excess also
reaches its maximum there (Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-residuals}b).

\paragraph{A null test of the estimator.} $\Delta g$ is binned in the noisy $g_{\rm bar}$, so its errors are correlated with the
binning variable. We therefore test the estimator on mock data. Each mock keeps the real radii, velocity errors and assumed $g_{\rm bar}$
of the 153 galaxies. Per galaxy it draws:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item a true distance and inclination from their tabulated errors;
\item a true stellar mass-to-light ratio (0.1\,dex) and a true HI flux (10\%).
\end{itemize}
Per point it draws a velocity error. The true acceleration follows from the true $g_{\rm bar}$ at the true radius. The observer infers
$g_{\rm obs}$ with the assumed distance and inclination. The mock then passes the same precision cut, bins, low-acceleration cut, slope
estimator and galaxy bootstrap \chk{sparc:S2d\_mock}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Null.} For a constant $|\gamma_1|=4.54\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, 400 mocks return a slope of $+0.005$ [$-0.033$, $+0.046$]
(16--84\% over the mocks). One mock analysed exactly like the data gives $+0.012$, with bootstrap range [$-0.033$, $+0.048$]. None of the
400 reaches the data's 0.436; the largest is 0.10.
\item \emph{Positive control.} For the RAR the mocks return $0.469$ [0.424, 0.518], against 0.45 without noise. 76\% of them lie above
the data's value.
\end{itemize}
The estimator cannot turn a constant excess into the observed slope, and the data sit inside the RAR's mock distribution.

\subsection{One \texorpdfstring{$|\gamma_1|$}{|gamma1|} per galaxy and the Oppenheim--Russo scaling}\label{sec:sparc-pergal}

If the remnant were a boundary constant per galaxy, each galaxy could carry its own value. We fit one $|\gamma_1|$ to each of the 132
galaxies with at least five points, with $\Upsilon$ fixed and velocity errors per point. Distance, inclination, $\Upsilon$ ($\pm25\%$) and
HI ($\pm10\%$) errors enter as whole-galaxy refits, added in quadrature; the median total error is 0.18\,dex. The values have median
$5.95\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, a 16--84\% range of 3.69 to $10.4\times10^{-11}$, and a spread of 0.25\,dex
(Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-gamma1}). 131 of the 132 lie below Bridge~A beyond their errors \chk{sparc:S2c}.

\paragraph{The mass trend.} $|\gamma_1|$ rises with $M_{\rm bar}=\Upsilon_{\rm d}L_{[3.6]}+1.33M_{\rm HI}$: Spearman $\rho=0.45$
($p=4\times10^{-8}$), log-log slope 0.14 [0.12, 0.17]. Against $V_{\rm flat}$ (116 galaxies), $\rho=0.61$ and the slope is 0.69
[0.60, 0.76]. Part of the mass trend trades against $\Upsilon$. With $\Upsilon_{\rm d}$ free (a log-normal prior of 0.11\,dex around 0.5,
bounded to 0.1--1.0), the slope is 0.09 and $\rho=0.34$.

\paragraph{The Oppenheim--Russo scaling} rises far more steeply than the data. We fit four laws to the per-galaxy $\log|\gamma_1|$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item a constant: rms 0.249\,dex, BIC 17.2;
\item a power law in $M_{\rm bar}$: rms 0.215\,dex, BIC $-16.7$;
\item $g_0(1+M/10^{10}M_\odot)$ with $g_0$ fitted ($2.04\times10^{-11}$): rms 0.395\,dex, BIC 138.8;
\item the same law with the $g_0$ of Ref.~\cite{oppenheimrusso}: rms 0.531\,dex, BIC 212.5.
\end{itemize}
Even with its amplitude fitted, the scaling is worse than a constant by $+122$. It also has a structural flaw. For massive galaxies it
makes $|\gamma_1|\propto M$, so $v_*^4\propto M^2$: a Tully--Fisher slope of 2, where the data give 3.7.

\paragraph{The shape within galaxies.} We fit one free parameter per galaxy for each law, with velocity errors only (2652 points). A
free constant gives $\chi^2=109720$, and a free $g_\dagger$ gives 26790. The RAR shape wins in 98 of 132 galaxies. Both $\chi^2$ are large
because velocity errors omit non-circular motions and the galaxy-wide errors.

\subsection{The baryonic Tully--Fisher relation}\label{sec:sparc-btfr}

We repeat the analysis of Ref.~\cite{lelli2016a} \tagP:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the galaxies with $V_{\rm flat}$ and $i\ge30^\circ$, without the eight starburst dwarfs and Holmberg~II (UGC~4305) it excludes;
\item $M_{\rm bar}=\Upsilon_{\rm d}L_{[3.6]}+1.33M_{\rm HI}$ with $\Upsilon_{\rm d}=0.5$, and the errors of its Eq.~(5), with 10\% on
$M_{\rm HI}$;
\item a fit of $\log M_{\rm bar}=s\log V_{\rm flat}+\log A$ with errors in both variables and an intrinsic scatter that sets
$\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=1$;
\item $2.6\sigma$ clipping as in LTS\_LINEFIT~\cite{cappellari2013}, which removes UGC~7125, the outlier the paper names.
\end{itemize}
We find $s=3.67\pm0.10$ and $\log A=2.36\pm0.21$ (bootstrap), with an intrinsic scatter of 0.109\,dex for 123 galaxies. The paper finds
$3.71\pm0.08$, $2.27\pm0.18$ and $0.10\pm0.02$ for 118; the public table has $V_{\rm flat}$ for more galaxies. Without clipping we find
3.65, 2.41 and 0.119 \chk{sparc:S3}.

\paragraph{The turnover relation.} With the slope fixed at 4, $\log A=1.676$, i.e.\ $V_{\rm flat}^4=GM_{\rm bar}a$ with
$a=1.59\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, against MOND's $a_0=1.2\times10^{-10}$~\cite{milgrom}. Through Eq.~\eqref{eq:btf} this needs
$|\gamma_1|=7.95\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$. Two estimators compare Bridge~A with the data in $v^4$ at fixed mass:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the ratio of normalisations, Bridge~A's $|\gamma_1|$ over the $7.95\times10^{-11}$ that the fitted $\log A$ needs: 6.1 to 6.8;
\item the median offset of the galaxies from the Bridge~A relation at their observed $V_{\rm flat}$,
$10^{\,{\rm median}(\log M_{\rm obs}-\log M_{\rm pred})}$: 6.4 to 7.1.
\end{itemize}
Both use $v_*$, the minimum of the model curve, while $V_{\rm flat}$ is measured further out. Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter} gives the
estimate against $a_0/2$.

\paragraph{The outer speed.} The direct test predicts each galaxy's speed at its outermost measured radius from its own baryons (124
galaxies). The median $\log(V_{\rm pred}/V_{\rm flat})$ and the rms are:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the RAR: $+0.014$ and 0.061\,dex;
\item the best universal constant: $+0.006$ and 0.087\,dex;
\item Bridge~A: $+0.48$ to $+0.50$\,dex, a factor 80 to 98 in $v^4$;
\item Newton: $-0.32$\,dex.
\end{itemize}
The constant gets the outer normalisation right on average. It has more scatter than the RAR, though, and a trend with mass (Spearman
$\rho=-0.42$, against $-0.08$ for the RAR). It overshoots small galaxies and undershoots large ones \chk{sparc:S3}.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The $g_\dagger$ check first required $1.20\pm3\times0.02$ and failed at 1.10. It now uses the total uncertainty, $\pm0.24$.
\item The 1\% tolerance on the point counts was set after seeing 3168 and 2696.
\item The first BTFR fit kept Holmberg~II and did not clip, and it missed the published slope by more than $2\sigma$. Both steps were then
taken from the published method~\cite{lelli2016a,cappellari2013}.
\item The bound $0.1\le\Upsilon_{\rm d}\le1$ was added after unbounded fits ran to $\Upsilon_{\rm d}\approx9$ in a few galaxies.
\item The outer-speed test was added after we saw that $v_*$ misrepresents rising model curves.
\item The mock null test of the slope estimator was added after the slope was known. Its two tolerances were fixed before the mocks were
first run: $|{\rm median}|<0.1$ for the null, and within 0.1 of the noiseless RAR slope for the control.
\end{itemize}
None of these choices changes a verdict.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item We test the remnant as written: a constant $|\gamma_1|$, independent of the mass distribution, from a static, spherically symmetric
calculation~\cite{oppenheimrusso}. Hertzberg and Loeb dispute its large-scale force~\cite{hertzberg2024}; Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}
gives its other limits.
\item A remnant that follows the matter distribution is untested here, as are lensing, the microwave background and clusters.
\item Point-wise $\Delta$BIC overstates the evidence, but every comparison keeps its sign in the bootstrap and in the galaxy counts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verdict.} The reproduction of the RAR from the public SPARC data \vd{holds}: the same 153 galaxies, the same scatter, and
$g_\dagger$ inside its published uncertainty. Bridge~A is \vd{refuted} by the rotation-curve points themselves; it overshoots 98\% of them,
by a dex at low acceleration. A constant extra acceleration of any size, and the Oppenheim--Russo mass scaling, are \vd{refuted} as the
explanation of rotation curves. The best constant fits worse than the one-parameter RAR ($\Delta$BIC $+5066$, worse in 109 of 147
galaxies), and the observed excess rises as $g_{\rm bar}^{0.44}$ where a constant predicts zero, a slope the estimator does not create in
mocks. Section~\ref{sec:remnant} takes up the accumulated remnant, halos and primordial black holes, and gives the dark-matter verdict.
