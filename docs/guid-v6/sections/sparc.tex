\section{Rotation curves: the stochastic remnant against SPARC}\label{sec:sparc}

Hypothesis~H7 (Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}) lets the frame's diffusion leave a remnant in the gravitational field. In the
classical-quantum calculation of Oppenheim and Russo~\cite{oppenheimrusso} it is a term linear in the radius in the static potential of
every mass, and Bridge~A (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}) fixes its size by the cosmological event horizon. A linear potential term adds the
same centripetal acceleration at every radius. Rotation curves therefore test the remnant point by point, not only through the
Tully--Fisher normalisation that Part~I used. We test it against the SPARC galaxies~\cite{lelli2016}, after reproducing the published
radial acceleration relation (RAR)~\cite{mcgaugh2016} as a check of the pipeline. Bridge~A is refuted by the rotation-curve points
themselves. A constant extra acceleration of any size is refuted as a description of the data: the observed excess rises as
$g_{\rm bar}^{0.44}$, where a constant predicts slope zero.

\subsection{The prediction and the comparison models}\label{sec:sparc-model}

In the Newtonian limit of the metric of Ref.~\cite[Eq.~(18)]{oppenheimrusso}, the potential of a mass $M$ and its circular speed are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-phi}
\Phi(r)=-\frac{GM}{r}-\frac{\gamma_0}{2}-\frac{\gamma_1}{2}\,r-\frac{\gamma_2}{2}\,r^2,\qquad
v^2=r\,\frac{\dd\Phi}{\dd r}=\frac{GM}{r}+\frac{|\gamma_1|}{2}\,r-\gamma_2\,r^2.
\end{equation}
Here $\gamma_2=\Lambda c^2/3>0$, the mean $\gamma_1<0$ is in units of acceleration ($c^2$ restored), and the constant $\gamma_0$ has no
dynamical effect \tagH. In Ref.~\cite{oppenheimrusso} $\gamma_1$ is a boundary condition of the path integral, not a field sourced by
local matter, so we take the remnant to be independent of how the baryons are distributed. At radius $R$ of a disc galaxy, the remnant as
written in Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter} then predicts the centripetal acceleration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-model}
g_{\rm model}(R)=g_{\rm bar}(R)+\tfrac12|\gamma_1|-\gamma_2 R ,
\end{equation}
where $g_{\rm bar}$ is the Newtonian acceleration of the observed stars and gas.

\paragraph{The $\Lambda$ term and the turnover.} With $H_0=67.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.685$~\cite{planck18},
$\gamma_2=3.27\times10^{-36}$\,s$^{-2}$. The term $\gamma_2R$ stays below $7\times10^{-4}$ of the observed acceleration at every point,
and it moves the fitted $|\gamma_1|$ by $7\times10^{-5}$ of its value \chk{sparc:S2a}. For a point mass, $v$ has its minimum
$v_*^4=2GM|\gamma_1|$ at $r_*=\sqrt{2GM/|\gamma_1|}$, and beyond it the curve rises as $\sqrt{|\gamma_1|r/2}$.

\paragraph{The values tested.} (i) Bridge~A, $|\gamma_1|=0.9$ to $1.0\,cH_\infty=4.88$ to $5.42\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$
\chk{Q19}, with no free parameter. (ii) One universal fitted $|\gamma_1|$. (iii) The dwarf-galaxy scaling of
Ref.~\cite{oppenheimrusso}, $|\gamma_1|(M)=g_0(1+M/10^{10}M_\odot)$ with $g_0\approx10^{-28}$\,m$^{-1}$ ($c=1$), i.e.\
$8.99\times10^{-12}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, taken over from conformal-gravity fits rather than derived.

\paragraph{Comparison models.} We compare with Newton, $g_{\rm model}=g_{\rm bar}$, and with the empirical RAR \tagP~\cite{mcgaugh2016}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-rar}
g_{\rm model}=F(g_{\rm bar})=g_{\rm bar}\big/\big[1-\exp\big(-\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}/g_\dagger}\big)\big],
\end{equation}
which tends to $g_{\rm bar}$ for $g_{\rm bar}\gg g_\dagger$ and to the deep-MOND form $\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}g_\dagger}$~\cite{milgrom} for
$g_{\rm bar}\ll g_\dagger$.

\subsection{Data, error model and likelihood}\label{sec:sparc-data}

\paragraph{Data and provenance.} SPARC~\cite{lelli2016} gives, at 3391 radii of 175 disc galaxies, the rotation speed $V_{\rm obs}$,
its random error $\delta V$, and the speeds $V_{\rm gas}$ (with helium), $V_{\rm disk}$ and $V_{\rm bul}$ that each component would
produce alone at unit mass-to-light ratio. Per galaxy it gives $D$, $i$, their errors, $L_{[3.6]}$, $M_{\rm HI}$, $V_{\rm flat}$ and a
quality flag $Q$. The script reads the two public files, which are not in the repository, and stops if their SHA256 differs
\chk{sparc:S0}:

As in Ref.~\cite{mcgaugh2016}, $Q=3$ removes 12 galaxies and $i<30^\circ$ removes 10 more, leaving 153. The cut
$\delta V/V_{\rm obs}<0.1$ keeps 2696 of their 3168 points, from 147 galaxies \chk{sparc:S1}.

\paragraph{Accelerations and errors.} With $\Upsilon_{\rm d}=0.5$ and $\Upsilon_{\rm b}=0.7$~\cite{mcgaugh2016},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-g}
g_{\rm obs}=\frac{V_{\rm obs}^2}{R},\qquad
g_{\rm bar}=\frac{V_{\rm gas}|V_{\rm gas}|+\Upsilon_{\rm d}V_{\rm disk}|V_{\rm disk}|+\Upsilon_{\rm b}V_{\rm bul}|V_{\rm bul}|}{R}.
\end{equation}
The errors follow Ref.~\cite[Eq.~(2), Sect.~2.1.2]{lelli2017}, with $g_{\rm gas}$ and $g_\star$ the gas and stellar parts of
$g_{\rm bar}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-err}
\frac{\delta g_{\rm obs}}{g_{\rm obs}}=\Big[\Big(\frac{2\delta V}{V_{\rm obs}}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{2\delta i}{\tan i}\Big)^2
+\Big(\frac{\delta D}{D}\Big)^2\Big]^{1/2},\qquad \delta g_{\rm bar}=\big[(0.1\,g_{\rm gas})^2+(0.25\,g_\star)^2\big]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The terms of $\delta g_{\rm bar}$ are a 10\% HI flux calibration and a 25\% scatter in $\Upsilon$. In dex the two errors average 0.104
and 0.089 (Ref.~\cite{lelli2017}: 0.1 and 0.08). We work in $\log_{10}g$ because the dominant errors are multiplicative.

\paragraph{Likelihood.} Let $r_i=\log_{10}g_{{\rm obs},i}-\log_{10}g_{{\rm model},i}$ be the residual of point $i$ for a model with $k$
parameters, and $\sigma_{\rm obs}$, $\sigma_{\rm bar}$ the errors of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-err} in dex. The primary likelihood, L1, is
Gaussian with an effective variance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-like}
\begin{gathered}
\sigma_i^2=\sigma_{{\rm obs},i}^2+s_i^2\,\sigma_{{\rm bar},i}^2,\qquad s_i=\frac{\partial\log g_{\rm model}}{\partial\log g_{\rm bar}},\\
-2\ln\mathcal L=\sum_{i=1}^N\Big[\frac{r_i^2}{\sigma_i^2}+\ln(2\pi\sigma_i^2)\Big],\qquad \mathrm{BIC}=-2\ln\mathcal L_{\max}+k\ln N,
\qquad N=2696.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The variant L2 uses velocity errors plus an intrinsic floor $\sigma_{\rm int}$ fitted per model, which measures how much unexplained
scatter each model needs. Points of one galaxy share its distance, inclination and $\Upsilon$ errors, so point-wise BIC differences overstate the evidence. We
therefore take parameter ranges from a bootstrap over galaxies (1000 draws, fixed seed; brackets give the 16--84\% range), and we count
galaxy by galaxy which model fits better.

\subsection{The radial acceleration relation reproduced}\label{sec:sparc-rar}

We fit Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-rar} by orthogonal-distance regression in $(\log g_{\rm bar},\log g_{\rm obs})$ with errors in both
variables, as Refs.~\cite{mcgaugh2016,lelli2017} did; our implementation reproduces \texttt{scipy.odr} to $10^{-3}$. The galaxy cuts
reproduce exactly (153). The public release has slightly more points than the 2016 version (3168 and 2696, against 3149 and 2693). The
scatter reproduces: rms 0.133\,dex (published 0.13), Gaussian width 0.110 (0.11), and 0.130 expected from the errors (0.12)
\chk{sparc:S1}. Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-rar-btfr}a shows the relation.

\paragraph{The scale $g_\dagger$.} We find $g_\dagger=(1.102\pm0.016)\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ ($1\sigma$, scaled by
$\sqrt{\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}}=\sqrt{1.55}$), against the published $(1.20\pm0.02\,\mathrm{stat}\pm0.24\,\mathrm{sys})\times10^{-10}$. Ours
lies within the total uncertainty but not within three times the random error. Across six fitting choices it ranges from 1.02 to 1.27
(orthogonal distance in log or linear space, with full or velocity-only errors on $g_{\rm obs}$, and least squares in log space)
\chk{sparc:S1}, and the papers do not state the space of their fit. The pipeline is right; the quoted random error leaves out a
fitting-choice dependence of about $\pm0.12$.

\subsection{Bridge A is refuted}\label{sec:sparc-bridgea}

With $|\gamma_1|=4.88$ to $5.42\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-model} has no free parameter, and it lies above the data
at 98\% of the points. The median of $\log(g_{\rm obs}/g_{\rm model})$ is $-0.59$ to $-0.63$\,dex overall, $-0.97$ to $-1.02$\,dex at
$g_{\rm bar}<10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, and $-0.15$ to $-0.17$\,dex at $g_{\rm bar}>10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$. The misfit grows where the remnant dominates (Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-residuals}a). The fit has $\chi^2/N=96$ to 107, and its BIC exceeds
the RAR's by 2.5 to $2.8\times10^5$ \chk{sparc:S2b}. Bridge~A's value is 11 times the best universal one and 8 times the per-galaxy
median. Section~\ref{sec:sparc-btfr} gives its Tully--Fisher consequences.

\subsection{One universal \texorpdfstring{$|\gamma_1|$}{|gamma1|} against the RAR}\label{sec:sparc-universal}

The best universal remnant is $|\gamma_1|=4.54\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ [4.15, 4.98] (Table~\ref{tab:sparc-models}). That is below
Bridge~A, and below the $a_0/2$ that Part~I inferred. The value is a compromise: it overshoots the lowest accelerations and undershoots
intermediate ones (Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-residuals}a). Against the one-parameter RAR, its BIC is worse by $+5066$ under L1. Under L2,
where each model fits its own floor, it is worse by $+1542$; the constant then needs $\sigma_{\rm int}=0.169$\,dex where the RAR needs
0.123. The RAR fits better in 109 of the 147 galaxies \chk{sparc:S2a}.

\subsection{The excess acceleration is not constant}\label{sec:sparc-slope}

Whatever the value of $|\gamma_1|$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-model} predicts the same excess $\Delta g=g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar}$ at every
$g_{\rm bar}$. The RAR instead has $\Delta g\simeq\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}g_\dagger}$ for $g_{\rm bar}\ll g_\dagger$, a logarithmic slope of
$1/2$. We take binned medians of $\Delta g$ in 0.3\,dex bins, below $g_\dagger/10=1.12\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (914 points), and fit
their slope, resampling galaxies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparc-slope}
\dd\log\Delta g/\dd\log g_{\rm bar}=0.44\ [0.39,\ 0.49]\qquad\text{(a constant: 0; the RAR in the same bins: 0.45)}
\end{equation}
\chk{sparc:S2d}. The RAR's local slope is below $1/2$ because these bins are not deep in the low-acceleration regime. The law
$g_{\rm bar}+K(g_{\rm bar}/10^{-10}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}})^p$, which contains the constant at $p=0$, agrees: $p=0.436$ [0.396, 0.464] (0.543
from the low-acceleration points), $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=-4983$ against $p=0$, yet $+82$ against the RAR. The binned excess rises from 1.4 to $8.4\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ over two decades in $g_{\rm bar}$, with no plateau
(Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-residuals}b).

\subsection{One \texorpdfstring{$|\gamma_1|$}{|gamma1|} per galaxy and the Oppenheim--Russo scaling}\label{sec:sparc-pergal}

If the remnant were a boundary constant per galaxy, each galaxy could carry its own value. We fit one $|\gamma_1|$ to each of the 132
galaxies with at least five points ($\Upsilon$ fixed, velocity errors per point). Distance, inclination, $\Upsilon$ ($\pm25\%$) and HI
($\pm10\%$) errors enter as whole-galaxy refits, added in quadrature (median total error 0.18\,dex). The values have median $5.95\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, 16--84\% range 3.69 to $10.4\times10^{-11}$, and spread 0.25\,dex
(Figure~\ref{fig:sparc-gamma1}). 131 of the 132 lie below Bridge~A beyond their errors \chk{sparc:S2c}.

\paragraph{The mass trend.} $|\gamma_1|$ rises with $M_{\rm bar}=\Upsilon_{\rm d}L_{[3.6]}+1.33M_{\rm HI}$: Spearman $\rho=0.45$
($p=4\times10^{-8}$), log-log slope 0.14 [0.12, 0.17]. Against $V_{\rm flat}$ (116 galaxies) $\rho=0.61$ and the slope is 0.69
[0.60, 0.76]. Part of the mass trend trades against $\Upsilon$. With $\Upsilon_{\rm d}$ free (a log-normal prior of 0.11\,dex around 0.5,
bounded to 0.1--1.0), the slope is 0.09 and $\rho=0.34$.

\paragraph{The Oppenheim--Russo scaling} rises far more steeply than the data. Fitted to the per-galaxy $\log|\gamma_1|$, a constant
gives rms 0.249\,dex (BIC 17.2) and a power law in $M_{\rm bar}$ 0.215\,dex ($-16.7$). The law $g_0(1+M/10^{10}M_\odot)$ gives 0.395\,dex
(138.8) with $g_0$ fitted ($2.04\times10^{-11}$), and 0.531\,dex (212.5) with the $g_0$ of Ref.~\cite{oppenheimrusso}. Even with its amplitude fitted, the scaling is worse than a constant by $+122$. It also has a structural flaw: for massive galaxies it
makes $|\gamma_1|\propto M$, so $v_*^4\propto M^2$, a Tully--Fisher slope of 2 where the data give 3.7.

\paragraph{The shape within galaxies.} With one free parameter per galaxy for each law and velocity errors only (2652 points), a free
constant gives $\chi^2=109720$ and a free $g_\dagger$ gives 26790; the RAR shape wins in 98 of 132 galaxies. Both $\chi^2$ are large
because velocity errors omit non-circular motions and galaxy-wide errors.

\subsection{The baryonic Tully--Fisher relation}\label{sec:sparc-btfr}

We repeat the analysis of Ref.~\cite{lelli2016a} \tagP. The sample is the galaxies with $V_{\rm flat}$ and $i\ge30^\circ$, without the
eight starburst dwarfs and Holmberg~II (UGC~4305) it excludes. We use $M_{\rm bar}=\Upsilon_{\rm d}L_{[3.6]}+1.33M_{\rm HI}$ with
$\Upsilon_{\rm d}=0.5$ and the errors of its Eq.~(5) (10\% on $M_{\rm HI}$). The fit of $\log M_{\rm bar}=s\log V_{\rm flat}+\log A$ has
errors in both variables, an intrinsic scatter that sets $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=1$, and $2.6\sigma$ clipping as in
LTS\_LINEFIT~\cite{cappellari2013}; the clipping removes UGC~7125, the outlier the paper names. We find $s=3.67\pm0.10$, $\log A=2.36\pm0.21$ (bootstrap) and an intrinsic scatter of 0.109\,dex for 123 galaxies. The paper finds
$3.71\pm0.08$, $2.27\pm0.18$ and $0.10\pm0.02$ for 118; the public table has $V_{\rm flat}$ for more galaxies. Without clipping we find
3.65, 2.41 and 0.119 \chk{sparc:S3}.

\paragraph{The turnover relation.} With the slope fixed at 4, $\log A=1.676$, i.e.\ $V_{\rm flat}^4=GM_{\rm bar}a$ with
$a=1.59\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (MOND: $a_0=1.2\times10^{-10}$~\cite{milgrom}). Through $v_*^4=2GM|\gamma_1|$ this needs
$|\gamma_1|=7.95\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, a sixth of Bridge~A. At fixed mass, Bridge~A gives 6.4 to 7.1 times the observed
$V_{\rm flat}^4$ (Part~I estimated 8). But $v_*$ is the minimum of the model curve, and $V_{\rm flat}$ is measured further out.

\paragraph{The outer speed.} The direct test predicts each galaxy's speed at its outermost measured radius from its own baryons (124
galaxies). The median $\log(V_{\rm pred}/V_{\rm flat})$ and the rms are $+0.014$ and 0.061\,dex for the RAR, and $+0.006$ and
0.087\,dex for the best universal constant. Bridge~A gives $+0.48$ to $+0.50$\,dex, a factor 80 to 98 in $v^4$; Newton gives
$-0.32$\,dex. The constant gets the outer normalisation right on average, but with more scatter, and with a mass trend (Spearman $\rho=-0.42$, against
$-0.08$ for the RAR). It overshoots small galaxies and undershoots large ones \chk{sparc:S3}.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The $g_\dagger$ check first required $1.20\pm3\times0.02$ and failed at 1.10; it now uses the total uncertainty, $\pm0.24$.
\item The 1\% tolerance on the point counts was set after seeing 3168 and 2696.
\item The first BTFR fit kept Holmberg~II and did not clip, missing the published slope by over $2\sigma$; both steps then followed
the published method~\cite{lelli2016a,cappellari2013}.
\item The bound $0.1\le\Upsilon_{\rm d}\le1$ was added after unbounded fits ran to $\Upsilon_{\rm d}\approx9$ in a few galaxies.
\item The outer-speed test was added after we saw that $v_*$ misrepresents rising model curves.
\end{itemize}
None of these choices changes a verdict.

\paragraph{Caveats.} We test the remnant as written: a constant $|\gamma_1|$, independent of the mass distribution, from a static,
spherically symmetric calculation its authors call preliminary~\cite{oppenheimrusso}; Hertzberg and Loeb dispute that the large-scale
force follows from the theory~\cite{hertzberg2024}. A remnant that follows the matter distribution is untested here, as are lensing, the
microwave background and clusters. Point-wise $\Delta$BIC overstates the evidence, but every comparison keeps its sign in the bootstrap.

\paragraph{Verdict.} The pipeline reproduces the RAR from the public SPARC data: the same 153 galaxies, the same scatter, and $g_\dagger$
inside its published uncertainty. Bridge~A is \vd{refuted} by the rotation-curve points themselves: it overshoots 98\% of them, by a dex
at low acceleration. A constant extra acceleration of any size, and the Oppenheim--Russo mass scaling, are \vd{refuted} as the
explanation of rotation curves. The best constant fits worse than the one-parameter RAR (BIC $+5066$, worse in 109 of 147 galaxies), and
the observed excess rises as $g_{\rm bar}^{0.44}$ where a constant predicts slope zero. Section~\ref{sec:remnant} takes up the
accumulated form of the remnant, halos and primordial black holes.
