\section{Hadrons as trees of dualities}\label{sec:exotics}

This section tests Hypotheses~\ref{h:tree} (H8) and~\ref{h:mint} (H9) of Section~\ref{sec:trees} against hadron spectroscopy: the
masses of compact trees, the closeness of molecular states to thresholds, the partners a tree implies, and H9's four registered
predictions.

\subsection{The model and the scoring}\label{sec:exotics-model}

H8 fixes no mass by itself. To give its compact trees masses we use the colour-spin (chromomagnetic) model of De R\'ujula, Georgi and
Glashow~\cite{derujula1975} in the form of Karliner and Rosner~\cite{karliner2014,karliner2017} (KR), the model that predicted the doubly
charmed baryon and a bound doubly bottom tetraquark (Primer~\ref{box:hadron}). The mass of a hadron is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exo-H}
M=\sum_i m_i+\sum_{i<j}B_{ij}-\sum_{i<j}C_{ij}\,\langle\boldsymbol\lambda_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol\lambda_j\,
\boldsymbol\sigma_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol\sigma_j\rangle ,
\end{equation}
with effective quark masses $m_i$, binding terms $B_{ij}$ for heavy and strange pairs, and hyperfine strengths $C_{ij}\propto
1/(m_im_j)$; $\boldsymbol\lambda$ are the colour (Gell-Mann) and $\boldsymbol\sigma$ the spin (Pauli) matrices. The expectation value is
taken in the H8 tree: each node below the root couples two colour triplets to the conjugate triplet, the root couples to a singlet, and
node spins with the same total $J$ are mixed. As in KR, every constituent of a multiquark takes its effective mass from baryons, and a
binding term scales with the colour factor of its pair.

We fit all 18 parameters to 42 ordinary ground-state mesons and baryons and to nothing exotic. The spread of the fit,
$\sigma_{\rm model}=8.1$\,MeV, fixed before any exotic comparison, sets the model's uncertainty. The held-out doubly charmed baryon
$\Xi_{cc}^{++}$ comes out at $3642.9\pm12.3$\,MeV against LHCb's $3621.55\pm0.38$\,MeV~\cite{lhcb2020j} (pull $+1.74$). With KR's own
parameters the code reproduces KR's published masses to 0.15\,MeV \chk{exotics:H}, \chk{exotics:F}. Rows are scored as in
Section~\ref{sec:scoring}: OK, and the prediction \emph{holds}, for $|{\rm pull}|<1.96$, with the model's $\sigma_{\rm pred}$ and the
data error on the side of the model. States not yet seen are \emph{live}. Each assignment of a model level to a state was fixed before
the comparison. Appendix~\ref{app:exotics} gives the couplings, the fit, two variants of the mass convention and the full spectra.

(Table~\ref{tab:exo-a}, Fig.~\ref{fig:exo-spectrum}) \chk{exotics:A}. What the table tests is that model's masses for compact trees, in
KR's mass convention, which dominates them (Appendix~\ref{app:exotics-variants}). It does not test GUID: H8 is a reading QCD allows.

\paragraph{Doubly heavy tetraquarks.} $T_{cc}^+$, LHCb's narrow $D^0D^0\pi^+$ state 0.36\,MeV below the $D^{*+}D^0$ threshold
\cite{lhcb2022h,lhcb2022i}, \emph{holds} at $z=+1.86$. The model, however, puts it 22\,MeV above that threshold; with
$\sigma_{\rm pred}=12$\,MeV it cannot tell whether the state is bound, as KR said of their own 3882\,MeV~\cite{karliner2017}. The
bound $T_{bb}^-$ is KR's prediction (215\,MeV below $B\bar B^*$~\cite{karliner2017}) and lattice QCD's (83 to 189\,MeV
\cite{francis2017,leskovec2019,aoki2023,hudspith2023,tripathy2025}). Refitted here, it lies at $10399\pm12$\,MeV, 205\,MeV below the
threshold. \emph{Live}, as their prediction. The $T_{bc}$ states lie within $\sigma_{\rm pred}$ of their thresholds.

\paragraph{The refutations.} Five masses fail. $X_0(2900)$ ($z=-9.3$) and $T_{c\bar s0}(2900)$ ($z=-4.1$) come out 100\,MeV too light.
LHCb's $X(6900)$ lies 480\,MeV above even the most favourable S-wave assignment ($z=-15$). The lowest pentaquark levels miss
$P_c(4312)$ ($z=-3.1$) and $P_{\psi s}(4338)$ ($z=+4.8$). Three states hold only by proximity, among levels dense enough to match most
peaks. The junction variant would rescue the two states near 2.9\,GeV, but it puts $T_{cc}^+$ 120\,MeV too high; no convention fits all.
The compact tree $[cq][\bar c\bar q]$ places $1^{++}$ 236\,MeV above $X(3872)$, which sits on the $D^0\bar D^{*0}$ threshold as a
molecular tree would.

\subsection{Partners}\label{sec:exotics-partners}

A tree with light-quark halves comes in isospin multiplets; one whose halves carry spin comes in spin multiplets \chk{exotics:C}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Charged partners of a compact $X(3872)$.} The diquark-antidiquark reading of $X(3872)$~\cite{maiani2005} implies charged
$X^\pm$ near 3872\,MeV. BaBar finds $\mathcal B(B^0\to X^-K^+,X^-\to J/\psi\pi^-\pi^0)<5.4\times10^{-6}$ (90\% CL), where an isovector
partner implies $2.56\times10^{-5}$~\cite{babar2005h}; Belle's limits are also well below that expectation~\cite{belle2011h}.
\emph{Failed prediction} of the compact reading. Our model's charged $1^{++}$ partners lie near 4108\,MeV, where no search was found.
\item \emph{Isospin partners of $T_{cc}^+$} with $J=0,1,2$: 4042, 4071 and 4131\,MeV, above their open thresholds and hence broad. LHCb
saw nothing narrow near threshold~\cite{lhcb2022i}, as the model has it; the predicted states lie outside the searched windows:
\emph{untested}.
\item \emph{Isospin partners of $T_{bb}^-$}: 10583, 10593 and 10613\,MeV: \emph{live}.
\item \emph{Pentaquark multiplets}: 10 levels with isospin $1/2$ and partners with isospin $3/2$; three peaks are seen, and their
production favours a spin-0 light diquark~\cite{maiani2015}: \emph{untested}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Threshold proximity}\label{sec:exotics-thresholds}

Where both halves are colour singlets, H8 makes the state a molecule, which lies near the threshold of its two hadrons. We take the 26
exotic hadrons reported as observations, find for each the nearest two-hadron threshold allowed in an S wave, and compare the distance
with a null that places each mass uniformly within $\pm150$\,MeV (Appendix~\ref{app:exotics-thresholds}). Thirteen states lie within
10\,MeV of an allowed threshold, where 7.0 are expected. Fisher's combined $p$ is $5.4\times10^{-5}$ ($4.1\times10^{-6}$ for
$\pm300$\,MeV) \chk{exotics:B}. Two states carry it: $X(3872)$ sits 0.005\,MeV above $D^0\bar D^{*0}$ and $T_{cc}^+$ 0.36\,MeV below
$D^{*+}D^0$; without them, $p=0.057$. With distances floored at the mass error, $p=5.1\times10^{-3}$. Exotic hadrons do lie near
thresholds. That is the molecular branch of H8, but it is also the standard behaviour of hadronic molecules and threshold cusps in QCD.
Many of these states were found as narrow structures near a threshold, so part of the excess is selection. \emph{Verdict: holds} as a
statement about exotic hadrons; as evidence for H8 over QCD, \emph{holds as a reading}.

\subsection{The minting in hadrons (H9)}\label{sec:exotics-mint}

H9 was conceived after seeing that every established pentaquark carries $c\bar c$; those states agree with it by construction and are
not evidence. We test the four predictions registered as SM7 before this analysis.

\paragraph{I1, no open flavour.} Table~\ref{tab:exo-mint} lists every pentaquark claim we could find and verify. The three claims with an
antiquark that has no partner are all unconfirmed. LEPS's $\Theta^+(1540)$~\cite{nakano2003} was not seen by CLAS with more than 30
times the luminosity~\cite{mckinnon2006}. NA49's $\Xi^{--}(1860)$~\cite{na492004} and H1's $\Theta_c(3100)$~\cite{h12004} were not seen by
HERA-B~\cite{knoepfle2004} and ZEUS~\cite{zeus2004}. Crossing hidden or open with established or not gives Fisher's exact $p=0.17$
(one-sided) \chk{exotics:I1}. The contrast is weak and not clean:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item the open claims date from 2003 and 2004 and rest on small samples;
\item the hidden-charm states come from LHCb's samples of $b$-hadron decays, whose $b\to c\bar cs$ transition supplies a $c\bar c$ pair
\tagP;
\item light hidden-flavour pentaquarks share their quantum numbers with $N^*$ resonances and are not tagged as pentaquarks.
\end{itemize}
SM7 names doubly charmed pentaquarks as forbidden, but H9's rule forbids only those whose light antiquark lacks a partner, such as
$ccuu\bar d$. So the model's lowest doubly charmed level, $([cc]_1[ud]_0)\bar q$ at 4232\,MeV, is allowed. Its isospin-$3/2$ level
$ccuu\bar d$ at $4301\pm12$\,MeV, 540\,MeV above $\Xi_{cc}^{++}\pi^+$, is forbidden. The two readings conflict there; LHCb's null search
in $\Sigma_cD$ and $\Lambda_cD$~\cite{lhcb2024h} bounds production, not existence. \emph{Verdict: holds} as registered, with no
statistical weight.

\paragraph{I2, hidden bottom.} Heavy-quark flavour symmetry lets the light cloud bind a $\Sigma_b\bar B^{(*)}$ pair as it binds
$\Sigma_c\bar D^{(*)}$. Carrying the binding energies of $P_c(4312)$, $P_c(4440)$, $P_c(4457)$ and $P_{\psi s}(4338)$ over unchanged
gives molecular states at 11085\,MeV (below $\Sigma_b\bar B$ at 11093.7\,MeV), 11117 and 11134\,MeV (below $\Sigma_b\bar B^*$ at
11139.0\,MeV), and a $udsb\bar b$ state at 11074\,MeV. Scaling the binding with the reduced mass moves them by up to 33\,MeV
(Appendix~\ref{app:exotics-mint}). The compact tree $([bq][qq])\bar b$ has its lowest level at $10883\pm11$\,MeV, about 200\,MeV lower,
so a measurement would decide between the readings \chk{exotics:I2}. We found no experimental search on arXiv. These states cannot be
made in $b$-hadron decays as the $P_c$ were; photoproduction $\gamma p\to\Upsilon p$ at an electron-ion collider has been proposed
\cite{cao2020}. \emph{Verdict: live.}

\paragraph{I3, the general rule.} ``Every exotic hadron contains a pair of one flavour'' fails on four established states:
$T_{cc}^+$ ($cc\bar u\bar d$), $X_0(2900)$ and $X_1(2900)$ ($\bar c\bar sud$), and $T_{c\bar s0}(2900)$ ($c\bar su\bar d$) \chk{exotics:I3}.
\emph{Verdict: refuted.} H9 itself is restricted to pentaquarks and is not touched.

\paragraph{I4, the splitting $\alpha M/2$.} The rule says the two members of a spin doublet are split by $\alpha M/2$, with $M$ their mean
mass~\cite{codata2022}. $P_c(4440)/P_c(4457)$ suggested it (17.0 against 16.2\,MeV) and does not count. Eligible pairs were fixed from
quark content and $J^P$ only, before any splitting was computed: 25 pairs of observed exotic states (Appendix~\ref{app:exotics-mint}).
Two pass at 1.96$\sigma$, both with errors as large as the prediction, where 4.2 are expected by chance; 22 fail by $3\sigma$ or more.
The two pairs most often read as heavy-quark spin partners fail: $Z_b(10650)-Z_b(10610)=45.0\pm2.5$\,MeV against 38.8\,MeV, and
$Z_c(4020)-Z_c(3900)=123.9\pm6.7$\,MeV against 14.5\,MeV. The colour-spin model gives the $Z_c$ splitting, $131.6\pm8.9$\,MeV
\chk{exotics:I4}. \emph{Verdict: refuted}, with no excess of agreements over chance.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item The binding rule across the two halves of a multiquark was written into the script before any exotic state was computed, by
authors who knew the published masses; the alternative rule is reported for every state (Appendix~\ref{app:exotics-variants}).
\item Sharing KR's heavy-strange hyperfine enhancement between mesons and baryons was fixed before the fit, from KR's published
residuals.
\item One measurement per catalogue state was taken, the most recent one we could read; $X(3915)$, $P_{cs}(4459)$ and $P_c(4337)$ were left
out of the threshold catalogue.
\item The chance probabilities of the proximity assignments, and the threshold tests without the two sub-MeV states and with floored
distances, were added after seeing the results.
\item H9 was conceived after seeing the established pentaquarks. Reading SM7's doubly charmed example by H9's own rule was decided
before the I1 counts.
\item The I4 list was written before any splitting was computed, but all masses were known from the threshold catalogue.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveats.} KR's mass convention for multiquarks dominates the compact masses: the junction variant shifts tetraquarks by 90
to 113\,MeV and pentaquarks by about 205\,MeV, and ordinary hadrons cannot choose between them. $\sigma_{\rm model}$ does not contain the
uncertainty of carrying the model to four and five quarks. Only S-wave ground states are computed. The threshold test inherits the
selection of its catalogue. Appendix~\ref{app:exotics-caveats} gives the full list.

All numbers in this section and in Appendix~\ref{app:exotics} come from the script \texttt{exotic\_hadrons.py} in
\texttt{docs/simulation} (80 of 80 checks, about one minute on one core).

\paragraph{Verdict.} H8 holds as a reading: QCD's colour algebra already writes every hadron as a tree of conjugate halves. KR's
colour-spin model on H8's compact trees, fitted to ordinary hadrons, holds for $T_{cc}^+$ and fails for 5 of 9 measured masses. These
refutations hit that model's compact masses, not GUID. The bound $T_{bb}^-$ that the model and lattice QCD predict, $10399\pm12$\,MeV
here, remains live. Exotic hadrons lie closer to thresholds than chance ($p=5\times10^{-5}$, carried by two states), as hadronic
molecules in QCD do. Of H9's predictions, I1 holds without statistical weight, the hidden-bottom pentaquarks of I2 are live near 11.1\,GeV,
and the general rule I3 and the splitting rule I4 are refuted.
