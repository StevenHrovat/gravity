\section{Gravity simulated: the recorded frame against the coherent field}\label{sec:gravsim}

This section compares the frame, modelled as measurement and feedback (Proposition~\ref{pr:ktm}, Section~\ref{sec:cq}), with a coherent
Newtonian field for many masses (G1), and validates the simulation on two known results: the trade-off of Oppenheim et al.\ (G3) and the
Kafri--Taylor--Milburn channel (G4). The frame's trajectories are product states by construction, and G3 and G4 reproduce published theorems,
so the new content is the many-body comparison here and the two-mass analysis of Section~\ref{sec:bmv}.

\subsection{Two models of the same masses}\label{ss:gs-models}

\paragraph{Masses on a line.} We take $N$ masses $m$ on a line with spacing $d$. Each mass is a two-level system with branch
$s_i\in\{0,1\}$ and position $x_i=i\,d+(s_i-\tfrac12)\Delta x$, so $\delta=\Delta x/d<1$. Phases are measured in units of
$\omega_0=Gm^2/(\hbar d)$, and $\tau=\omega_0t$. A coherent Newtonian field gives the branch configuration $s$ the phase $\tau\,\Omega(s)$,
with $\Omega(s)=\sum_{i<j}d/|x_i(s_i)-x_j(s_j)|$. From $\ket{+}^{\otimes N}$ the state is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gs-psi}
\ket{\Psi(\tau)}=2^{-N/2}\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^N}e^{i\tau\Omega(s)}\ket{s}.
\end{equation}
Since every $s_i$ takes two values, every pair term is affine in each bit, and $\Omega$ is exactly an Ising form:
\begin{gather}
\Omega(s)=\text{const}+\sum_ih_is_i+\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}s_is_j,\qquad J_{ij}=-g(|i-j|),\label{eq:gs-ising}\\
g(n)=\frac1{n-\delta}+\frac1{n+\delta}-\frac2n=\frac{2\delta^2}{n(n^2-\delta^2)}.\nonumber
\end{gather}
The local fields $h_i$ collect $1/(n-\delta)-1/n$ from every partner on the right and $1/(n+\delta)-1/n$ from every partner on the left. A
direct sum over all 256 branches at $N=8$ agrees with~\eqref{eq:gs-ising} to $10^{-14}$ \chk{gravsim:G1}. The nearest-neighbour coupling
$g(1)=2\delta^2/(1-\delta^2)$ is the entangling rate of an isolated pair, the BMV (Bose--Marletto--Vedral) rate of Section~\ref{sec:bmv}. The
couplings fall as $g(n)\approx2\delta^2/n^3$.

Measuring every mass in the $X$ basis after the diagonal phases implements $H^{\otimes N}e^{i\tau\Omega}H^{\otimes N}$ on
$\ket0^{\otimes N}$. This is an IQP (instantaneous quantum polynomial-time) circuit~\cite{bremner2011}: commuting diagonal gates between two
layers of Hadamard gates.

\paragraph{The instance.} We use the instance of Section~\ref{sec:exp}, so $\delta=5/9$, with a uniform diamond sphere of radius
$R=0.879\,\mu$m. The G1 phase unit is $\omega_0=0.1406$\,rad/s. An isolated pair reaches the phase $\pi$ at $\tau_\pi=\pi/g(1)=3.52$. The
Bridge-B rate of Section~\ref{sec:bmv} is $\lambda=(d/R)\,\omega_0=511.7\,\omega_0$ \chk{gravsim:G1}.

\paragraph{The recorded frame.} The frame of Proposition~\ref{pr:ktm} is modelled as follows \tagH.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Each mass $j$ is measured continuously in its branch basis. This contributes the Lindblad term $\kappa(Z_j\rho Z_j-\rho)$ with
$Z_j=1-2s_j$, which decays its coherence at $2\kappa$.
\item The measurement leaves the record $\dd Y_j=2\sqrt\kappa\,\langle Z_j\rangle\,\dd t+\dd W_j$, with independent Wiener increments
$\dd W_j$.
\item The frame turns each record into a position estimate, $\hat s_j\,\dd t=\tfrac12(\dd t-\dd Y_j/2\sqrt\kappa)$.
\item It acts on mass $j$ with the classical Newtonian field of the others' estimates: the phase of branch $s_j=1$ relative to $s_j=0$
advances at $h_j+\sum_iJ_{ij}\hat s_i$.
\end{itemize}
Nothing else couples the masses. Bridge~B sets $2\kappa=\lambda$. The record noise is the only noise the frame adds, which is the balance
rule~\eqref{eq:saturate}; Section~\ref{ss:gs-cq} shows that this is exactly the equality case of the trade-off.

We sample the records exactly. Each mass's branch is drawn with its Born probability. Then $Y_j(t)=2\sqrt\kappa\,z_jt+W_j(t)$, where
$z_j=\pm1$ is the drawn branch. The conditional state of mass $j$ is a function of $Y_j$ alone, and the frame's phases are linear in the
$W_i$. This representation is standard for a measured observable that commutes with the dynamics. We also check it against an Euler
integration of the joint stochastic Schr\"odinger equation. A time step costs three $N\times N$ matrix products, one for the phases and one
for each branch-dependent force, so the cost is $O(N^2)$ per step.

\paragraph{The averaged frame.} Averaged over records, measurement with Markovian feedback obeys the Wiseman--Milburn master
equation~\cite{wiseman1993}. Here every operator is diagonal in the branch basis, so it integrates in closed form:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:gs-me}
\rho_{ss'}(\tau)=\rho_{ss'}(0)\exp\Bigl\{\tau\Bigl[i\bigl(\Omega(s)-\Omega(s')\bigr)-\kappa\sum_k(1-z_kz'_k)\\
-\tfrac12\sum_i\bigl(F_i(s)-F_i(s')\bigr)^2\Bigr]\Bigr\},
\end{multline}
with $F_i(s)=(4\sqrt\kappa)^{-1}\sum_jJ_{ij}s_j$. The three terms in the exponent are:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item the coherent field's commutator $-i[H_Q,\rho]$, which the frame reproduces exactly on average;
\item the measurement dephasing;
\item the record noise fed back as force noise, which dephases masses that share a record in a correlated way.
\end{enumerate}
Mass $j$ loses coherence at $\Gamma_j=2\kappa+\sum_iJ_{ij}^2/(32\kappa)$. This is at least $\frac12(\sum_iJ_{ij}^2)^{1/2}$, with equality at
$\kappa=(\sum_iJ_{ij}^2)^{1/2}/8$. For a single partner the minimum is $\omega/2$, half the entangling rate. This is the qubit form of the
Kafri--Taylor--Milburn (KTM) minimum~\cite{ktm}, used again in Section~\ref{ss:gs-ktm} and Section~\ref{sec:bmv}.

\subsection{Many masses: what each picture produces}\label{ss:gs-many}

\paragraph{The coherent field builds volume-law entanglement, slowly.} We build~\eqref{eq:gs-psi} exactly for $N=8$ to 24. The largest case is
a statevector of $2^{24}$ amplitudes, 256\,MB in double precision. Let $S$ be the entropy, in bits, of the reduced state of the first $N/2$
masses. We compare it with the Page entropy, the mean of $S$ over random pure states~\cite{page1993}:
$S_{\rm Page}=[\psi(q^2+1)-\psi(q+1)-(q-1)/2q]/\ln2$, with $q=2^{N/2}$ and $\psi$ the digamma function. Table~\ref{tab:gs-entropy} shows how
$S$ grows \chk{gravsim:G1}.
At $\tau_\pi$ the cut carries about one ebit (one maximally entangled pair): 1.009 at both $N=20$ and 24, the bond across the cut. $S$ then
grows as longer couplings wind past $2\pi$. Over twelve times with $\tau=10^4$ to $10^7$ it averages 9.282 at $N=20$ (Page: 9.279); at
$N=24$ and $\tau=10^5$ it is 11.270 (Page: 11.279). Because the couplings fall as $1/n^3$, the state stays close to an area law at the times a laboratory can reach ($t\lesssim100$\,s,
$\tau\lesssim14$). It reaches Page entanglement only after hours.

\paragraph{Pairwise entanglement dies of monogamy.} The two-body marginals need no statevector. In~\eqref{eq:gs-psi} the sum over the other
masses factorises:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gs-pair}
\rho_{ij}\bigl[(a,b),(a',b')\bigr]=\tfrac14\,e^{i\tau[e(a,b)-e(a',b')]}\prod_{k\ne i,j}\frac{1+e^{i\tau[J_{ik}(a-a')+J_{jk}(b-b')]}}{2},
\end{equation}
with $e(a,b)=h_ia+h_jb+J_{ij}ab$. At $N=16$, 20 and 24 this agrees with partial traces of the statevector to $2.3\times10^{-14}$. It runs at
$N=4000$, where all 3999 neighbouring pairs take about one second \chk{gravsim:G1}. We measure entanglement by the negativity
$\mathcal N$~\cite{vw}, the magnitude of the sum of the negative eigenvalues of the partial transpose~\cite{peres1996,horodecki1996}
(Primer~\ref{box:qi}). The results at $N=4000$ are these.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Bulk neighbours reach $\mathcal N=0.153$ at $\tau=1.27$ (9\,s). At $\tau=1.3$ every one of the 3999 neighbouring pairs is entangled;
the minimum is 0.1528, and the end pair has 0.212.
\item A bulk pair is first separable at $\tau=2.67$ (19\,s). It is separable at $\tau_\pi$, where an isolated pair would have
$\mathcal N=1/2$.
\item No scanned pair is entangled beyond $\tau\approx19.5$ (2.3\,min; scan to $\tau=300$). An isolated pair keeps oscillating up to
$\frac12|\sin(g(1)\tau/2)|=0.499$.
\end{itemize}
At $N=16$ the entangled pairs number 15 of 120 at $\tau=1.3$, none at $\tau_\pi$, two (the end pairs) at $\tau=10$, and none at $\tau=10^3$.
Each mass is entangled with both of its neighbours, and entanglement is monogamous. The coherent field's entanglement therefore becomes
multipartite. At scale, pairwise negativity is the wrong witness, and the half-chain entropy is the right one.

\paragraph{Porter--Thomas statistics emerge with $N$.} Let $p(x)$ be the distribution of the $X$-basis outcomes $x\in\{0,1\}^N$. A random
quantum state makes $2^Np(x)$ exponentially distributed; this is the Porter--Thomas distribution~\cite{porter1956}. We use two moments, the collision probability $Z=2^N\sum_xp(x)^2$ and the
third moment $M_3=2^{2N}\sum_xp(x)^3$, which are 2 and 6 under Porter--Thomas. Anticoncentration ($Z=O(1)$) is the regime in which classical sampling of IQP circuits is argued to be hard~\cite{bremner2011,bremner2016}.

As a reference, IQP circuits with independent uniform angles give $\mathbb E[Z]=2-2^{-N}$ exactly: in the fourth moment the phase average
forces $\{s_1,s_3\}=\{s_2,s_4\}$ bit by bit, a quadratic term forbids mixing the two pairings, and $2\cdot4^N-2^N$ quadruples remain. Sixty
random instances give $2.036\pm0.018$ at $N=10$ and $1.9953\pm0.0033$ at $N=14$ (1$\sigma$) \chk{gravsim:G1}.

For the gravitational circuit:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $Z=2^N$ at $t=0$, and $Z=1.38$ to 1.41 at $\tau_\pi$ for every $N$. This is flatter than Porter--Thomas: the near-cluster state has
almost uniform $X$ outcomes.
\item At $\tau=10$ (71\,s), $Z=1.74$, 2.10, 2.78, 3.78 and 5.16 for $N=8$, 12, 16, 20 and 24. $Z$ grows with $N$, so the output is not
anticoncentrated.
\item From $\tau\approx10^3$ (2\,h), Porter--Thomas statistics emerge. Over twelve times with $\tau=10^4$ to $10^7$ (three at $N=24$),
$|Z-2|=0.113$, 0.069, 0.012 and 0.002 and $|M_3-6|=1.35$, 0.79, 0.11 and 0.02 for $N=12$, 16, 20 and 24.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The recorded frame at $N=4000$.} We run 4000 masses, 16 trajectories and 200 steps up to $t=4/\lambda$ (55.6\,ms) at the Bridge-B
rate \chk{gravsim:G1}. The time loop takes 8.2\,s on one core, or 2.6\,ms per trajectory-step. Building the matrices takes 1.0\,s, and they
occupy 384\,MB.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Entanglement is exactly zero.} Each trajectory is a product state by construction. A direct Euler integration of the joint
stochastic Schr\"odinger equation for $N=4$ confirms this without the product ansatz: the largest second Schmidt coefficient is
$1.8\times10^{-15}$.
\item \emph{Coherence decays as $e^{-\lambda t}$.} The mean $|\rho_{01}|$ over 64\,000 mass-trajectories equals $\tfrac12e^{-\lambda t}$ within
five standard errors at $t=1$, 2 and $3/\lambda$. The fitted rate is $0.997\lambda$. The complex mean matches the closed form
$\tfrac12e^{-\lambda t}e^{-ih_jt}\prod_i(1+e^{-iJ_{ij}t})/2\cdot e^{-\sum_iJ_{ij}^2t/32\kappa}$. Record noise adds
$\sum_iJ_{ij}^2/(32\kappa)=3.8\times10^{-7}\lambda$ to the rate.
\item \emph{The mean force is Newton's.} At the mean records the frame's force equals a direct double sum over masses and branches of
$Gm^2\,\mathrm{sgn}(x_i-x_j)/(x_i-x_j)^2$. The agreement is $10^{-13}$ of the pair-force scale, and Newton's third law holds to
$1.9\times10^{-14}$.
\item \emph{With the record noise included}, let $P_j=\int_0^TF_j\,\dd t$ be the impulse on mass $j$ over the run. Here
$T=4/\lambda=7.8\times10^{-3}/\omega_0$, forces are in units of $Gm^2/d^2$, and time is in units of $1/\omega_0$. Averaged over trajectories,
$P_j$ equals $T$ times the Newtonian force for all 4000 masses. The mean squared Student $t$ is 1.09, against 1.15 expected for 15 degrees of
freedom. For the end mass the ratio is $1.13\pm0.17$.
\item \emph{Momentum is conserved only on average.} The total impulse $\sum_jP_j$ has mean 0.16 and standard deviation 0.40 over the 16
trajectories. It is zero on average within the standard error of 0.10, as Newton's third law requires, but not per trajectory. This is the
frame's diffusion, the $D_2$ of Section~\ref{sec:cq}.
\item \emph{The output is a product distribution.} Per trajectory $p(x)=\prod_jp_j(x_j)$, so
$Z=\prod_j2(p_{j,0}^2+p_{j,1}^2)=\prod_j(1+\langle\sigma_x\rangle_j^2)$; enumeration at $N=10$ confirms this. The mean $\log_2Z$ falls from
4000 at $t=0$ to 1047, 315, 103 and 35 at $t=1$, 2, 3 and $4/\lambda$, towards 0, the uniform distribution. Porter--Thomas statistics never
appear.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The averaged frame never entangles.} Product trajectories make the mixture separable. Equation~\eqref{eq:gs-me} lets us test this
independently, at every measurement strength. For $N=4$ and 6 we take 13 strengths, $\kappa/\kappa_{\rm opt}=10^{-3}$ to $10^3$, and 25
times $\tau=10^{-2}$ to 50. The largest negative eigenvalue of the partial transpose, over all bipartitions (7 and 31 cuts) and all pairs, is
exactly 0; the coherent field gives $-0.5$ for the same masses \chk{gravsim:G1}. Two Monte Carlo checks at $N=4$ with $\kappa=0.2\,\omega_0$
reproduce~\eqref{eq:gs-me}: 40\,000 exactly sampled trajectories (largest $|z|$ over the 256 matrix elements 2.5) and 8000 Euler-integrated
trajectories of the joint equation (largest difference 0.005).

\paragraph{Cost.} On one core of a shared machine (timings vary by about 20\% between runs), the coherent field at $N=24$ takes 4.5\,s per
time for the state and $Z$, and 29\,s for $S$, in 256\,MB \chk{gravsim:G1}. Its time grows as $2^{1.21N}$ for $N=16$ to 24; the exponent
includes the $N2^N$ cost of the Hadamard transform and memory effects. Scaling conservatively by exactly 2 per added mass from $N=24$ gives at
least $10^{1198}$\,s at $N=4000$ (the fitted law gives $10^{1450}$\,s), and the statevector would need $10^{1205}$ bytes. The recorded frame
does the same 4000 masses in 8.2\,s for $16\times200$ trajectory-steps, in 384\,MB, at $O(N^2)$ per step.

\subsection{The classical-quantum toy and the trade-off}\label{ss:gs-cq}

\paragraph{Model.} We take one qubit with Lindblad operator $\sigma_z$, coupled to a classical momentum $p$ (Primer~\ref{box:cq}). The joint
object is a $p$-dependent $2\times2$ block $\rho(p)$, with $\int\Tr\rho(p)\,\dd p=1$. It evolves as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gs-cqme}
\partial_t\rho(p)=D_0\bigl(\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z-\rho\bigr)-\frac{D_1}{2}\,\partial_p\{\sigma_z,\rho(p)\}+\frac{D_2}{2}\,\partial_p^2\rho(p).
\end{equation}
In blocks, $\rho_{00}$ drifts at $+D_1$ and $\rho_{11}$ at $-D_1$, both diffusing with $D_2$. The coherence $\rho_{01}$ does not drift; it
diffuses and decays at $2D_0$.

Start from $\ket+\bra+$ times a Gaussian of width $s_0$. The blocks stay Gaussian, with common variance $s^2=s_0^2+D_2t$. A block is positive
iff $|\rho_{01}|^2\le\rho_{00}\rho_{11}$. Since $N(p;a,s)N(p;-a,s)=N(p;0,s)^2e^{-a^2/s^2}$, the condition does not depend on $p$. It reads
$e^{-4D_0t}\le e^{-D_1^2t^2/(s_0^2+D_2t)}$. Positivity therefore holds for all $t$ iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gs-bound}
D_1^2\le4D_0D_2 .
\end{equation}
Beyond the bound it fails from $t^*=4D_0s_0^2/(D_1^2-4D_0D_2)$ on \tagP. The joint operator $\sum_p\ket p\bra p\otimes\rho(p)$ is
block-diagonal, so its spectrum is the union of the blocks' spectra.

\paragraph{Mapping to the trade-off.} Oppenheim et al.\ write the master equation with moments $D_n$ that already carry the factor $1/n!$.
They write the back-reaction as $-\partial_p(D_1^{0\alpha}L_\alpha\rho+\text{h.c.})$~\cite[Eq.~(9)]{osswd}. For~\eqref{eq:gs-cqme} this gives
$D_1^{\rm OSSWD}=D_1/2$ and $D_2^{\rm OSSWD}=D_2/2$. Their trade-off~\cite[Eq.~(23)]{osswd}, $D_1D_0^{-1}D_1^\dagger\preceq2D_2$, is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tradeoff} of Part~I. It then reads $(D_1/2)^2/D_0\le2\,(D_2/2)$, which is exactly~\eqref{eq:gs-bound}. Eq.~\eqref{eq:tradeoff} is
therefore correct as written, in Oppenheim et al.'s normalisation \chk{gravsim:G3}.

\paragraph{Numerics.} We discretise $p\in[-22,22]$ on 4401 points (spacing 0.01) with second-order central differences. We evolve to $T=3$
with a Krylov matrix exponential, taking $D_0=D_2=1$ and $s_0=0.1$ and sampling 61 times. The largest deviation from the exact Gaussian
solution is $1.1\times10^{-4}$ of the peak. Table~\ref{tab:gs-cq} gives the results \chk{gravsim:G3}. Inside the bound and at equality no block ever turns negative. At 1\% beyond the
bound positivity fails first at $t=1.00$, as predicted, and at twice the bound the smallest block eigenvalue reaches $-0.24$ of the peak.
Bisection on $D_2$ finds the smallest diffusion that keeps positivity up to $T=50$: $0.9998=D_1^2/(4D_0)-s_0^2/T$, which tends to the bound
as $T$ grows.

\paragraph{What equality is.} Measure $\sigma_z$ at strength $D_0$ and drive $p$ by the record, $\dd p=\alpha\,\dd Y$. By It\^o calculus the
joint law of $(p,\rho_c)$ obeys~\eqref{eq:gs-cqme} with $D_1=2\alpha\sqrt{D_0}$ and $D_2=\alpha^2$: exactly the equality case. Any additional
independent noise on $p$ raises $D_2$ above the bound. We sample 400\,000 trajectories at $t=1$, with $D_0=1$, $\alpha=1$ and $s_0=0$
\chk{gravsim:G3}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item They reproduce $\int\rho_{01}\,\dd p=\tfrac12e^{-2D_0t}$ (0.06784 against 0.06767) and the branch drift $D_1t$ (2.001 against 2).
\item At equality $p$ determines the conditional state, so $p$ is a perfect record and every block $\rho(p)$ is pure. The mixedness
$4\det\rho(p)/(\Tr\rho(p))^2$ is at most $8\times10^{-4}$, set by the bin width.
\item With $D_2$ doubled the blocks are mixed. At $p=0$ the mixedness is 0.8646, against $1-e^{-(4D_0D_2-D_1^2)t/D_2}=0.8647$.
\end{itemize}
The balance rule~\eqref{eq:saturate} therefore says that the frame keeps every bit of its record. It gives minimal diffusion for the given
decoherence, or equivalently minimal decoherence for the given diffusion.

\subsection{Two oscillators: the quantum coupling against the Kafri--Taylor--Milburn channel}\label{ss:gs-ktm}

\paragraph{Model.} Two oscillators interact through the linearised Newtonian coupling. In units $\hbar=m=\Omega=1$ the Hamiltonian is
$H=\sum_k(p_k^2+x_k^2)/2-Kx_1x_2$, with $K=0.1$. The covariance matrix $\sigma$ of $(x_1,p_1,x_2,p_2)$ obeys
$\dot\sigma=A\sigma+\sigma A^{\mathsf T}+D$, which we solve exactly with an augmented matrix exponential. The log-negativity is
$E_N=\max(0,-\log_2 2\tilde\nu_-)$, with $\tilde\nu_-$ the smallest symplectic eigenvalue of the partially transposed $\sigma$. The
Peres--Horodecki criterion is necessary and sufficient for two-mode Gaussian states~\cite{simon2000}.

In the KTM channel~\cite{ktm} each $x_k$ is measured at strength $k$, with Lindblad term $k(x\rho x-\tfrac12\{x^2,\rho\})$. The record
$\hat x_k$ drives the force $K\hat x_k$ on the other mass. Averaged over records, the dynamics is the quantum coupling plus a momentum
diffusion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gs-ktm}
D(k)=k+\frac{K^2}{4k}\ \ge\ K \qquad (\text{equality at } k=K/2).
\end{equation}
This is KTM's Eq.~(14) with their $\Gamma=k$. Its minimum is their Eq.~(15), $\Gamma=K/2$, and their Eq.~(19), heating $\hbar K$.

\paragraph{Results} \chk{gravsim:G4}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{The quantum coupling entangles.} The largest log-negativity is 0.145 from the vacuum (at $t=31$). It is 2.96 from vacua squeezed
locally by $r=1$ in each mode (at $t=141$).
\item \emph{The KTM channel never does.} We take nine strengths $k=(10^{-2}$ to $10^2)\,K/2$ and $t\le200$. The smallest $2\tilde\nu_-$ is
$\ge1$ to $10^{-9}$, so the log-negativity is exactly 0 for both initial states.
\item \emph{The minimum is the separability edge.} The quantum coupling plus dephasing at $0.99K$, just below the channel's minimum,
entangles the vacuum ($E_N=1.3\times10^{-4}$), as KTM's Eq.~(18) states.
\item \emph{Trajectories} (4000, $\dd t=0.002$, $T=10$, $k=K/2$). The conditional covariance stays block-diagonal (cross block exactly 0)
and pure ($4\det V_1=0.99995$), so every trajectory is a product state. The trajectory-averaged means follow the quantum mean dynamics
from $\langle x_1\rangle=1$ (largest $|z|=2.1$). The covariance of the means plus the conditional covariance equals the master-equation
covariance (largest $|z|=0.98$).
\item \emph{The instance.} KTM's Eq.~(5) gives $K=2Gm^2/d^3=1.465\times10^{-28}$\,N/m. The minimal coherence decay for $\Delta x=250\,\mu$m is
then $K\Delta x^2/(2\hbar)=0.0434$\,s$^{-1}$. This equals the qubit minimum $\omega/2=0.0628$\,s$^{-1}$ times $(d^2-\Delta x^2)/d^2$: the same
physics, linearised. The Bridge-B rate of Section~\ref{sec:bmv} lies far above this minimum. The reason is that it is set by each mass's own
record, not by the pair coupling.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.} Four checks were changed after a first run had failed them; the script records each change.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item We first tested pairwise entanglement at $\tau_\pi$ and found every pair separable. We kept that result, and moved the pair check to
$\tau=1.3$, near the bulk maximum, with a scan in $\tau$.
\item A 3\% tolerance on $Z-2$ failed at $N=16$ (deviation 3.4\%). We replaced it by a convergence check over $N$ and a 1\% check at
$N\ge20$, and added three late times at $N=24$.
\item We first normalised the force comparison by the near-cancelling bulk sum. It now uses the pair-force scale.
\item We report the conservative $2^N$ extrapolation instead of the fitted exponent.
\end{itemize}
The parameters of G3 and G4 were fixed before the runs. $K=0.1$ is far stronger than gravity, chosen so that the entanglement is visible.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{The $2^N$ figure is the brute-force cost.} At laboratory times the coherent chain carries about one bit across the cut, and
tensor networks would simulate it cheaply; it is hard only at late times. The hardness of IQP sampling~\cite{bremner2011,bremner2016} is a
complexity-theoretic conjecture for typical circuits, not a theorem about this structured one.
\item \emph{Only each mass's own record is measured.} The inter-mass cross term of the Di\'osi--Penrose kernel is not modelled; for the
instance it is small (Section~\ref{sec:bmv}).
\item \emph{The models are idealised.} The masses are two-level systems with branches along the line, and a ring was not run. G3 has one
qubit, one classical variable and constant coefficients. G4 has symmetric oscillators and unit detection efficiency.
\end{itemize}

All numbers in this section come from \texttt{docs/simulation/gravity\_simulated.py} (110 of 110 checks, about three minutes on one core;
deterministic, with fixed seeds).

\paragraph{Verdict.} At 4000 masses the frame reproduces Newton's law on average, decoheres each mass at $\lambda$ and adds momentum noise.
It never entangles, at any measurement strength (\emph{proven}, by construction and by the averaged master equation). The coherent field
entangles the same masses multipartitely (\emph{proven} for $N\le24$). It reaches Page entropy and Porter--Thomas statistics only after hours,
while its pairwise entanglement dies within seconds. Two known theorems are reproduced as validations (\emph{proven}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the trade-off of Section~\ref{sec:cq} is correct as written, and its equality case is the measurement-and-feedback unravelling with a
pure record;
\item the Kafri--Taylor--Milburn minimum is the exact separability edge.
\end{itemize}
