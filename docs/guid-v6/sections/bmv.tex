\section{The decisive experiment under Bridge B}\label{sec:bmv}

Gravitationally mediated entanglement of two masses (Bose et al.~\cite{bose}, Marletto and Vedral~\cite{mv}; Section~\ref{sec:exp}) is GUID's
sharpest test. A classical frame cannot entangle (Lemma~\ref{lem:classical}), so GUID predicts zero negativity. Bridge~B adds a second
prediction: every mass decoheres at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate $\lambda=Gm^2/(\hbar R)$ (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}). We ask whether the
experiment can still tell GUID from quantum gravity if decoherence at that rate is real, and whether the published spin witness certifies
what it is meant to certify. The answers: no geometry of two solid spheres builds up entanglement at the Bridge-B rate, whether gravity is
quantum or not (exactly, and for any two bodies at the exact Di\'osi--Penrose rate); and the witness as printed in the arXiv version is not
valid, while a corrected two-setting witness needs $\omega/\lambda>3.81$ under dephasing.

\subsection{Setting and hypotheses}\label{ss:bmv-setting}

Two masses $m$ have centres $d$ apart, and each is split by $\Delta x$ along the line joining them. A coherent Newtonian field gives the
branch pair $(a,b)$ the phase $\varphi_{ab}=Gm^2t/(\hbar d_{ab})$, with $d_{00}=d_{11}=d$, $d-\Delta x$ for the inner pair and $d+\Delta x$
for the outer pair. Up to local phases the state is $\tfrac12(\ket{00}+\ket{01}+\ket{10}+e^{i\Sigma}\ket{11})$, with $\Sigma=\omega t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmv-omega}
\omega=\frac{Gm^2}{\hbar}\Bigl[\frac1{d-\Delta x}+\frac1{d+\Delta x}-\frac2d\Bigr]=\frac{Gm^2}{\hbar}\,\frac{2\Delta x^2}{d(d-\Delta x)(d+\Delta x)},
\end{equation}
and negativity $\mathcal N=\tfrac12|\sin(\Sigma/2)|$~\cite{vw}. For the instance of Bose et al.\ ($m=10^{-14}$\,kg, $\Delta x=250\,\mu$m,
$d=450\,\mu$m, diamond radius $R=0.879\,\mu$m), $\omega=0.1256$\,rad/s. The phase reaches 1\,rad after 7.96\,s and $\pi$ after 25.0\,s
\chk{gravsim:G2}.

\paragraph{The Di\'osi--Penrose rate.} Di\'osi's master equation~\cite{diosi87} is
\begin{equation*}
\dot\rho=-\frac{G}{2\hbar}\iint\frac{[f(x),[f(y),\rho]]}{|x-y|}\,\dd^3x\,\dd^3y ,
\end{equation*}
with $f$ the mass-density operator. Under it the coherence between two mass configurations decays at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmv-dp}
\Gamma=\frac{I(\Delta f,\Delta f)}{2\hbar},\qquad I(f,g)=G\iint\frac{f(x)\,g(y)}{|x-y|}\,\dd^3x\,\dd^3y ,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta f$ is the difference of the two densities. For a uniform sphere displaced by $b$ this gives $\Gamma=E_G/\hbar$, with
$E_G=(GM^2/R)(2\ell^2-\tfrac32\ell^3+\tfrac15\ell^5)$ for $\ell=b/2R\le1$ and $E_G=GM^2(6/5R-1/b)$ beyond~\cite{penrose96,howl2019}. We checked
this by radial quadrature of $I$: the self-energy integral is $\tfrac65GM^2/R$ to machine precision, and the closed form is reproduced to
$2\times10^{-16}$ \chk{gravsim:G2}. Beyond one diameter the mutual term is exactly $GM^2/b$ (the shell theorem), so the phases between
non-overlapping spheres are exactly those of point masses. Bridge~B's $\lambda=Gm^2/(\hbar R)$ differs from $E_G/\hbar$ only by the factor
$6/5-R/b$, between 0.7 and 1.2. For the instance $\lambda=71.97$\,s$^{-1}$ (coherence time 13.9\,ms) and $E_G/\hbar=86.1$\,s$^{-1}$.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} We compare five evolutions of the same two masses. In each, coherence factors multiply the coherent-field state
element by element.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{(Q)}: the coherent field, with no collapse.
\item \textbf{(Q+DP local)} and \textbf{(Q+DP exact)}: (Q) with each mass dephasing independently, at $\lambda$ and at the exact sphere rate
$E_G/\hbar$ respectively.
\item \textbf{(Q+DP full)}: (Q) with the joint master equation~\eqref{eq:bmv-dp} for the total density of both masses. Its cross term
$I(\Delta f_A,\Delta f_B)/\hbar=\mp\omega$ makes the coherence between branches that differ in both masses decay at $2\Gamma\mp\omega$.
\item \textbf{(G)}: the recorded frame of Section~\ref{sec:gravsim}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gs-me}, with $2\kappa=\lambda$. We also run it at the
Kafri--Taylor--Milburn optimum $\kappa=\omega/8$, the least noisy classical channel.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The ratio \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho} and its supremum}\label{ss:bmv-rho}

Under Bridge~B the entangling phase built up per coherence time is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmv-rho}
\rho=\frac{\omega}{\lambda}=R\Bigl[\frac1{d-\Delta x}+\frac1{d+\Delta x}-\frac2d\Bigr]=\frac{2R\Delta x^2}{d(d-\Delta x)(d+\Delta x)} .
\end{equation}
$G$, $\hbar$ and $m$ cancel, so $\rho$ is fixed by geometry alone. Numerically it is independent of $m$ from $10^{-18}$ to 1\,kg to
$4\times10^{-16}$. For the instance $\rho=1.745\times10^{-3}$, or $1.458\times10^{-3}$ at the exact sphere rate, as in~\chk{Q19}
\chk{gravsim:G2}.

\paragraph{Supremum.} The spheres must not overlap, so the closest approach is $u=d-\Delta x\ge2R$. Then
$\rho=2R\Delta x^2/[u(u+\Delta x)(u+2\Delta x)]$. Its derivative in $\Delta x$ has numerator $\Delta x(2u^2+3u\Delta x)>0$, so $\rho$
increases towards $R/u$, and it decreases with $u$. Hence $\sup\rho=1/2$, approached as $\Delta x\to\infty$ with the inner branches touching
and never attained.

Bose et al.'s closest approach of $200\,\mu$m, chosen so that the Casimir--Polder potential is a tenth of the gravitational one~\cite{bose},
gives $\rho\le R/u=4.4\times10^{-3}$. For any arrangement of two spheres, two of the four branch distances enter with a minus sign, so
$\omega\le(Gm^2/\hbar)(1/r_{00}+1/r_{11})\le Gm^2/(\hbar R)$ and $\rho\le1$. This bound is approached by parallel splits side by side at
contact as $\Delta x\to\infty$; 188\,398 random non-overlapping geometries all stay below it (largest 0.446). At the exact sphere rate the
suprema are 1/2 for collinear splits (now as $\Delta x\to0$ at $d=2R+\Delta x$; it is 5/12 as $\Delta x\to\infty$) and 5/6 for any
arrangement \chk{gravsim:G2}.

\subsection{An exact threshold for entanglement}\label{ss:bmv-threshold}

\begin{proposition}[threshold]\label{pr:bmv-threshold}
Let two qubits evolve with the Ising coupling of~\eqref{eq:bmv-omega}, with each coherence decaying as $e^{-\lambda t}$ through independent
local dephasing. Some product input becomes entangled at some time iff $\omega>2\lambda$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{If.} From $\ket{++}$ the smallest eigenvalue of the partial transpose is $\tfrac t4(2\lambda-\omega)+O(t^2)$; at $\omega=3\lambda$ the
computed slope is $-0.25$ \chk{gravsim:G2}. \emph{Only if.} For $\lambda\ge\omega/2$ the generator is the sum of two parts. The first is the
averaged Kafri--Taylor--Milburn feedback generator at $\kappa=\omega/8$, which is the coherent coupling plus dephasing at $\omega/2$, the
record average of an LOCC protocol (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gs-me}). The second is local dephasing at $\lambda-\omega/2\ge0$. Both generate LOCC
semigroups. By the Trotter product formula the evolution is a limit of LOCC maps, and the separable set is closed \tagP.
\end{proof}

The proof works instant by instant, so it also covers geometries that change in time, provided $\lambda(t)\ge\omega(t)/2$ throughout.
Bisection over 40\,s of evolution gives the threshold 2.0000 \chk{gravsim:G2}. With the full kernel the correlated term lowers the threshold
to $\sqrt2$: bisection gives 1.41421, and at first order the eigenvalues of the partial transpose are $2\lambda\pm\sqrt2\,\omega$. The
threshold coincides with the minimum decoherence $\omega/2$ that a classical channel needs (Section~\ref{ss:gs-ktm}).

\begin{corollary}[any two bodies]\label{co:bmv-any}
For any two bodies, (Q) with independent dephasing at the exact Di\'osi--Penrose rates never becomes entangled.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The entangling rate is $\omega=|I(\Delta f_A,\Delta f_B)|/\hbar$, and the Di\'osi--Penrose rates are
$\Gamma_A=I(\Delta f_A,\Delta f_A)/2\hbar$ and $\Gamma_B=I(\Delta f_B,\Delta f_B)/2\hbar$. The kernel $1/|x-y|$ is positive (its Fourier transform is $4\pi/k^2$), so Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\omega\le2\sqrt{\Gamma_A\Gamma_B}$. With
$2\kappa_{A,B}=\Gamma_{A,B}/2$ the decomposition used in the proof of Proposition~\ref{pr:bmv-threshold} applies with unequal rates.
\end{proof}

Equality is impossible for disjoint bodies. The largest $\omega/(2\sqrt{\Gamma_A\Gamma_B})$ among the random geometries is 0.22
\chk{gravsim:G2}. Decoherence at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate always meets the classical-channel minimum. Table~\ref{tab:bmv-hyp} shows the
instance in time.

\subsection{The spin witness of Bose et al., checked and corrected}\label{ss:bmv-witness}

We read the arXiv text, arXiv:1707.06050, which has a single version (v1, 19 July 2017). The published PRL text~\cite{bose} was not read,
because the publisher's host was outside the network we could reach; whether the journal version corrects the operator is open. All
statements below refer to the arXiv text \chk{witness:B1}. In spin form, with $\ket\uparrow=\ket0$, the state after the two Stern--Gerlach
interferometers (the unnumbered equation after their Eq.~(6)) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmv-spin}
\ket\Psi=\tfrac12\bigl[\ket{00}+e^{ia}\ket{01}+e^{ib}\ket{10}+\ket{11}\bigr],\qquad a=\Delta\varphi_{LR},\ b=\Delta\varphi_{RL},\ \Sigma=a+b ,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal N=\tfrac12\sin(\Sigma/2)$.

\paragraph{The printed witness is not a witness.} The paper defines $W=|\langle\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_z\rangle-\langle\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_z\rangle|$
and states that $W>1$ proves entanglement. The two terms share $\sigma_z$ on the second spin, so
$\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_z-\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_z=\sqrt2\,\sigma_n\otimes\sigma_z$ with $n=(\hat x-\hat y)/\sqrt2$. A product state with spin~1
along $n$ and spin~2 up gives $W=\sqrt2$, the largest value any state can give. An optimiser over product states finds $\sqrt2$, and 400\,000
random product states reach 1.4097. On the proposal's own states the printed $W$ reaches at most $\sqrt2$, the value a product state already
gives, so it can never certify entanglement there. At $a=0$, $b=1$\,rad it is 0.651, although the state is entangled ($\mathcal N=0.240$).
The value $W\approx1.16$ that the paper quotes for $\Delta\varphi_{LR}\approx-0.2$, $\Delta\varphi_{RL}\approx0.7$ lies below $\sqrt2$ and
certifies nothing.

\paragraph{Valid two-setting witnesses.} Let $a_1\perp a_2$ and $b_1\perp b_2$ be unit axes, and let a product state have Bloch vectors $u$
and $v$. By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmv-lemma}
|(u\!\cdot\!a_1)(v\!\cdot\!b_1)+(u\!\cdot\!a_2)(v\!\cdot\!b_2)|\le\sqrt{(u\!\cdot\!a_1)^2+(u\!\cdot\!a_2)^2}\,\sqrt{(v\!\cdot\!b_1)^2+(v\!\cdot\!b_2)^2}\le1 ,
\end{equation}
and convexity extends the bound to every separable state~\cite{guhne2009}. The printed operator fails exactly because $b_1=b_2=\hat z$. In
the symmetric gauge $a=b=\Sigma/2$ the corrected witness
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmv-wc}
W_c=|\langle\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x\rangle+\langle\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_z\rangle|\ \le1\ \text{(separable)},\qquad W_c=1+\sin(\Sigma/2)=1+2\mathcal N
\end{equation}
detects every $\Sigma>0$ \chk{witness:B2}. In the laboratory gauge the axes rotate by $\theta=(\Delta\varphi_{LR}-\Delta\varphi_{RL})/2$:
$x'=\cos\theta\,\hat x-\sin\theta\,\hat y$ and $y'=\sin\theta\,\hat x+\cos\theta\,\hat y$ on spin~1, $x''=\cos\theta\,\hat x+\sin\theta\,\hat y$ on
spin~2, with $z$ unchanged. Then $|\langle\sigma_{x'}\otimes\sigma_{x''}\rangle+\langle\sigma_{y'}\otimes\sigma_z\rangle|=1+\sin(\Sigma/2)$
for every $(a,b)$. This is the readjustment ``in accordance to these local phases'' that the paper asks for. The angle is local data: the
single-spin Bloch vectors are $\cos(\Sigma/2)(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta,0)$ and $\cos(\Sigma/2)(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0)$, measured on
calibration runs separate from the witness runs.

\paragraph{Gauge-free and weak alternatives.} Let $T_{ij}=\langle\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j\rangle$ be the correlation matrix. Separable states
satisfy $\|T\|_1\le1$ (trace norm): for a product state $T=uv^{\mathsf T}$, so $\|T\|_1=|u||v|\le1$, and convexity does the rest. This is the
two-qubit case of de Vicente's criterion~\cite{devicente2007}. It needs nine settings and no phase knowledge, and on~\eqref{eq:bmv-spin}
$\|T\|_1=1+2\sin(\Sigma/2)$ in every gauge. Its estimate is biased upward: on a product state the bias is $+0.250$, $+0.075$ and $+0.023$ at
100, 1000 and 10\,000 runs per setting. It therefore needs a calibrated null, whereas $W_c$ has an unbiased linear estimator and is the one
for a hypothesis test \chk{witness:B4}. The variant $|\langle\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_z\rangle+\langle\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y\rangle|$ satisfies
the lemma but is weak. In the paper's gauge ($a=0$) it equals $1-\cos\Sigma$ and detects only $\Sigma>\pi/2$; in the symmetric gauge it is
0 for every $\Sigma$ \chk{witness:B2}. Table~\ref{tab:bmv-w} collects the values at the paper's target phase.

\paragraph{Under dephasing.} Let each mass dephase at $\lambda$, with $q=e^{-\lambda t}$ and $s=\sin(\omega t/2)$. In the symmetric gauge
$T=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}q^2&0&0\\0&0&qs\\0&qs&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$: $\langle xx\rangle$ lives on the coherences between branches
that differ in both masses, and $\langle yz\rangle$, $\langle zy\rangle$ on those that differ in one \chk{witness:B3}. The trace norm is
$q^2+2qs=1+(\omega-2\lambda)t+\dots$, so its threshold in $\omega/\lambda$ is 2, as tight as the negativity. $W_c=q^2+qs=1+(\omega/2-2\lambda)t+\dots$
has threshold 4. The pair $|\langle yz\rangle+\langle zy\rangle|=2qs$ peaks at a finite time, where $\tan(\omega t/2)=u$ with
$u=\omega/2\lambda$; its peak value $2u\,e^{-\arctan(u)/u}/\sqrt{1+u^2}$ equals 1 at $\omega/\lambda=3.8057$. This is also the threshold of
the best two-pair witness, with axes re-optimised at every time. A two-setting witness therefore needs about twice the margin that
entanglement itself needs.

\paragraph{Runs needed.} With two settings and $n$ runs each, $\langle xx\rangle=1$ has no shot noise on the pure state, and
$\langle yz\rangle=\sin(\Sigma/2)$ has variance $\cos^2(\Sigma/2)/n$. A result at $k$ standard deviations therefore needs
$n\ge k^2\cot^2(\Sigma/2)$ runs per setting \chk{witness:B4}. That is 31 runs (3$\sigma$) or 84 runs (5$\sigma$) at $\Sigma=1$, 10 or 26 at
$\pi/2$, $3.6\times10^5$ or $1.0\times10^6$ at $10^{-2}$, and $3.6\times10^9$ or $1.0\times10^{10}$ at $\Sigma=10^{-4}$, the phase of the
paper's more modest variant ($\Delta x\approx1\,\mu$m). Twenty thousand simulated experiments of $2\times84$ runs at $\Sigma=1$ give a mean
estimate of 1.481 (against 1.479) and a mean significance of $5.06\sigma$.

\subsection{What the experiment decides}\label{ss:bmv-decides}

Every two-sphere geometry has $\omega/\lambda\le1$ at the Bridge-B rate, and at most 5/6 at the exact rate. Entanglement needs more than 2,
and a two-setting witness more than 3.81. If decoherence at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate is real, a BMV experiment with two solid spheres
therefore finds no entanglement, whether gravity is quantum or classical \tagS. Bose et al.\ already remark that collapse mechanisms ``if
true, will typically lead to a strong loss of coherence on the time-scale of the experiment and inhibit the gravitationally mediated
entanglement''~\cite{bose}. Proposition~\ref{pr:bmv-threshold} and Corollary~\ref{co:bmv-any} make this exact for Di\'osi--Penrose rates.

Three consequences follow. First, a null BMV result does not separate GUID with Bridge~B from quantum gravity with Di\'osi--Penrose collapse.
Second, a positive result needs decoherence below $\omega/2$, the minimum any classical channel needs; it would refute GUID
(Lemma~\ref{lem:classical}), decoherence at the Di\'osi--Penrose rate (Bridge~B) and every classical mediator together. Third, under
Bridge~B the discriminating measurement is the decoherence itself. That is the coherence time of a mesoscopic superposition,
$\hbar R/(GM^2)$ up to the factor $6/5-R/\Delta x$, together with the force noise that the balance rule ties to it: the ``diffusion against
decoherence'' test of Section~\ref{sec:exp}.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.} The hypotheses, geometries and time grid were fixed before the first run. The random
geometries ($\Delta x$ from $10^{-2}R$ to $10^4R$) were sampled once, and the ranges were not tuned. The witness analysis was added after we
found the printed operator to be invalid, and it uses the paper's own state. No threshold or tolerance in this section was changed after a
run.

\paragraph{Caveats.} Di\'osi--Penrose rates in the literature differ by factors of order one. At half the exact rate the spheres' supremum
becomes 5/3, still below 2. At half the paper's $\lambda$ it becomes 2, approached but never attained, so the margin vanishes in the extreme
parallel geometry. With the full kernel and half the rate, extreme parallel geometries would reach $5/3>\sqrt2$. Only uniform rigid spheres
with two branches were scanned, and nuclear granularity is neglected (\chk{Q19} shows that the bulk term dominates). The debate about
classical gravity and quantum matter fields~\cite{azizhowl,movw,diosi25} is outside these models. The witness statements refer to the arXiv
text, since the PRL text was not read.

All numbers in this section come from \texttt{docs/simulation/gravity\_simulated.py} (110 of 110 checks, about three minutes on one core)
and \texttt{docs/simulation/bmv\_witness.py} (32 of 32 checks, under a minute); both are deterministic.

\paragraph{Verdict.} At the Bridge-B rate no BMV geometry of two spheres yields entanglement, whatever gravity is. The best case falls short
of the exact threshold $\omega/\lambda=2$ by a factor of 2, and at the exact rates the Newton kernel forbids entanglement for any two bodies
(\emph{derived}). GUID's prediction $\mathcal N=0$ therefore \emph{holds} but does not discriminate. Under Bridge~B the decisive measurement
is the decoherence rate of mesoscopic superpositions (\emph{live}). The spin witness as printed in arXiv:1707.06050 is not a valid witness.
Its correction, $W_c=1+\sin(\Sigma/2)$ with laboratory-gauge axes, works and needs $k^2\cot^2(\Sigma/2)$ runs per setting.
