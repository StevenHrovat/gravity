\section{Remnants with a history: accumulation, halos and cosmic time}\label{sec:remnant}

Section~\ref{sec:sparc} rejected the stochastic remnant of Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter} in the static form Oppenheim and Russo
derived~\cite{oppenheimrusso}: a constant extra acceleration $|\gamma_1|/2$ cannot make the excess $g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar}$ over the
baryons' Newtonian acceleration rise with $g_{\rm bar}$ with the observed logarithmic slope of 0.44 \chk{sparc:S2d}. GUID's cosmology
(Section~\ref{sec:cosmo}) is a long evolution, and Hypothesis~H7 keeps the remnants of interactions in the frame, so they may accumulate. We
test what history allows: an accumulated stochastic term, its dependence on each galaxy's past, the contained remnant (primordial black holes,
which form cold dark matter halos), the two together, the change of scale with cosmic time, and what cannot be fitted. The result: an
accumulated term of the self-consistent form fits the rotation curves as well as the radial acceleration relation (RAR), but it is MOND's
phenomenology, fails the solar system, carries no galaxy-specific history and has the wrong arrow in time, and once cold halos are present
the data leave it at most a few per cent of the dark acceleration. Data, cuts, error model and the likelihoods L1 (full error model) and L2
(velocity errors and a fitted floor) are those of Section~\ref{sec:sparc} ($N=2696$ points in 147 SPARC galaxies~\cite{lelli2016},
$\Upsilon_{\rm disk}=0.5$, $\Upsilon_{\rm bulge}=0.7$); uncertainties come from resampling galaxies, since the point-wise BIC,
$-2\ln L+k\ln N$, overstates the evidence of correlated points.

\subsection{The accumulated stochastic remnant}\label{sec:remnant-acc}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} \tagH\ The random kicks that the frame's diffusion leaves in the field (Section~\ref{sec:cq}) accumulate as a random
walk. If the variance of the extra acceleration grows with the local source and with time, $\langle\delta g^2\rangle=\kappa\,g_{\rm bar}\,t$,
and the entropic attraction is of the order of its root mean square, the extra acceleration is the geometric mean of $g_{\rm bar}$ and an
accumulated scale $a_{\rm acc}\equiv\kappa t$. The classical-quantum dynamics does not give this. We fixed two forms before any fit:
\begin{align}
\text{form B (Brownian sum):}\quad & g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}+\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}\,a_{\rm acc}}\,,\label{eq:formB}\\
\text{form S (self-consistent):}\quad & g_{\rm obs}=\tfrac12 g_{\rm bar}+\big(\tfrac14 g_{\rm bar}^2+g_{\rm bar}\,a_{\rm acc}\big)^{1/2}\,,
\label{eq:formS}
\end{align}
where form S solves $(g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar})\,g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm acc}$: the excess is sourced by the total field it helps to make.
Both tend to $\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm acc}}$ at low acceleration, so $a_{\rm acc}$ plays the role of Milgrom's $a_0$~\cite{milgrom}. Form S
\emph{is} MOND's ``simple'' interpolating function, $\nu(y)=\tfrac12+(\tfrac14+1/y)^{1/2}$ in $g_{\rm obs}=\nu(g_{\rm bar}/a_0)g_{\rm bar}$;
form B adds the Newtonian and the deep-MOND acceleration. The square-root form was proposed after the slope 0.44 was measured, so agreeing
with it is not a prediction. The reference is the RAR, $g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}/[1-\exp(-\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}/g_\dagger})]$~\cite{mcgaugh2016}.

\paragraph{The solar system.} Both forms are pointwise laws in $g_{\rm bar}$, and the Sun's field is the cleanest $g_{\rm bar}$ there is.
A small extra radial force $f=k\,r^n$ with $\epsilon=fr^2/GM$ changes the apsidal angle $\psi=\pi[3+\dd\ln F/\dd\ln r]^{-1/2}$ of near-circular
orbits through $\dd\ln F/\dd\ln r=-2+(n+2)\epsilon$, so the perihelion moves by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precession}
\Delta\varpi=2\psi-2\pi=-\pi\,(n+2)\,\epsilon\quad\text{per orbit}
\end{equation}
(checked by integrating orbits \chk{remnant:R1a}). Form B adds $\sqrt{GMa_{\rm acc}}/r$ ($n=-1$), form S tends to the constant $a_{\rm acc}$
($n=0$). With the fitted scales, form B turns the perihelion of Mars by $-6.0\times10^4$\,mas/yr and form S that of Saturn by $-74$\,mas/yr,
where the EPM2011 ephemerides allow $-0.00020\pm0.00037$ and $-0.0032\pm0.0047$\,mas/yr~\cite{pitjeva2013}. Over six planets form B exceeds
the allowed range by factors of $10^5$ to $1.6\times10^8$ and form S by 190 to $8\times10^4$: as written, both are \vd{refuted}. A survivor
would need a rule that switches the term off at high acceleration, as the RAR's exponential does; accumulation supplies none.

\subsection{Natural scales and the look-elsewhere effect}\label{sec:remnant-scales}

We fixed nine candidates for $a_{\rm acc}$ before fitting: $cH_0$, $cH_0/2\pi$, $cH_\infty$, $cH_\infty/2\pi$ ($H_\infty=\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}H_0$,
$\Omega_\Lambda=0.685$) for $H_0=67.4$~\cite{planck18} and 73\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, and $c/t_0$ with $t_0=13.8$\,Gyr. SPARC's 97
Hubble-flow distances assume $H_0=73$~\cite{lelli2016}; rescaled to 67.4 they give $a_{\rm acc}=1.03\times10^{-10}$ (form S), and each 67.4
candidate is compared with these data. The error on $\log a_{\rm acc}$ is 0.110 dex for form S (galaxy bootstrap 0.034, a $\pm0.1$\,dex shift
of $\Upsilon$ 0.105, likelihood choice 0.008) and 0.131 dex for form B \chk{remnant:R1b}.

\subsection{Per-galaxy scales and history}\label{sec:remnant-history}

We fitted one scale to each of the 132 galaxies with at least five points, with velocity errors per point and distance, inclination,
$\Upsilon$ ($\pm25\%$) and H\,\textsc{i} mass ($\pm10\%$) as whole-galaxy shifts added in quadrature into an error $s_i$, and estimated the
intrinsic scatter $\tau$ of $\ell_i=\log a_i$ by maximising $-\sum_i[(\ell_i-\mu)^2/(s_i^2+\tau^2)+\ln(s_i^2+\tau^2)]$.

\paragraph{Universality.} Form S gives $\tau=0.20$\,dex, as much as the RAR's per-galaxy $g_\dagger$; form B gives 0.23 \chk{remnant:R1c}.
Mock universes with a universal RAR, passed through the SPARC error model and the same analysis, give $\tau=0.04$ (16--84\%: 0.00 to 0.10)
\chk{remnant:R2}. The galaxies scatter more than this error model allows, but equally for $g_\dagger$ and $a_{\rm acc}$: either the model misses
a galaxy-level term (an $\Upsilon$ varying by more than 0.1 dex could cause it) or neither scale is universal. Li et al.~\cite{li2018}, who
marginalise each galaxy over $\Upsilon$, distance and inclination, find no credible variation; Rodrigues et al.~\cite{rodrigues2018} conclude
the opposite. Form S and the RAR stand or fall together here.

\paragraph{History.} A universal $a_{\rm acc}$ carries no history beyond the global time; a galaxy-specific accumulation would give older,
more evolved systems a larger scale. Before computing anything we fixed five proxies and the predicted signs: $\log a_i$ should fall with
Hubble type, gas fraction and disk scale length, and rise with effective surface brightness and with $N_{\rm orb}=t_0V_{\rm out}/(2\pi R_{\rm out})$,
the orbits completed at the outermost radius in a Hubble time (median 17); a fixed variance per orbit gives $a_i\propto N_{\rm orb}$. For form B's
$a_i$, the mean residual from the universal form B and the RAR's $g_{\dagger,i}$ we computed 15 Spearman and 15 partial (fixed
$\log M_{\rm bar}$) correlations with bootstrap intervals and the Holm correction over all 30. Because a universal law fitted per galaxy with a
slightly wrong shape already correlates with proxies that track a galaxy's place on the $g_{\rm bar}$ axis, we also built 500 mock universes
per null model (distances, inclinations, $\Upsilon$ and gas drawn from their errors, plus 0.044 dex of point scatter calibrated to the data's
median reduced $\chi^2$ of 3.01), and tested each observed $\rho$ against the mock mean with the data's own bootstrap spread.

None of the 30 correlations survives the Holm correction (smallest adjusted $p=0.33$), and none departs from the universal-RAR baseline
(smallest adjusted $p=0.35$) \chk{remnant:R2}. Six partial correlations fall outside the mocks' 95\% bands, which are too narrow because the
mocks lack the extra scatter found above. The predicted signs hold for two of five proxies, and the slope of $\log a_i$ on $\log N_{\rm orb}$ is
$+0.12$ (95\%: $-0.17$ to $+0.46$) where a per-orbit random walk predicts $+1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:remnant-history}). There is no history dependence
beyond the global time, and per-orbit accumulation is \vd{refuted}.

\subsection{The contained remnant: halos}\label{sec:remnant-halos}

The second route of Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter} is energy contained behind horizons at the start: primordial black holes, cold and
collisionless, which form halos as cold dark matter does. We fitted each of the 132 galaxies with $g_{\rm model}=g_{\rm bar}+GM_{\rm h}(<R)/R^2$
under L1, maximising likelihood times prior. (i) NFW, $M_{\rm h}(<r)=M_{200}\,\mu(cr/r_{200})/\mu(c)$ with $\mu(u)=\ln(1+u)-u/(1+u)$ and
$M_{200}=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}200\rho_{\rm crit}r_{200}^3$, with the concentration prior $\log c_{200}=0.905-0.101\log[M_{200}/(10^{12}h^{-1}M_\odot)]$,
scatter 0.11 dex~\cite{dutton2014}. (ii) Cosmological NFW: in addition the stellar-to-halo relation
$m_\star/M_{200}=2N[(M_{200}/M_1)^{-\beta}+(M_{200}/M_1)^{\gamma}]^{-1}$ ($\log M_1=11.590$, $N=0.0351$, $\beta=1.376$, $\gamma=0.608$, 0.15
dex)~\cite{moster2013} with $m_\star=0.5L_{[3.6]}$: the halos a universe whose dark matter is all primordial black holes must have. (iii)
Burkert, cored, $\rho=\rho_0r_0^3/[(r+r_0)(r^2+r_0^2)]$, no prior. Per-galaxy BIC counts $k=2$ for a halo, 1 for a free scale, 0 for a
universal law.

Summed over galaxies and relative to the universal RAR, $\Delta$BIC is $-1360$ for NFW (264 parameters), $-1108$ for cosmological NFW and
$-1780$ for Burkert, while the universal form S has $-4.6$ and form B $+218$ \chk{remnant:R3} \tagS. Every per-galaxy model thus beats every
universal law, largely because L1 treats a galaxy's common distance, inclination and $\Upsilon$ errors as independent per point, which any
per-galaxy parameter absorbs: one free RAR scale per galaxy (132 parameters) already reaches $-1883$. Against that yardstick NFW halos are worse
by $+523$, Burkert halos by $+103$ and cosmological NFW halos by $+775$, and cored halos beat cuspy ones by $-420$, the known cusp-core issue.
Per galaxy, NFW beats the universal RAR in 88 of 132, Burkert in 98, cosmological NFW in 82. The NFW halos have $\log M_{200}=11.47$
(16--84\%: 10.76 to 12.18), about 24 times the baryonic mass. Halos accommodate the RAR's one-parameter regularity with two parameters per
galaxy; they do not predict it.

\subsection{Halos and a universal stochastic term together}\label{sec:remnant-joint}

The cohesive whole would be baryons, a halo per galaxy and one universal term, $S=A$ or $S=\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}A}$. At 66 amplitudes from
$10^{-16}$ to $10^{-9.5}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ we refitted every halo and summed the profile
$T(A)=\sum_i\min_{\theta_i}[-2\ln L_i(\theta_i,A)-2\ln\pi(\theta_i)]$ ($\pi$: halo prior). The one-sided 95\% bound is where $T$ rises by 2.71;
adding the term costs $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=T(\hat A)-T(0)+\ln N$.

With NFW halos the best square-root amplitude is zero (in 87 to 97\% of galaxy resamples) and the profile rises roughly as $\sqrt A$: the

mass exist and a stochastic term must not count their dark acceleration twice. The room left with cosmological halos is a median 2.1\% of the

\subsection{Cosmic time}\label{sec:remnant-time}

We compare four laws fixed in advance: $a_{\rm acc}$ constant, $\propto H(z)=H_0[\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_\Lambda]^{1/2}$, $\propto1/t$ and
$\propto t$ (random-walk accumulation), with $\Omega_m=0.315$~\cite{planck18} and the flat-$\Lambda$CDM age
$t(z)=\tfrac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}\mathrm{arsinh}[\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m}(1+z)^{-3/2}]$. They give $a(1)/a(0)=1$, 1.79, 2.36 and
0.42, and $a(2)/a(0)=1$, 3.03, 4.21 and 0.24 \chk{remnant:R5}. In the deep regime $V^4=GMa_{\rm acc}$, so at fixed rotation speed
$\Delta\log M_{\rm bar}=-\log[a(z)/a(0)]$: $-0.23$, $-0.34$ and $+0.34$ dex at $z=0.9$ for the $H$, $1/t$ and $t$ laws, and $-0.54$, $-0.69$ and
$+0.69$ at $z=2.3$. Massive disks at high redshift sit at 3 to 11 $a_0$ at their half-light radii~\cite{milgrom2017}, where any change is
diluted: for a RAR-shaped law at fixed $V$ and $R$ with $g_{\rm obs}=3g_\dagger$ the shifts are $-0.07$, $-0.12$, $+0.07$ ($z=0.9$) and
$-0.22$, $-0.31$, $+0.10$ ($z=2.3$).

\paragraph{Measurements.} \"Ubler et al.~\cite{ubler2017} find baryonic Tully--Fisher zero-point offsets of $-0.44\pm0.04$\,dex at
$z\approx0.9$ (65 disks) and $-0.27\pm0.05$ at $z\approx2.3$ (46), with statistical errors only and the local slope 3.75; without their
pressure-support correction, $-0.33$ and $-0.09$. Their baryonic masses carry about 0.15 dex each, with gas from scaling relations. Read as a
change of scale, they imply $a(z)/a(0)=2.8$ (deep) to 7.6 ($3g_\dagger$) at $z\approx0.9$ and 1.9 to 4.2 at $z\approx2.3$ \chk{remnant:R5}.
MUSE-DARK III~\cite{ciocan2026} fits the RAR of 79 galaxies at $0.33<z<1.44$ and finds $a_0(z\approx1)=2.38^{+0.12}_{-0.10}\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$,
$1.98\pm0.44$ times the local 1.2, with $g_{\rm obs}$ and $g_{\rm bar}$ from one model. Six rotation curves at $z\approx0.9$--2.4 ``all but
exclude'' about $4a_0$ at $z\approx2$~\cite{milgrom2017}. The dark-matter fractions of 100 massive disks~\cite{nestorshachar2023} would need each
galaxy's $g_{\rm bar}$ and are not used.

\paragraph{Comparison.} Accumulation predicts a smaller scale in the past, hence more baryons at fixed speed; every measurement points the
other way (Fig.~\ref{fig:remnant-redshift}). MUSE-DARK's $2.0\pm0.4$ at $z=1$ lies between the $H(z)$ and $1/t$ laws; at $z=2$ the $1/t$ law
reaches the excluded $4a_0$. The \"Ubler offsets have the sign of a larger past scale, but in the deep reading the one at $z\approx0.9$ is
larger and the one at $z\approx2.3$ smaller than either falling law predicts, and no monotonic law fits both. With systematics (gas masses,
pressure support, model-dependent $g_{\rm bar}$) as large as the effect, and with $\Lambda$CDM galaxy evolution producing such offsets too, this
is not a clean test; read literally, the data disfavour accumulation. The time arrows also disagree in principle: an accumulated scale grows,
while $cH$ and $c/t$ fall, so an $a_{\rm acc}$ that accumulates and equals $cH_0/2\pi$ today does so by a coincidence of the present epoch.

\subsection{What cannot be fitted}\label{sec:remnant-cannot}

The stochastic remnant exists only as the Newtonian limit of a static, spherical calculation~\cite{oppenheimrusso}. The acoustic peaks need
$\Omega_ch^2=0.120$ of pressureless matter at $z\approx1100$~\cite{planck18}, hence a cosmological perturbation theory of the remnant, and
lensing needs the metric; neither exists (\vd{untested}). MOND-like laws reduce the mass discrepancy of clusters three- to four-fold without
removing it~\cite{sanders2003}, and forms B and S inherit that residual (no cluster test was run). In 1E0657$-$558 the lensing mass follows the
galaxies, offset from the X-ray gas at $8\sigma$~\cite{clowe}; a term set by the local baryonic field follows the gas. The contained remnant is
cold dark matter and covers all four as $\Lambda$CDM does (\vd{holds as a reading}).

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.} (1) The amplitude grid of Section~\ref{sec:remnant-joint} first started at
$10^{-13}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$; the first run put the square-root bound below it, and that amplitude is not negligible ($\sqrt{10^{-12}\cdot10^{-13}}$
is 30\% of the lowest $g_{\rm bar}$), so the grid now starts at $10^{-16}$ and the consistency check uses $10^{-30}$. (2) Two checks failed on the
first run because the checks were wrong (a logarithm base, an angle unwrapping); they were fixed and no tolerance was loosened. (3) The mocks
were raised from 200 to 500 per null model after a test run. (4) The mocks' extra point scatter is calibrated on the data. (5) One free RAR
scale per galaxy was chosen as the halos' yardstick after seeing that every per-galaxy model beats every universal one. (6) Reading the
Burkert constant as a halo-shape correction is an interpretation made after seeing it. (7) The shift test and the mock calibration of $\tau$
were added after test runs with 4 to 20 mocks whose bands looked narrower than the bootstrap intervals; all are reported.

\paragraph{Caveats.} $\Upsilon$ is fixed at 0.5 and 0.7, with $m_\star=0.5L_{[3.6]}$ (bulge light at the disk value) in the stellar-to-halo
prior; a free $\Upsilon$ would improve halo fits and universal laws alike. The stellar-to-halo relation was derived for a WMAP7 cosmology, and we
did not check its constraint at SPARC's lowest stellar masses. The halos are not adiabatically contracted. The high-redshift comparison uses
published summary numbers for massive disks selected differently from SPARC.

All numbers in this section come from \texttt{docs/simulation/remnant\_history.py} (39 of 39 checks, about two and a half minutes on one
core).

\paragraph{Verdict.} An accumulated stochastic remnant of the self-consistent form fits the rotation curves as well as the radial acceleration
relation, but it is MOND's simple interpolating function: its scale matches $cH_0/2\pi$ only as Milgrom's $a_0$ always has, it is
\vd{refuted} in the solar system as a pointwise law, it carries no galaxy-specific history, and the high-redshift data favour a scale that was
larger in the past rather than one that accumulates. The contained remnant, primordial black holes as cold dark matter, covers the microwave
background, lensing and the merging cluster and fits the rotation curves with two parameters per galaxy (\vd{live}); once its halos are
present, the stochastic term is bounded to a few per cent of the dark acceleration. The two remnants compete rather than combine, and the
consistent reading of Hypothesis~H7 is primordial black holes as the cold dark matter.
