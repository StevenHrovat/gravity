\section{Dark matter II: accumulation, halos, cosmic time and primordial black holes}\label{sec:remnant}

This section tests the two routes of Hypothesis~\ref{h:remnant} (Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}) once their history is included, and scores
primordial black holes, the contained remnant's candidate, against published constraints.

\subsection{The accumulated stochastic remnant}\label{sec:remnant-acc}

Data, cuts, error model, the likelihoods L1 and L2 and the BIC are those of Section~\ref{sec:sparc-data}. Primers~\ref{box:galaxy}
and~\ref{box:stats} explain the quantities and the statistics. Our galaxy-bootstrap resamples use a different random seed, so bootstrap
ranges can differ from that section's in the last digit.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} \tagH\ The random kicks that the frame's diffusion leaves in the field (Section~\ref{sec:cq}) accumulate as a random
walk. Suppose the variance of the extra acceleration grows with the local source and with time at a constant rate $\dot a$,
$\langle\delta g^2\rangle=g_{\rm bar}\,\dot a\,t$. If the entropic attraction is of the order of its root mean square, the extra acceleration
is the geometric mean of $g_{\rm bar}$ and an accumulated scale $a_{\rm acc}\equiv\dot a\,t$. The classical-quantum dynamics does not give this
law; it is an assumption. We fixed two forms before any fit:
\begin{align}
\text{form B (Brownian sum):}\quad & g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}+\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}\,a_{\rm acc}}\,,\label{eq:formB}\\
\text{form S (self-consistent):}\quad & g_{\rm obs}=\tfrac12 g_{\rm bar}+\big(\tfrac14 g_{\rm bar}^2+g_{\rm bar}\,a_{\rm acc}\big)^{1/2}\,.
\label{eq:formS}
\end{align}
Form S solves $(g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar})\,g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm acc}$: the excess is sourced by the total field it helps to make. Both
forms tend to $\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm acc}}$ at low acceleration, so $a_{\rm acc}$ plays the role of Milgrom's $a_0$~\cite{milgrom}. Form S
\emph{is} MOND's ``simple'' interpolating function, $\nu(y)=\tfrac12+(\tfrac14+1/y)^{1/2}$ in $g_{\rm obs}=\nu(g_{\rm bar}/a_0)\,g_{\rm bar}$.
Form B adds the Newtonian and the deep-MOND acceleration. Both forms were introduced after Section~\ref{sec:sparc-slope} had measured the
residual slope of 0.44. Agreeing with that slope is therefore not a prediction, and refuting them refutes these two forms, not every form a
GUID prediction could take. The reference is the RAR of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparc-rar}~\cite{mcgaugh2016}, with one universal $g_\dagger$.

\paragraph{The solar system.} Both forms are pointwise laws in $g_{\rm bar}$, and the Sun's field is the cleanest $g_{\rm bar}$ there is. Add a
small radial force $f=k\,r^n$ to $F=GM/r^2$ and write $\epsilon=fr^2/GM$. Near-circular orbits have the apsidal angle
$\psi=\pi[3+\dd\ln F/\dd\ln r]^{-1/2}$, and $\dd\ln F/\dd\ln r=-2+(n+2)\epsilon$. The perihelion therefore moves by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precession}
\Delta\varpi=2\psi-2\pi=-\pi\,(n+2)\,\epsilon\quad\text{per orbit},
\end{equation}
which we checked by integrating orbits \chk{remnant:R1a}. Form B adds $\sqrt{GMa_{\rm acc}}/r$ ($n=-1$); form S tends to the constant
$a_{\rm acc}$ ($n=0$). With the fitted scales, form B turns the perihelion of Mars by $-6.0\times10^4$\,mas/yr and form S that of Saturn by
$-74$\,mas/yr. The EPM2011 ephemerides allow $-0.00020\pm0.00037$ and $-0.0032\pm0.0047$\,mas/yr~\cite{pitjeva2013}. Over six planets,
form B exceeds the allowed range by factors of $10^5$ to $1.6\times10^8$ and form S by 190 to $8\times10^4$. As written, both forms are
\vd{refuted}. A survivor would need a rule that switches the term off at high acceleration, as the RAR's exponential does; accumulation
supplies none.

\subsection{Natural scales and the look-elsewhere effect}\label{sec:remnant-scales}

We fixed nine candidates for $a_{\rm acc}$ before fitting: $cH_0$, $cH_0/2\pi$, $cH_\infty$ and $cH_\infty/2\pi$, with
$H_\infty=\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}H_0$ and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.685$, each for $H_0=67.4$~\cite{planck18} and 73\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$; and $c/t_0$
with $t_0=13.8$\,Gyr. SPARC's 97 Hubble-flow distances assume $H_0=73$~\cite{lelli2016}. Rescaled to 67.4 they give
$a_{\rm acc}=1.03\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ for form S, and each 67.4 candidate is compared with these rescaled data. The error on
$\log a_{\rm acc}$ is 0.110 dex for form S: galaxy bootstrap 0.034, a $\pm0.1$\,dex shift of $\Upsilon$ 0.105, likelihood choice 0.008. For
form B it is 0.131 dex \chk{remnant:R1b}.

\subsection{Per-galaxy scales and history}\label{sec:remnant-history}

We fitted one scale to each of the 132 galaxies with at least five points. Each fit uses velocity errors per point. Distance, inclination,
$\Upsilon$ ($\pm25\%$) and H\,\textsc{i} mass ($\pm10\%$) enter as whole-galaxy shifts, added in quadrature into an error $s_i$. The intrinsic
scatter $\tau$ of $\ell_i=\log a_i$ maximises $-\sum_i[(\ell_i-\mu)^2/(s_i^2+\tau^2)+\ln(s_i^2+\tau^2)]$.

\paragraph{Universality.} Form S gives $\tau=0.20$\,dex, as much as the RAR's per-galaxy $g_\dagger$; form B gives 0.23 \chk{remnant:R1c}.
Mock universes with a universal RAR, passed through the SPARC error model and the same analysis, give $\tau=0.04$ (16--84\%: 0.00 to 0.10)
\chk{remnant:R2}. The galaxies thus scatter more than this error model allows, equally for $g_\dagger$ and $a_{\rm acc}$. Either a
galaxy-level term is missing, such as an $\Upsilon$ that varies by more than 0.1 dex, or neither scale is universal. Marginalising each
galaxy over $\Upsilon$, distance and inclination, Li et al.\ find no credible variation~\cite{li2018}; Rodrigues et al.\ conclude the
opposite~\cite{rodrigues2018}. Here form S and the RAR stand or fall together.

\paragraph{History.} A universal $a_{\rm acc}$ carries no history beyond the global time. A galaxy-specific accumulation would give older,
more evolved systems a larger scale. Before computing anything we fixed five proxies and the predicted signs. $\log a_i$ should fall with
Hubble type, gas fraction and disk scale length. It should rise with effective surface brightness and with
$N_{\rm orb}=t_0V_{\rm out}/(2\pi R_{\rm out})$, the orbits completed at the outermost radius in a Hubble time (median 17). A fixed variance per
orbit gives $a_i\propto N_{\rm orb}$. We correlated three per-galaxy quantities with the proxies: form B's $a_i$, the mean residual from the
universal form B, and the RAR's $g_{\dagger,i}$. That gives 15 Spearman and 15 partial correlations (at fixed $\log M_{\rm bar}$), with
bootstrap intervals and the Holm correction over all 30. A universal law fitted per galaxy with a slightly wrong shape already correlates
with proxies that track a galaxy's place on the $g_{\rm bar}$ axis. We therefore also tested each $\rho$ against its mean in 500 mock
universes, using the data's own bootstrap spread. The mocks draw distances, inclinations, $\Upsilon$ and gas from their errors and add
0.044 dex of point scatter, calibrated on the data.

None of the 30 correlations survives the Holm correction (smallest adjusted $p=0.33$). None departs from the universal-RAR baseline
(smallest adjusted $p=0.35$) \chk{remnant:R2}. Six partial correlations fall outside the mocks' 95\% bands, but those bands are too narrow,
because the mocks lack the extra scatter found above. The predicted signs hold for two of five proxies. The slope of $\log a_i$ on
$\log N_{\rm orb}$ is $+0.12$ (95\%: $-0.17$ to $+0.46$), where a per-orbit random walk predicts $+1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:remnant-history}). There
is no history dependence beyond the global time, and per-orbit accumulation is \vd{refuted}.

\subsection{The contained remnant: halos}\label{sec:remnant-halos}

Primordial black holes are cold and collisionless, so they would form halos as any cold dark matter does. We fitted each of the 132
galaxies with $g_{\rm model}=g_{\rm bar}+GM_{\rm h}(<R)/R^2$ under L1, maximising likelihood times prior. Three halo models enter.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item NFW, $M_{\rm h}(<r)=M_{200}\,\mu(cr/r_{200})/\mu(c)$ with $\mu(u)=\ln(1+u)-u/(1+u)$ and $M_{200}=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}200\rho_{\rm crit}r_{200}^3$.
The concentration prior is $\log c_{200}=0.905-0.101\log[M_{200}/(10^{12}h^{-1}M_\odot)]$, with 0.11 dex scatter~\cite{dutton2014}.
\item Cosmological NFW: in addition, the stellar-to-halo relation of Moster et al.~\cite{moster2013},
\[
m_\star/M_{200}=2N\big[(M_{200}/M_1)^{-\beta}+(M_{200}/M_1)^{\gamma}\big]^{-1},
\]
with $\log M_1=11.590$, $N=0.0351$, $\beta=1.376$, $\gamma=0.608$, 0.15 dex scatter and $m_\star=0.5L_{[3.6]}$. These are the halos that any
cold dark matter must form.
\item Burkert, a cored profile, $\rho=\rho_0r_0^3/[(r+r_0)(r^2+r_0^2)]$, without prior.
\end{enumerate}
The per-galaxy BIC counts $k=2$ for a halo, 1 for a free scale and 0 for a universal law.

Summed over galaxies and relative to the universal RAR, $\Delta$BIC is $-1360$ for NFW (264 parameters), $-1108$ for cosmological NFW and
$-1780$ for Burkert \chk{remnant:R3} \tagS. The universal forms S and B have $-4.6$ and $+218$. Every per-galaxy model beats every universal law. This is
largely because L1 treats a galaxy's common distance, inclination and $\Upsilon$ errors as independent per point, and any per-galaxy
parameter absorbs them: one free RAR scale per galaxy already reaches $-1883$. Against that yardstick NFW halos are worse by $+523$,
Burkert halos by $+103$ and cosmological NFW halos by $+775$. Cored halos beat cuspy ones by $-420$, the known cusp-core issue. Per galaxy,
NFW beats the universal RAR in 88 of 132 galaxies and Burkert in 98. The NFW halos have $\log M_{200}=11.47$ (16--84\%: 10.76 to 12.18),
about 24 times the baryonic mass. Halos accommodate the RAR's one-parameter regularity with two parameters per galaxy; they do not predict
it. These fits test generic cold dark matter halos: nothing here distinguishes primordial black holes from particle dark matter.

\subsection{The contained remnant: primordial black holes}\label{sec:remnant-pbh}

This paper does not test primordial black holes. Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter} takes them as GUID's contained remnant, holes formed in the
start when the inflaton fragments into oscillons. That formation channel is the result of Cotner, Kusenko and Takhistov~\cite{cotner}, not
ours. The mass window is adopted, not predicted. Gorton and Green find that primordial black holes can be all of the dark matter between
about $10^{17}$ and $10^{22}$\,g ($10^{14}$ to $10^{19}$\,kg) for a single mass. For extended mass functions the window is
narrower~\cite{gortongreen}. Products of Hawking evaporation close the window below (INTEGRAL/SPI MeV gamma rays, Voyager~1 electrons and
positrons). Microlensing of M31 by Subaru-HSC closes it above. Three rows of Section~\ref{sec:data} record the comparison, in the classes of
Section~\ref{sec:scoring} \chk{data:D7.1-D7.3}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item D7.1, the window: GUID's window coincides with the open one because it was taken from it (CONS).
\item D7.2, the evaporation edge: holes lighter than $1.7\times10^{11}$\,kg have evaporated within 13.8\,Gyr (a Stefan--Boltzmann estimate).
That is a factor 5.8 below the ``about $10^{15}$\,g'' of Carr et al.~\cite{carr2021}, a value without a stated uncertainty (INFO).
\item D7.3, the edge temperatures: a hole of $10^{14}$\,kg glows at $1.23\times10^9$\,K ($k_BT=0.106$\,MeV), one of $10^{19}$\,kg at
$1.23\times10^4$\,K. The glow at the light edge is what bounds the window (INFO).
\end{itemize}
\emph{Verdict:} \vd{untested} in this paper. As a candidate it is \vd{live}: evaporation and microlensing searches can close the
window~\cite{gortongreen}.

\subsection{Halos and a universal stochastic term together}\label{sec:remnant-joint}

The cohesive whole would be baryons, a halo per galaxy and one universal term, $\Delta g_{\rm u}=A$ or $\Delta g_{\rm u}=\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}A}$.
At 66 amplitudes from $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-9.5}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ we refitted every halo and summed the profile
$\mathcal{P}(A)=\sum_i\min_{\theta_i}[-2\ln L_i(\theta_i,A)-2\ln p(\theta_i)]$, with $p$ the halo prior. The one-sided 95\% bound is where
$\mathcal{P}$ rises by 2.71. Adding the term costs $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=\mathcal{P}(\hat A)-\mathcal{P}(0)+\ln N$, with $N=2652$ points.

With NFW halos the best square-root amplitude is zero, in 87 to 97\% of galaxy resamples (Table~\ref{tab:remnant-joint},
Fig.~\ref{fig:remnant-halos}) \tagS. The profile rises roughly as $\sqrt A$: the refitted halos cannot absorb the term, so it costs at first
order. With cosmological halos its bound, $3.0\times10^{-14}$ at the bootstrap 84th percentile, is about 2600 times below what form B needs
alone. With NFW halos a constant is bounded at 4 to 5\% of the value that replaces the halos. Burkert halos prefer one at about a fifth of
that value ($\Delta$BIC $-50$); since NFW halos do not want it, we read it as a correction to the cored halo's outer shape.

\emph{Consistency requirement.} The acoustic peaks fix $\Omega_ch^2=0.120\pm0.001$ of collisionless matter at $z\approx1100$~\cite{planck18}.
If that is the contained remnant, halos of cosmological mass exist, and a stochastic term must not count their dark acceleration twice. The
room left is the share in Table~\ref{tab:remnant-joint}, measured at each galaxy's outermost radius. For the square-root term it is 1 to 5\%
of the dark acceleration, 2.1\% with cosmological halos. For a constant it is 8 to 31\%: 8.5\% with cosmological halos and 31\% with the
Burkert halos that prefer it \chk{remnant:R4}.

\subsection{Cosmic time}\label{sec:remnant-time}

We compare four laws, fixed in advance: $a_{\rm acc}$ constant, $\propto H(z)=H_0[\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_\Lambda]^{1/2}$, $\propto1/t$, and
$\propto t$ (random-walk accumulation). We use $\Omega_m=0.315$~\cite{planck18} and the flat-$\Lambda$CDM age
$t(z)=\tfrac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}\mathrm{arsinh}[\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m}(1+z)^{-3/2}]$. The laws give $a(1)/a(0)=1$, 1.79, 2.36
and 0.42, and $a(2)/a(0)=1$, 3.03, 4.21 and 0.24 \chk{remnant:R5}. In the deep regime $V^4=GMa_{\rm acc}$, so at fixed rotation speed
$\Delta\log M_{\rm bar}=-\log[a(z)/a(0)]$. For accumulation this is $+0.34$ dex at $z=0.9$ and $+0.69$ at $z=2.3$. Massive disks at high redshift
sit at 3 to 11 $a_0$ at their half-light radii~\cite{milgrom2017}, where any change is diluted. For a RAR-shaped law at fixed $V$ and $R$ with
$g_{\rm obs}=3g_\dagger$ the shifts shrink by factors of 2 to 7 (dashed in Fig.~\ref{fig:remnant-redshift}).

\paragraph{Measurements.} \"Ubler et al.~\cite{ubler2017} fit KMOS$^{\rm 3D}$ disks with the local slope 3.75. They find baryonic
Tully--Fisher zero-point offsets of $-0.44\pm0.04$\,dex at $z\approx0.9$ (65 disks) and $-0.27\pm0.05$ at $z\approx2.3$ (46), with
statistical errors only. Without their
pressure-support correction the offsets are $-0.33$ and $-0.09$. Their baryonic masses carry about 0.15 dex each, with gas from scaling
relations. Read as a change of scale, the offsets imply $a(z)/a(0)=2.8$ (deep) to 7.6 ($3g_\dagger$) at $z\approx0.9$, and 1.9 to 4.2 at
$z\approx2.3$ \chk{remnant:R5}. MUSE-DARK III~\cite{ciocan2026} fits the RAR of 79 galaxies at $0.33<z<1.44$. It finds
$a_0(z\approx1)=2.38^{+0.12}_{-0.10}\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, $1.98\pm0.44$ times the local 1.2, with $g_{\rm obs}$ and $g_{\rm bar}$ taken
from one model. Six rotation curves at $z\approx0.9$--2.4 ``all but exclude'' about $4a_0$ at $z\approx2$~\cite{milgrom2017}. Dark-matter
fractions of high-redshift disks~\cite{nestorshachar2023} would need each galaxy's $g_{\rm bar}$ and are not used.

\paragraph{Comparison.} Accumulation predicts a smaller scale in the past, hence more baryons at fixed speed. Every measurement points the
other way (Fig.~\ref{fig:remnant-redshift}). MUSE-DARK's $2.0\pm0.4$ lies between the $H(z)$ and $1/t$ laws, and at $z=2$ the $1/t$ law
reaches the excluded $4a_0$. The \"Ubler offsets have the sign of a larger past scale, but they fall between $z\approx0.9$ and 2.3, which no
monotonic law does. The systematics (gas masses, pressure support, model-dependent $g_{\rm bar}$) are as large as the effect, and
$\Lambda$CDM galaxy evolution makes such offsets too. This is therefore not a clean test; read literally, the data disfavour accumulation.
The time arrows also disagree in principle. An accumulated scale grows, while $cH$ and $c/t$ fall, so an accumulating $a_{\rm acc}$ equals
$cH_0/2\pi$ only by a coincidence of the present epoch.

\subsection{What cannot be fitted}\label{sec:remnant-cannot}

The stochastic remnant exists only as the Newtonian limit of a static, spherical calculation~\cite{oppenheimrusso}. The acoustic peaks
($\Omega_ch^2=0.120$ of pressureless matter at $z\approx1100$~\cite{planck18}) need its cosmological perturbation theory, and lensing needs
its metric. Neither exists (\vd{untested}). MOND-like laws reduce the mass discrepancy of clusters three- to four-fold without removing
it~\cite{sanders2003}; forms B and S inherit that residual, and no cluster test was run. In 1E0657$-$558 the lensing mass follows the
galaxies, offset from the X-ray gas at $8\sigma$~\cite{clowe}, whereas a term set by the local baryonic field follows the gas. Cold dark
matter of any kind, primordial black holes included, covers all four as $\Lambda$CDM does (\vd{live}).

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The amplitude grid of Section~\ref{sec:remnant-joint} first started at $10^{-13}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$. The first run put the square-root bound
below it, and that amplitude is not negligible: $\sqrt{10^{-12}\cdot10^{-13}}$ is 30\% of the lowest $g_{\rm bar}$. The grid now starts at
$10^{-16}$, and the consistency check uses $10^{-30}$.
\item The mocks were raised from 200 to 500 per null model after a test run.
\item The mocks' extra point scatter is calibrated on the data.
\item One free RAR scale per galaxy was chosen as the halos' yardstick after seeing that every per-galaxy model beats every universal one.
\item Reading the Burkert constant as a halo-shape correction is an interpretation made after seeing it.
\item The shift test and the mock calibration of $\tau$ were added after test runs with 4 to 20 mocks, whose bands looked narrower than the
bootstrap intervals. All are reported.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\Upsilon$ is fixed at 0.5 and 0.7, and $m_\star=0.5L_{[3.6]}$ in the stellar-to-halo prior. A free $\Upsilon$ would improve halo fits and
universal laws alike.
\item The stellar-to-halo relation was derived for a WMAP7 cosmology and is not checked at SPARC's lowest masses. The halos are not
adiabatically contracted.
\item The high-redshift numbers are published summaries for disks selected differently from SPARC.
\end{itemize}

All numbers in this section come from \texttt{docs/simulation/remnant\_history.py} (39 of 39 checks, about two and a half minutes on one
core), except the D7 rows, which come from Section~\ref{sec:data}.

\paragraph{Verdict.} The stochastic remnant does not supply the dark acceleration of galaxies. Its static form is \vd{refuted} by the
rotation curves (Section~\ref{sec:sparc}). The accumulated forms B and S were introduced after the residual slope was measured, so their
failure refutes these two forms, not every form a GUID prediction could take. Form S fits as well as the RAR but is MOND's simple
interpolating function. As pointwise laws, both forms are \vd{refuted} in the solar system. Neither shows a galaxy-specific history, and the
high-redshift data favour a scale that was larger in the past, not one that accumulates. The rotation curves are consistent with cold dark
matter halos of any kind (\vd{live}), although NFW halos lose to one free RAR scale per galaxy. With halos present, the stochastic term keeps
only the small room of Table~\ref{tab:remnant-joint}, so the two routes compete rather than combine. GUID's remaining dark-matter candidate is
primordial black holes formed in the start~\cite{cotner}; this paper does not test it.
