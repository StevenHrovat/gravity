\section{The holographic DMP code at thousands of qubits}\label{sec:holocode}

Section~\ref{sec:holo} assembles DMP's parts as a holographic quantum error-correcting code on a fixed background and checks its
kinematics on 25 qubits. Those checks cover perfect-tensor tiles, the Ryu--Takayanagi formula as a minimal cut through a layer's tree,
entanglement-wedge reconstruction, and two witnesses that read the existence of content and never the content. At that size cuts,
wedges and errors have no room to interact. We therefore run the same assembly exactly, as a stabiliser state, on a tree of 1706 tiles
with 5120 boundary qubits (6826 qubits with references). We add an erasure test of the hierarchy and, as a control with a known answer,
the toric code. In one sentence: the kinematics of Section~\ref{sec:holo} hold at scale with one qualification. The minimal-cut formula
is exact for every region when the bulk legs are open, but with the bulk fixed it holds only for contiguous regions, and only in one
orientation of the tile. Meanwhile the root of a layer survives random erasure of up to $p_c=(5-\sqrt{13})/6\approx0.2324$ of the
boundary, and the toric code gives $\gamma=\ln2$ at every size.

\subsection{The code and how it is computed}\label{sec:holocode-model}

\paragraph{The tile.} A tile is the six-leg perfect tensor of the five-qubit code~\cite{laflamme,bennett1996}. Let $E$ be the encoding
isometry of $[[5,1,3]]$, whose stabilisers are $XZZXI$ and its cyclic shifts, with $\bar X=XXXXX$ and $\bar Z=ZZZZZ$. The tile is
\[
T_{b\,q_1\dots q_5}=\bra{q_1\dots q_5}E\ket{b}/\sqrt2 ,
\]
where $b$ is the logical leg. Every split of its six legs into three and three is unitary~\cite{happy}.

\paragraph{The tree.} A layer of depth $D$ is a rooted tree of tiles. The root has its bulk leg $b$ and five legs to children; every
other tile has a bulk leg, a leg to its parent and four legs to children. The leaves' legs form the boundary: $n=5\cdot4^D$ qubits, in
ring order, on $1+5(4^D-1)/3$ tiles. For $D=1,\dots,5$ this gives $n=20$, 80, 320, 1280 and 5120 on 6, 26, 106, 426 and 1706 tiles.

\paragraph{Two orientations.} A child tile is a perfect tensor in either of two orientations. In both, the children sit on
$q_2,\dots,q_5$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{A}, the main construction: the 25-qubit assembly of Section~\ref{sec:holo}, with the parent edge on the logical leg $b$
  and the bulk on $q_1$;
\item \emph{H}, the HaPPY convention~\cite{happy}, reported beside it: the bulk on $b$ and the parent edge on $q_1$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two settings.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Setting (a)}: every bulk leg is fixed to $\ket0$, which leaves a pure boundary state.
\item \emph{Setting (b)}: every bulk leg is maximally entangled with its own reference $R_t$. This is the code with all bulk qubits as
  unknown logical inputs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Exact computation.} Every tile map is a Clifford isometry, $V\ket v=U\ket{v,0,\dots,0}$, with $U$ found from the dense
tensor. The stabiliser generators are pushed through the tree as signed Pauli strings~\cite{aaronson2004}. In all 20 networks (five
depths, two settings, two orientations) they are $N$ independent generators on $N$ qubits. Each one, sign included, is a $+1$
stabiliser of an independent composition of the same Cliffords by stim~\cite{gidney2021} \chk{holo:H1}.

\paragraph{Entropies.} Entropies follow exactly from the generator matrix $G$ over GF(2)~\cite{fattal2004,hamma2005}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-rank}
S(A)=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(G|_A\bigr)-|A|\qquad\text{(bits)}.
\end{equation}
At $D=1$ they agree with a dense contraction of the tensors to $2\times10^{-15}$, and on a $3\times3$ toric code they match the dense
state vector exactly \chk{holo:H1}, \chk{holo:H6}.

\paragraph{Recoverability.} Bulk qubit $t$ is recoverable from region $A$ when $I(R_t\!:\!A)=S(R_t)+S(A)-S(R_tA)=2$ bits. For a
stabiliser state
\[
I(R_t\!:\!A)=\dim\bigl(V\cap\langle X_{R_t},Z_{R_t}\rangle\bigr),
\]
where $V$ is the reference part of the stabilisers supported on $A\cup R$. One ordered elimination gives $V$ for all bulk qubits at once.

\paragraph{Cuts and wedges.} The minimal cut $\mathrm{mc}(A)$ counts the legs crossed by the cheapest assignment of tiles to the two
sides, with unit capacity per leg; these are Section~\ref{sec:holo}'s ``fewest hops''. It is computed by dynamic programming on the tree.
In setting (b) a bulk leg is crossed when its tile lies on $A$'s side, because its reference belongs to the complement. The greedy
algorithm of Ref.~\cite{happy} absorbs a tile into $A$'s wedge when at least 3 of its 5 non-bulk legs lie in the region, and is run to
its fixed point. The \emph{residual region} is the set of tiles absorbed neither from $A$ nor from its boundary complement.

\paragraph{Bulk legs open.} In setting (b), $S(A)=\mathrm{mc}(A)$ in all 2\,206\,008 region--network cases, including all $2^{20}$ regions
at $D=1$ \tagS. This is the Ryu--Takayanagi formula~\cite{rt} with the bulk entropy included (Eq.~\eqref{eq:holocode-qrt} below), and
it \vd{holds}.

\paragraph{Bulk legs fixed.} In setting (a), $S(A)\le\mathrm{mc}(A)$ in all 4\,303\,160 cases, as it must be for any tensor network. In
orientation A, equality holds for all 42\,428 contiguous intervals tested at $D=1,\dots,5$. This extends the 25-qubit claim of
Section~\ref{sec:holo} to 5120 qubits. For non-contiguous regions equality fails (Table~\ref{tab:holocode-rt}): in 13.3\,\% of all
regions at $D=1$, and in 37\,\% of the random subsets at $D=5$, with deficits of up to 53 bits. In orientation H it fails even for
10.5 to 32\,\% of the intervals.

\paragraph{Why the exceptions occur.} With its bulk fixed, a tile is a five-leg state whose parent edge shares exactly 1 bit of mutual
information with every pair of its child legs; with the bulk leg open it shares none \chk{holo:H0}. A cut that splits a tile's legs two
against two therefore lets one classical bit past. Whether those bits line up between tiles depends on the orientation.

\paragraph{What characterises them.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Every exception has a non-empty residual region, so the two greedy cuts do not meet.
\item The deficit never exceeded $\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ for $r$ residual tiles. This is observed, not proved.
\item A degenerate minimal cut is not the cause: 95\,735 exceptions have a unique minimal cut.
\end{itemize}
The argument of Ref.~\cite{happy} for connected regions rests on the two greedy cuts meeting. On a tree with fixed bulk they need not
meet, and then the minimal cut is only an upper bound.

\subsection{Entanglement-wedge reconstruction}\label{sec:holocode-wedge}

In setting (b) we computed $I(R_t\!:\!A)$ in 13\,243\,616 (region, bulk qubit) pairs: all $2^{20}$ regions $\times$ 6 qubits $\times$
2 orientations at $D=1$, and intervals and random subsets of 11 sizes at $D=2,\dots,5$ \chk{holo:H3}. $I$ is always 0 or 2 bits, never
1. Four predictions agree in every pair:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item exact recoverability;
\item the greedy wedge;
\item the recursion of Proposition~\ref{prop:holocode-erasure};
\item the min-cut prediction $I_{\rm cut}=1+\mathrm{mc}(A)-\mathrm{mc}(A\cup R_t)$, which never ties.
\end{itemize}
Wedge reconstruction~\cite{adh,dong2016} therefore \vd{holds} at scale \tagS. Table~\ref{tab:holocode-wedge} shows the two regimes:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item An interval holds the bulk geometrically: a deep qubit is in the wedge when its subtree is, and the root needs more than half the
  ring.
\item A random subset holds almost nothing until about 77\,\% of the boundary survives ($1-p_c$, Section~\ref{sec:holocode-erasure}),
  and then nearly everything.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The erasure threshold of the hierarchy}\label{sec:holocode-erasure}

Erase each boundary qubit independently with probability $p$, and let $A$ be the qubits that survive. The edge above a tile is
\emph{available from below} (from above) when its qubit can be recovered from the part of $A$ below the tile (outside its subtree).
A surviving boundary leg counts as available.

\begin{proposition}[exact erasure recursion, setting (b)]\label{prop:holocode-erasure}
Seen from either side, every edge is either available or decoupled, that is, its channel to that part of $A$ is constant.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item It is available from below if and only if at least 3 of the 4 legs below its tile are available.
\item It is available from above if and only if at least 3 of the parent's other 4 non-bulk legs are available.
\item A bulk qubit has $I(R_t\!:\!A)=2$ if at least 3 of its tile's 5 non-bulk legs are available (for the root, 3 of its 5
  children), and $I(R_t\!:\!A)=0$ otherwise.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Any three legs of a perfect tensor are a unitary image of the other three, and any three legs are maximally mixed.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item If at least 3 legs below a tile are available, decoding them recovers the remaining at most three legs: the edge, the bulk and
  at most one child.
\item If at most 2 are available, the edge and those legs are three maximally mixed legs, so $I=1+2-3=0$.
\item By induction on the height, an unavailable subtree is a constant channel. After local decoding, an available subtree is
  equivalent to its edge qubit.
\end{itemize}
The same argument runs from above, and for the bulk leg as target. \hfill$\square$

\smallskip
These availabilities depend on disjoint boundary sets, so they are independent. Let $a_h$ be the probability that the edge above a tile
of height $h$ is available from below (a leaf has $h=1$). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-rec}
a_0=1-p,\qquad a_h=P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(4,a_{h-1})\ge3\bigr)=4a_{h-1}^3-3a_{h-1}^4,\qquad
P_{\rm root}(D)=P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(5,a_D)\ge3\bigr),
\end{equation}
with analogous closed forms for every depth.

\paragraph{The threshold.} The map $f(a)=4a^3-3a^4$ has a fixed point $a^*=(1+\sqrt{13})/6=0.7676$ with slope
$f'(a^*)=12a^{*2}(1-a^*)=1.643>1$. It is unstable, so the erasure threshold is \tagD
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-pc}
p_c=1-a^*=\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}{6}=0.232408 .
\end{equation}
Below $p_c$ the root survives with probability tending to 1 as $D$ grows; above it, with probability tending to 0. The transition
window narrows as $1.643^{-D}$.

\paragraph{At the threshold.} At $p=p_c$ every level sits at the fixed point. Every bulk qubit, at every depth and for every $D$, is then
recovered with probability $P(\mathrm{Bin}(5,a^*)\ge3)=0.914162$. The hierarchy is scale invariant there, and the curves for all $D$
cross exactly at $p_c$ \chk{holo:H4}.

\paragraph{Comparison.} A plain concatenation of the five-qubit code, with no bulk qubit in its inner tiles, needs 3 of 5 children at
every level. Its threshold is $1/2$, the no-cloning bound of the erasure channel~\cite{bennett1997}. Giving every tile its own bulk qubit
lowers the threshold to 0.232.

\paragraph{Checks of the recursion.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item At $D=1$ it equals, to $3\times10^{-13}$, the exact recovery polynomial built from all $2^{20}$ erasure patterns.
\item In 97\,900 Monte Carlo samples (orientation A, $D=1,\dots,5$, 11 values of $p$) the stabiliser computation and the recursion
  agree in every pair of sample and bulk qubit, with no exception. A check in orientation H at $D=5$ agrees in the same way.
\item The fractions match the probabilities at every $(D,p,\text{depth})$ point, with largest $|z|=2.55$ under binomial errors
  (Table~\ref{tab:holocode-mc}).
\item The Monte Carlo curves for $D=4$ and $D=5$ cross at $p=0.2331$, within the noise of 400 samples \chk{holo:H4}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The two witnesses at scale}\label{sec:holocode-witness}

Section~\ref{sec:holo} reads Axiom~3's existence clause as the syndrome. Stabilisers commute with the logical operators, so the syndrome
reports that content is present and never what it is. The $D=5$ code has 5120 qubits, 3414 generators and 1706 logical qubits
\chk{holo:H5}.

\paragraph{Contents and witnesses.} We used six bulk contents: five random products of cardinal states, and one with 400 bulk Bell
pairs. Witness T measures the generators directly. Witness I is coded independently: it measures another basis
($g_1,g_1g_2,g_2g_3,\dots$) through ancilla circuits and decodes it.

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Without noise}, both read the trivial syndrome for every content, and all 10\,236 content observables keep the value $+1$.
\item \emph{Under six Pauli-noise realisations} ($p=0.0005$ to $0.01$), the syndrome is identical bit for bit for all six contents.
  The two witnesses agree in all 42 runs, both equal the error's commutation pattern, and both refuse.
\item \emph{Reading the content} (logical $Z$, then $X$) on 86 bulk qubits drops their mean fidelity to 0.517 (expected $\tfrac12$).
  The other 1620 keep fidelity 1, and the syndrome stays trivial, so the gate cannot see that content was read.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{One tile, all $4^5=1024$ Pauli errors.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The witnesses agree on every error, and the syndrome never depends on the content.
\item The gate refuses 960 errors. It passes 16 stabilisers and 48 undetectable logical errors, all of weight at least 3.
\item Lookup correction restores every single-qubit error. The correctable errors number $1,15,0,60,135,45$ by weight $0,\dots,5$.
\end{itemize}
Under depolarising noise of rate $p$ the logical failure probability is therefore exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-pfail}
P_{\rm fail}=1-\Bigl[(1-p)^5+5p(1-p)^4+\tfrac{20}{9}p^3(1-p)^2+\tfrac53p^4(1-p)+\tfrac{5}{27}p^5\Bigr]=10p^2+O(p^3).
\end{equation}
This lies below the closed form $1-(1-p)^5-5p(1-p)^4$ for a distance-3 code that corrects only the weight-1 errors. The two give
$9.978\times10^{-6}$ against $9.980\times10^{-6}$ at $p=10^{-3}$, $0.0795$ against $0.0815$ at $p=0.1$, and $0.249$ against $0.263$
at $p=0.2$. The difference comes from errors of weight 3 to 5 that equal a correctable error times a stabiliser. The pseudo-threshold
($P_{\rm fail}=p$) is $p=0.1376$.

Haar-random contents on one tile give the same syndrome distribution to machine precision, and reading their content leaves fidelity
exactly $\tfrac12$ \chk{holo:H5}. The existence-only reading \vd{holds} \tagS.

\subsection{The classical centre}\label{sec:holocode-centre}

Section~\ref{sec:holo} closes on Harlow's formula~\cite{harlow},
\[
S(\rho_R)=\Tr(\rho\,\mathcal L_R)+S_{\rm bulk}(\rho,\mathcal M_R),
\]
whose area operator lies in the centre of the reconstructable algebra. In setting (b) we can read it off directly. The cut function is
submodular, so minimal cuts are closed under union, and their largest $A$-side is itself a minimal cut. Because $I_{\rm cut}=I$, that
side is the wedge $W_A$. The cut crosses one bulk leg per tile of $W_A$ and $|\gamma_A|$ non-bulk legs, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-qrt}
S(A)=|\gamma_A|+|W_A|\qquad\text{(setting b)}.
\end{equation}
Suppose the residual region is empty. Then every bulk qubit lies in $W_A$ or in the complement's wedge. Since operators on $A$ commute
with operators on the complement, $\mathcal M_A$ is exactly the algebra of the qubits in $W_A$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:holocode-qrt} is
Harlow's formula with $S_{\rm bulk}=|W_A|$ and $\Tr(\rho\,\mathcal L_A)=|\gamma_A|$, a number. On a fixed tree the area can only be a
number: the assembly realises one fixed-area sector, as Lemma~\ref{lem:classical} requires, and cannot represent a superposition of
areas. That \vd{holds} by construction; the superposition of geometries remains \vd{untested}.

The setting-(a) exceptions show the other side of this. With an empty residual region and a pure bulk, Harlow's formula gives
$S=|\gamma_A|$, and we find $S=\mathrm{mc}$ in every such case. A residual tile is a bulk qubit that neither side can reconstruct alone.
There $\mathcal M_A$ cannot be a tensor factor over whole qubits, Harlow's theorem needs a non-trivial centre, and the entropy of a fixed
bulk falls below the cut. We read the deficits, at most one bit per pair of residual tiles, as the classical centre at work inside one
fixed geometry. This \vd{holds as a reading}: the script does not construct the centre.

\subsection{A control: the toric code}\label{sec:holocode-toric}

We built the ground state of the toric code~\cite{kitaev2003} on $L\times L$ tori for $L=8$, 16, 24, 32, 40 and 45, up to 4050 qubits.
Its generators are the stars, the plaquettes and two non-contractible $Z$ loops, of GF(2) rank $2L^2$. Regions are sets of edges in
disks or annuli about a generic centre. The topological entanglement entropy was computed two ways \chk{holo:H6}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item by the construction of Kitaev and Preskill~\cite{kitaev2006},
  $-\gamma=S_A+S_B+S_C-S_{AB}-S_{BC}-S_{CA}+S_{ABC}$, on disks of radius 2 to 18 cut into three sectors (33 cases);
\item by that of Levin and Wen~\cite{levin2006}, $-2\gamma=(S_1-S_2)-(S_3-S_4)$, on 20 annuli of radii from $2$--$4.5$ to
  $10$--$19.5$. Region 1 is the annulus, 2 and 3 are the annulus with one gap, and 4 has both gaps.
\end{itemize}
Every case gives $\gamma=1$ bit $=\ln2$, the value for $\mathbb Z_2$ topological order, independent of region size and of $L$ \tagP.
The machinery that finds the deficits of Table~\ref{tab:holocode-rt} reproduces this universal constant exactly.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Orientation H was added after the exhaustive $D=1$ run showed interval exceptions in the HaPPY convention.
\item Our first characterisation (exceptions need a degenerate minimal cut) failed on 95\,735 regions. We replaced it by the residual
  region and the observed $\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ bound.
\item The first witness run at scale failed because shortening the pushed generators mixed reference factors into the code stabilisers.
  Shortening now uses only reference-free generators, which leaves every entropy unchanged.
\item The Monte Carlo samples at $D=5$ were raised from 160 to 400.
\item Two floating-point tolerances were widened tenfold, to $10^{-11}$ and $10^{-13}$, after rounding exceeded them.
\item The region sets were sized for runtime.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Kinematics only, on a fixed background.
\item Stabiliser states only. Non-stabiliser contents were tested on one tile.
\item Pauli or erasure noise only, with noiseless syndrome extraction.
\item The $\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ bound is observed in 4.3 million cases, not proved, and the regions at $D\ge2$ are samples.
\item The recursion is exact for setting (b) only.
\item A tree is not our space (Section~\ref{sec:holo}).
\end{itemize}
All numbers in this section come from \texttt{docs/simulation/holographic\_code.py} (77 of 77 checks, about 8.5 minutes on one core).

\paragraph{Verdict.} At 5120 qubits the holographic DMP code behaves as it does at 25. Wedge reconstruction is all or nothing and
coincides exactly with the greedy wedge and the minimal cut. The two witnesses read existence and never content. The Ryu--Takayanagi
formula \vd{holds} exactly with the bulk legs open. With the bulk fixed it holds for contiguous regions only in the orientation of
Section~\ref{sec:holo}, and as a statement about every region it is \vd{refuted}. The root survives erasure of up to
$p_c=(5-\sqrt{13})/6\approx0.232$ of the boundary, a threshold that is \vd{derived} and confirmed sample by sample. The toric-code
control returns $\gamma=\ln2$ exactly.
