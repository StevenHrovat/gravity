\section{The holographic code at scale}\label{sec:holocode}

Section~\ref{sec:holo} assembles DMP's parts as a holographic code on a fixed tree. It states three kinematic properties of that code:
the Ryu--Takayanagi formula as a minimal cut, entanglement-wedge reconstruction, and witnesses that read the existence of content and
never the content. We test all three exactly, on a tree of 1706 tiles with 5120 boundary qubits. Appendix~\ref{app:holocode} holds
the construction, the proofs and the remaining tables.

\paragraph{The test.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{The tiles.} Each tile is the six-leg perfect tensor of the five-qubit code~\cite{laflamme,happy}
  (Primer~\ref{box:holo}).
\item \emph{The tree.} A layer of depth $D$ is a rooted tree. The root has five children and every other tile has four. The $n=5\cdot4^D$
  legs of the leaves form the boundary; we go up to $D=5$, where $n=5120$.
\item \emph{Two orientations of a child tile.} In orientation A, the one of Section~\ref{sec:holo}, the edge to the parent sits on the
  child's logical leg. In orientation H, the HaPPY convention~\cite{happy}, the bulk leg sits there instead.
\item \emph{Two settings.} In setting (a) every bulk leg is fixed to $\ket0$, which leaves a pure boundary state. In setting (b) every
  bulk leg is maximally entangled with its own reference qubit, so all bulk qubits are unknown inputs.
\item \emph{What is computed.} The network is a stabiliser state with generator matrix $G$, so the entropy of a boundary region $A$ is
  exact~\cite{fattal2004}: $S(A)=\operatorname{rank}(G|_A)-|A|$ bits.
\item \emph{What it is compared with.} The minimal cut $\mathrm{mc}(A)$, the fewest legs a cut must cross to separate $A$ from the rest.
  These are the ``fewest hops'' of Section~\ref{sec:holo}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Ryu--Takayanagi.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Bulk legs open.} In setting (b), $S(A)=\mathrm{mc}(A)$ in all 2\,206\,008 region--network cases. These include all $2^{20}$
  regions at $D=1$ \tagS. This is the Ryu--Takayanagi formula~\cite{rt}, with the bulk entropy counted.
\item \emph{Bulk legs fixed.} In setting (a), $S(A)\le\mathrm{mc}(A)$ in all 2\,206\,008 cases, as in any tensor network.
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item In orientation A, equality holds for all 42\,428 tested intervals (exhaustive at $D=1$).
  \item For non-contiguous regions it fails (Table~\ref{tab:holocode-rt}): in 13.3\,\% of all regions at $D=1$, and in 37\,\% of the
    random subsets at $D=5$. The deficits there reach 53 bits.
  \item In orientation H it fails even for 10.5 to 32\,\% of the intervals.
  \end{itemize}
\item \emph{The cause.} With the bulk fixed, a tile's parent edge shares one bit with every pair of its child legs; with the bulk leg
  open it shares none \chk{holo:H0}. A cut that splits a tile's legs two against two therefore lets one classical bit past. Every
  exception contains tiles that neither side's greedy wedge reaches (Appendix~\ref{sec:holocode-rt}).
\item \emph{Wedge reconstruction.} In setting (b), $I(R_t\!:\!A)$ is 0 or 2 bits, never 1, in all 13\,243\,616 pairs of region and bulk
  qubit $t$ \chk{holo:H3}. Four predictions of the wedge agree in every pair: exact recoverability, the greedy wedge~\cite{happy}, the
  recursion below, and the minimal cut (Appendix~\ref{sec:holocode-wedge}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The erasure threshold.} Erase each boundary qubit independently with probability $p$. An edge can be recovered from the
boundary below it exactly when at least 3 of the 4 legs under its tile can. The root can be recovered exactly when at least 3 of its 5
children can (Proposition~\ref{prop:holocode-erasure}, \vd{proven}). The availability $a_h$ of an edge above a tile of height $h$ is
therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-rec}
a_0=1-p,\qquad a_h=4a_{h-1}^3-3a_{h-1}^4,\qquad P_{\rm root}(D)=P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(5,a_D)\ge3\bigr).
\end{equation}
The unstable fixed point $a^*=(1+\sqrt{13})/6$ of this map sets the erasure threshold, \vd{proven}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holocode-pc}
p_c=1-a^*=\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}{6}=0.232408 .
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item As $D$ grows, the root survives with probability tending to 1 below $p_c$ and to 0 above it.
\item At $p_c$ every level sits at the fixed point. Every bulk qubit, at every depth and for every $D$, is then recovered with the same
  probability $P(\mathrm{Bin}(5,a^*)\ge3)=0.914162$. The hierarchy is scale invariant there.
\item A plain concatenation of the five-qubit code, with no bulk qubit inside, has threshold $1/2$. That is the no-cloning bound of the
  erasure channel~\cite{bennett1997}.
\item In 97\,900 Monte Carlo samples the stabiliser computation and the recursion agree for every sample and every bulk qubit
  (Table~\ref{tab:holocode-mc}) \chk{holo:H4}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The two witnesses.} A syndrome commutes with every logical operator. It therefore reports
that content is in the code sector, and never what the content is. So existence witnesses detect faults that leave the sector and miss
faults within it. We see both at scale \chk{holo:H5}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{The code.} At $D=5$ the code has 3414 stabiliser generators and 1706 logical qubits.
\item \emph{The two witnesses.} Witness T reads the generators directly. Witness I is coded independently: it reads another basis
  through ancilla circuits.
\item \emph{Without noise.} For six bulk contents, both read the trivial syndrome, and all 10\,236 content observables keep the value
  $+1$.
\item \emph{Under noise.} In six Pauli-noise realisations ($p=0.0005$ to $0.01$) the syndrome is identical bit for bit across the
  contents. The two witnesses agree in all 42 runs, and both refuse.
\item \emph{Reading the content.} Reading logical $Z$, then logical $X$, on 86 bulk qubits drops their mean fidelity to 0.517 (expected
  $\frac12$). The syndrome stays trivial, so the gate cannot see that the content was read.
\item \emph{One tile.} On one tile, 960 of the 1024 Pauli errors leave the sector, and both witnesses refuse them. Of the 64 that stay
  inside, 16 are stabilisers and do no harm. The other 48 are logical errors of weight at least 3: they change the content and pass
  both witnesses unseen.
\end{itemize}
A fault within the sector is invisible to existence witnesses by construction. Only the code distance protects against it: lookup
correction restores every single-qubit fault, and the logical failure rate falls as $10p^2$ at small $p$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:holocode-witness}).

All numbers here and in Appendix~\ref{app:holocode} come from one script,
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak simulation/\allowbreak holographic\_code.py}, which passes 77 of 77 checks in about 8.5 minutes on one core.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The Ryu--Takayanagi formula \vd{holds} exactly on this tree when the bulk legs are open.
\item With the bulk fixed it holds for contiguous regions, and then only in the orientation of Section~\ref{sec:holo}. As a statement
  about every region it is \vd{refuted}.
\item Wedge reconstruction \vd{holds}, and is all or nothing.
\item The erasure threshold $p_c=(5-\sqrt{13})/6\approx0.232$ is \vd{proven}, and at $p_c$ the recovery probability is the same at
  every depth.
\item The witnesses read existence and never content. They detect every fault that leaves the code sector and miss every fault within
  it (\vd{proven} for any stabiliser code, and seen at 5120 qubits).
\end{itemize}
