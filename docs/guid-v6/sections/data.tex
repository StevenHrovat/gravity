\section{Every other prediction against current data}\label{sec:data}

Part~I predicts more than galactic dynamics. It predicts universal free fall with an $\alpha$-setting scalar
(Sections~\ref{sec:fix} and~\ref{sec:alpha}), the post-Newtonian limit of general relativity, a late universe that settles into de Sitter
expansion after a start that is inflation (Section~\ref{sec:cosmo}), Kerr black holes (Section~\ref{sec:bh}), the Di\'osi--Penrose rate through
Bridge~B (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}), primordial black holes as the contained remnant (Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}), and the electroweak and
strong splits (Section~\ref{sec:matter}). This section confronts every one of these predictions that a published measurement reaches. Rotation
curves are treated in Sections~\ref{sec:sparc} and~\ref{sec:remnant}. In one sentence: 54 of 58 scored confrontations hold at 95\%; the four
that do not are tensions GUID inherits unchanged from $\Lambda$CDM or from the plateau inflation Part~I names; and no datum yet reaches a
prediction that tells GUID apart from general relativity, $\Lambda$CDM and the Standard Model \chk{data:summary}.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:data-method}

A confrontation sets a GUID value $x_G$, with a theory uncertainty $\sigma_G$ where one exists (for example propagated from Planck's
$\Omega_m$), against a datum. For a Gaussian datum $x\pm\sigma$ (one standard deviation; a published 95\% half-width $b$ enters as $\sigma=b/1.96$) we
form the \emph{pull}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pull}
p=\frac{x-x_G}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\sigma_G^2}} .
\end{equation}
Other data are allowed regions at 90\% or more, or probabilities and qualitative statements of the experimenters. The rule is one line. A
confrontation is \textbf{OK} when $|p|<1.96$ or $x_G$ lies inside the allowed region, and a \textbf{FAIL} otherwise; it stays a FAIL when general
relativity, $\Lambda$CDM or the Standard Model share it. Passing a 90\% region implies passing at 95\%. A row is \textbf{INFO}, reported but not
scored, when a newer measurement that includes its data supersedes it, when it is derived rather than measured, or when it overlaps a scored
datum; 14 of 72 rows are INFO. Independent determinations of one quantity that disagree beyond their errors are combined with the weighted
mean and the Particle Data Group's scale factor $S=[\chi^2/(N-1)]^{1/2}\ge1$, which multiplies the error of the mean. Were GUID right and the
58 scored data independent, $0.05\times58=2.9$ FAILs would occur by chance. Every measured number was checked against its publication (see
the caveats below). Six checks of the script's numerics (closed forms against limits and quadrature, the Kerr efficiency at $a=-1,0,1$) all
pass.

\subsection{D1: the information scalar}\label{sec:data-alpha}

\paragraph{Model.} Hypothesis~4 adds a scalar $\phi$ that sets the fine-structure constant (Section~\ref{sec:alpha}). In units $\hbar=c=1$ with
$\bar M_P^2=1/8\pi G$, Part~I's action gives it the kinetic term $-\tfrac12\bar M_P^2(\partial\phi)^2$, no potential, and the coupling
$\lambda=\dd\ln\alpha/\dd\phi$ \tagH. A body whose mass has electrostatic share $f_A$ then has $\partial\ln m_A/\partial\phi=\lambda f_A$. Exchange of
$\phi$ adds $2\lambda^2f_Af_B$ to the Newtonian force, and two bodies falling towards the Earth differ by $\eta=2\lambda^2f_\oplus(f_1-f_2)$
(Section~\ref{sec:fix};~\cite{dz}) \tagP. The Coulomb term $a_CZ(Z-1)/A^{1/3}$ of the semi-empirical mass formula gives $f_{\rm Ti}=2.02\times10^{-3}$,
$f_{\rm Pt}=4.05\times10^{-3}$, $f_{\rm Be}=4.89\times10^{-4}$ and, for a coarse Earth composition, $f_\oplus=1.71\times10^{-3}$. MICROSCOPE then
measures $\eta=k\lambda^2$ with $k=-6.94\times10^{-6}$ \chk{data:D1.1}.

\paragraph{The coupling.} We profile $\chi^2_{\rm EP}(\lambda)=\sum_i[(k_i\lambda^2-\eta_i)/\sigma_i]^2$ over MICROSCOPE~\cite{microscope} (errors in
quadrature, $2.75\times10^{-15}$) and the E\"ot-Wash Be/Ti balance~\cite{schlamminger2008}, with $\lambda^2\ge0$. The best fit, $|\lambda|=1.47\times10^{-5}$,
is not significant, and $\Delta\chi^2=3.84$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-lambda}
|\lambda|<3.1\times10^{-5}\quad(95\%\ \mathrm{CL})\qquad\chk{data:D1.1},
\end{equation}
set by MICROSCOPE alone. The value $2\times10^{-5}$ of earlier versions is the one-standard-deviation bound, $|\eta|<2.7\times10^{-15}$.

\paragraph{Model A: the action as written.} Without a potential, the scalar is driven only by the electromagnetic energy of matter, as in the
theories of Bekenstein~\cite{bekenstein82} and of Sandvik, Barrow and Magueijo~\cite{bsbm}. Dark matter, a gravitational remnant, carries none;
baryons carry an electrostatic share $f_b>0$. For pressureless baryons with $\partial\rho_b/\partial\phi=\lambda f_b\rho_b$, the field equation
$\bar M_P^2\Box\phi=\partial\rho_b/\partial\phi$ in a flat Friedmann universe reads, with $N=\ln a$ and $\phi'=\dd\phi/\dd N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-modelA}
\phi''+\Big(3+\frac{\dd\ln H}{\dd N}\Big)\phi'=-3\lambda f_b\,\Omega_b(N),
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_b(N)$ the baryon share of the critical density. In the matter era $\dd\ln H/\dd N=-3/2$, and every solution approaches
$\phi'=-2\lambda f_b\Omega_b/\Omega_m$ (checked numerically). Integrated through flat $\Lambda$CDM~\cite{planck18} to today, it gives
$\phi'_0=-0.141\,\lambda f_b$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-driftA}
\frac{\dot\alpha}{\alpha}\Big|_0=\lambda H_0\phi'_0=-9.70\times10^{-12}\,\lambda^2f_b\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}\le0\qquad\chk{data:D1.3}.
\end{equation}
The scalar rolls towards lower matter energy, and a positive electrostatic share makes that a fall in $\alpha$. GUID's forward statement that
$\alpha$ does not grow is therefore \vd{derived} for the action as written. It is not observable. The helium Coulomb energy alone gives
$f_b=6.0\times10^{-5}$. Even with $f_b=10^{-3}$, taken as a generous upper value for the electromagnetic self-energy of nucleons, and $|\lambda|$ at
the bound~\eqref{eq:data-lambda}, the drift is $-9.6\times10^{-24}$ per year. That is four orders of magnitude below the clocks, with
$\Delta\alpha/\alpha=2.1\times10^{-12}$ at recombination.

\paragraph{Model B: a slowly rolling scalar.} To ask what the data can say at all, we give the scalar a linear potential $V=V_0-s\phi$ and make it a
thawing dark energy, frozen early by Hubble friction. A canonical scalar has $\rho_\phi+p_\phi=\bar M_P^2\dot\phi^2$, so $\phi'^2=3\Omega_\phi(1+w)$
exactly. For small slopes the history has a universal shape, and each $\alpha$ datum measures the present drift $d=\dot\alpha/\alpha|_0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-G}
\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\alpha}(z)=-\frac{d}{H_0}G(z),\qquad G(z)=\int_{-\ln(1+z)}^{0}\frac{\phi'(N)}{\phi'(0)}\,\dd N .
\end{equation}
$G$ is $0.118$ at Oklo ($z=0.137$, the 1.8\,Gyr implied by the quoted bound and rate of Damour and Dyson~\cite{damour1996}), $0.435$ at $z=1.15$,
$0.514$ at $z=6.3$ and $0.516$ from recombination back. For $1+w_0=0.16$ these values change by less than 2\%. Each datum gives
$d_i=-H_0(\Delta\alpha/\alpha)_i/G(z_i)$, with one-standard-deviation widths of $2.5\times10^{-19}$ (clocks~\cite{filzinger2023}, which include the
data of~\cite{lange}), $3.3\times10^{-18}$ (Oklo~\cite{davis2015}), $2.2\times10^{-16}$ (ESPRESSO~\cite{espresso}), $9.7\times10^{-15}$
(quasars~\cite{wilczynska2020}), $3.3\times10^{-13}$ (CMB~\cite{hart2018}) and $6.7\times10^{-13}$ per year (nucleosynthesis~\cite{avelino2001};
Fig.~\ref{fig:data-alpha}a). Their weighted mean is $d=(1.79\pm2.49)\times10^{-19}$ per year, with $\chi^2=3.05$ for 5 degrees of freedom
\chk{data:D1.12}.

\paragraph{The sign.} The data say nothing about it. The clocks dominate; their central value is positive at $0.7\sigma$; $P(d<0)=0.24$; and
the 95\% interval $[-3.1,6.7]\times10^{-19}$ per year holds both signs. In Model B, drift and coupling are linked only by the field speed,
$d=\lambda H_0\phi'_0$. If the scalar drove the evolving dark energy DESI prefers, with $1+w_0=0.162$ and so $\phi'_0=0.577$, the clocks would
demand $|\lambda|<1.7\times10^{-8}$, about two thousand times below the free-fall bound. For $1+w_0=10^{-3}$ they would demand
$|\lambda|<2.1\times10^{-7}$ \chk{data:D1.12} (Fig.~\ref{fig:data-alpha}b). \emph{Verdict:} every $\alpha$ datum agrees with GUID (D1.1 to D1.12).
Free fall \vd{holds}, and the coupling is bounded by~\eqref{eq:data-lambda}. The negative drift is \vd{derived} for the action as written and
\vd{untested}; where a drift could be seen, its sign is not predicted.

\subsection{D2: post-Newtonian gravity}\label{sec:data-ppn}

Minimal coupling (Section~\ref{sec:fix}) gives the post-Newtonian limit of general relativity \tagP: $\gamma=\beta=1$, a Nordtvedt parameter
$\eta_N=4(\beta-1)-(\gamma-1)=0$~\cite{fienga2024}, the gravitational redshift of the lapse, and massless classical waves at $c$ with two tensor
polarisations. All twelve confrontations hold; the largest pull is $1.06\sigma$ (lunar $\gamma$), and $\chi^2=5.63$ for 10 Gaussian pulls gives
$p=0.84$, indicative only because Cassini's radio data also enter the ephemerides \chk{data:D2}. The final GWTC-3 tests also find no non-tensor polarisation~\cite{ligo2025a}. The
lunar errors are read as one standard deviation, which the paper does not state; this is the stricter reading. \emph{Verdict:} \vd{holds},
and adds nothing to general relativity.

\subsection{D3: the balanced state}\label{sec:data-cosmo}

GUID's late universe is flat $\Lambda$CDM settling into de Sitter expansion, $H\to H_\infty=\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}H_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:cosmo}).
Acceleration starts at $z_t=(2\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m)^{1/3}-1$: $0.632\pm0.018$ for Planck's $\Omega_m=0.315\pm0.007$~\cite{planck18}, or
$0.664\pm0.009$ for DESI DR2 with the CMB~\cite{desi2}. Two independent determinations exist. Cosmic chronometers give $0.4\pm0.1$~\cite{moresco2016}.
Supernovae with $H(z)$ give $0.883\pm0.110$~\cite{jesus2018}, the mean of three parametrisations with half their spread added as a systematic
error. They pull by $-2.3\sigma$ and $+2.2\sigma$ and disagree with each other by $3.2\sigma$. Combined with $S=3.25$ they give $0.62\pm0.24$, a pull of
$-0.06\sigma$ \chk{data:D3.1}. The de Sitter rate is derived, not measured: $H_\infty=55.79\pm0.60\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$, so
$c/H_\infty=1.66\times10^{26}$\,m (Planck), and it exists only if $w=-1$ \chk{data:D3.2}. The direct test is the equation of state. DESI DR2 fits
$w=w_0+w_a(1-a)$; with the CMB it finds $w_0=-0.42\pm0.21$, $w_a=-1.75\pm0.58$, which disfavours $\Lambda$CDM at $3.1\sigma$, and with the three
supernova samples it finds 2.8, 3.8 and $4.2\sigma$~\cite{desi2}. A recalibration of the DES supernovae lowers its value to $3.2\sigma$, with Bayesian
odds of about 5:1~\cite{popovic2025}. Scored on the weakest combination, $2.8\sigma$, the floor FAILs \chk{data:D3.3}. The model's
$H_0=67.4\pm0.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$ lies $4.9\sigma$ from the Cepheid ladder, $73.04\pm1.04$~\cite{riess2022}, and $1.5\sigma$ from the tip of the
red giant branch, $70.39\pm1.94$~\cite{freedman2025} \chk{data:D3.4,D3.5}. Inflation predicts $|\Omega_K|\lesssim10^{-5}$. Planck with BAO confirms
flatness to 0.4\% (95\%)~\cite{planck2020x}; DESI with the CMB finds $\Omega_K=(2.3\pm1.1)\times10^{-3}$, $2.1\sigma$ from flat, while reporting no
significant preference \chk{data:D3.6,D3.7}.

inflation~\cite{bezrukov} as an open alternative. At leading order both predict $n_s=1-2/N$ and $r=12/N^2$ for the $N$ e-folds after the pivot scale
leaves the horizon: $n_s=0.960$ to $0.967$ and $r=0.0048$ to $0.0033$ for $N=50$ to 60 \tagP. Planck's $n_s=0.9649\pm0.0042$~\cite{planck2020x}
allows $46\le N\le74$ at 95\% (pulls $+1.17$ to $-0.42\sigma$). ACT DR6 moves $n_s$ up. With Planck (P-ACT, $0.9709\pm0.0038$; reported, not
scored) it needs $N\ge54.7$; with CMB lensing and DESI BAO (P-ACT-LB, $0.974\pm0.003$~\cite{louis2025}) it needs $N\ge62.7$. That pulls $+2.44\sigma$ at
$N=60$, a FAIL \chk{data:D4.1,D4.3}. The ratio $r\le0.0048$ is far below $r<0.036$~\cite{bk18} \chk{data:D4.4}. Hypothesis~6$'$ puts the strong split
before or during inflation. After inflation, the Kibble--Preskill estimate of one monopole per horizon at $T_c\sim10^{16}$\,GeV~\cite{preskill}
would give a flux of about $4\times10^2\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}s^{-1}sr^{-1}}$ at $v=10^{-3}c$, $3\times10^{18}$ times MACRO's bound~\cite{macro};
inflation dilutes it to nothing \chk{data:D4.5} (scored once, as D8.2). The ordering also needs reheating below the unification energy.
Instantaneous reheating at the tensor bound gives at most $5.7\times10^{15}$\,GeV, against $M_X\gtrsim4.8\times10^{15}$\,GeV from proton decay
\chk{Q20}. The data allow either order, so this constrains the reheating model and does not test it \chk{data:D4.6}.

\subsection{D5: black holes}\label{sec:data-bh}

GUID's black holes are Kerr's (Section~\ref{sec:bh}). Christodoulou's formula~\cite{christodoulou} has no solution above $|a|=1$, spin energy is
extracted in the ergosphere, and the area never shrinks classically. Matter reaching the innermost stable orbit of a thin disk radiates
$\epsilon(a)=1-E_{\rm isco}(a)$~\cite{bpt}: $0.0377$ for $a=-1$ (retrograde), $0.0572$ for $a=0$, $0.423$ for $a\to1$ \chk{data:D5}. The Soltan argument
compares the light of all quasars with the mass in black holes today. It gives $\epsilon=0.04$ to $0.16$~\cite{marconi2004}, $0.075$ to
$0.094$~\cite{shankar2009}, above $0.1$ for luminous quasars~\cite{yu2002}, and a mean of $0.089$ from individual disk fits~\cite{davis2011}. All lie below
the Kerr maximum, and they imply typical spins of $0.40$ to $0.62$, above $0.67$ for luminous quasars, and $0.58$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:data-kerr}a). The
curve bounds the efficiency only from above: radiatively inefficient flows can fall below $5.7\%$. All 61 measured spins of the compilation of
Reynolds~\cite{reynolds2021}, 36 supermassive and 25 stellar, lie in $[-1,1]$ at 90\%. The test is weak, because the spectral models are Kerr models
capped at $a=0.998$. The ringdown of GW150914 shows an area increase with probability 0.97~\cite{isi2021}, and that of GW250114 shows it ``to high
credibility''~\cite{ligo2025b} \chk{data:D5.6,D5.7}. The Hawking radiation of a solar-mass hole, at $6.2\times10^{-8}$\,K, is unobservable and
unobserved. \emph{Verdict:} \vd{holds}, with general relativity.

\subsection{D6: Bridge B against the largest superpositions}\label{sec:data-bridgeB}

Bridge B (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}) gives the Di\'osi--Penrose rate~\cite{diosi87,penrose96} $\Gamma=E_\Delta/\hbar$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-EDelta}
E_\Delta=\frac G2\iint\frac{[\rho_1(\mathbf x)-\rho_2(\mathbf x)][\rho_1(\mathbf y)-\rho_2(\mathbf y)]}{|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|}\,\dd^3x\,\dd^3y
\end{equation}
for a superposition of the mass densities $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$; Part~I uses its order of magnitude $GM^2/\hbar R$. For a uniform sphere displaced by
$d=xR$, $E_\Delta=(GM^2/R)(x^2/2-3x^3/16+x^5/160)$ for $x<2$ and $(GM^2/R)(6/5-1/x)$ for $x\ge2$. For a mass smeared as a Gaussian of width $s$,
$E_\Delta=GM^2[1/(\sqrt\pi s)-\mathrm{erf}(d/2s)/d]$; both forms agree with Fourier quadrature \chk{data:method}. For each of the largest
superpositions made we take the larger of $GM^2/\hbar R$ and the full $E_\Delta/\hbar$. The full value is the bulk sphere plus every nucleus smeared
over the smallest allowed width $R_0=0.54\times10^{-10}$\,m, because a larger $R_0$ lowers the rate; an atom is its nucleus smeared over $R_0$. A run is
consistent when $\Gamma\tau<0.1$ over its observed coherence time $\tau$.

free in Bridge B. The absence of spontaneous radiation in an underground germanium detector requires $R_0>0.54\times10^{-10}$\,m with probability
0.95~\cite{donadi}, which excludes Penrose's parameter-free choice ($5\times10^{-12}$\,m for germanium). Current bounds are summarised as
$R_0\gtrsim4\times10^{-10}$\,m~\cite{figurato}. Bridge B therefore takes $R_0\ge4\times10^{-10}$\,m, at which the bulk term still exceeds the granular term
of the $10^{-14}$\,kg sphere a thousandfold \chk{data:D6.5,D6.6,data:method}. Its diffusion, $l_P^2D_2=m_P^2/c=1.6\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm{kg^2\,s/m}$,

\paragraph{The smallest decisive experiment.} A uniform sphere separated by more than its size has $\tau_{\rm DP}=\hbar R/(\tfrac65GM^2)\propto
M^{-5/3}$. For silica, $\tau_{\rm DP}$ is 10\,ms at $1.2\times10^{-14}$\,kg, 1\,s at $7.6\times10^{-16}$\,kg and 100\,s at $4.8\times10^{-17}$\,kg \chk{data:D6}.
The first experiment to see Bridge B superposes such a particle by more than its diameter for that long. That is five to eight orders of
magnitude in mass beyond the heaviest superposition so far, $3.3\times10^{-22}$\,kg~\cite{pedalino2025} (Fig.~\ref{fig:data-infl}, right). The diamond
microsphere of Bose et al.~\cite{bose} ($10^{-14}$\,kg, radius $0.88\,\mu$m) decoheres in 13.9\,ms by $GM^2/\hbar R$ and in 11.6\,ms by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:data-EDelta} at 250\,$\mu$m, long before its 8 to 25\,s entangling time (Section~\ref{sec:bmv}). \emph{Verdict:} consistent with every
superposition made, and tested by none: \vd{live}, \vd{untested}.

\subsection{D7, D8, D9: remnants, splits and the rest}\label{sec:data-rest}

\paragraph{Contained remnants.} The contained route makes dark matter of primordial black holes of $10^{14}$ to $10^{19}$\,kg from the start
(Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}). For a monochromatic mass function, Gorton and Green~\cite{gortongreen} find the window from about $10^{17}$ to
$10^{22}$\,g open for all of the dark matter; it narrows for extended mass functions. INTEGRAL/SPI gamma rays and Voyager~1's positrons and
electrons, products of Hawking evaporation, close it below about $10^{17}$\,g. Subaru-HSC microlensing of M31, blind below about $10^{22}$\,g
through wave optics, bounds it above. The Hawking temperatures at the edges are $1.2\times10^9$\,K ($k_BT=0.11$\,MeV) and $1.2\times10^4$\,K. The
mass evaporated within 13.8\,Gyr, $1.7\times10^{11}$\,kg, agrees within a factor of ten with the ``about $10^{15}$\,g'' of Carr et
al.~\cite{carr2021} \chk{data:D7.1-D7.3}. \emph{Verdict:} \vd{live}; the glow predicted at the light edge is what bounds the window.

\paragraph{The splits.} A unification before the start (Hypothesis~6$'$) would still let protons decay, with $\tau\sim M_X^4/(\alpha_G^2m_p^5)$.
That is $4.6\times10^{35}$\,yr for $M_X=10^{16}$\,GeV and $\alpha_G=1/40$, above Super-Kamiokande's $2.4\times10^{34}$\,yr~\cite{superk};
Hyper-Kamiokande~\cite{hyperk} is the live test. Relic monopoles are absent, as MACRO requires~\cite{macro} \chk{data:D8.1,D8.2}. At the electroweak
split the W and Z take their masses from $v=(\sqrt2G_F)^{-1/2}=246.22$\,GeV~\cite{mulan2013} \tagP. From $1/\hat\alpha(M_Z)=127.950\pm0.017$~\cite{erler2017}
and $\sin^2\hat\theta_W(M_Z)=0.23129\pm0.00004$~\cite{navas2024}, the tree-level $m_W=(\pi\hat\alpha/\sqrt2G_F\sin^2\hat\theta_W)^{1/2}=80.22$\,GeV lies $0.18\%$
below $80.369\pm0.013$\,GeV (without CDF)~\cite{lhctev2024}. Likewise $m_Z=m_W/\cos\hat\theta_W=91.50$\,GeV lies $0.34\%$ above
$91.1880\pm0.0020$\,GeV~\cite{navas2024}, within Part~I's half per cent \chk{data:D8.3,D8.4}. Tree level is scheme dependent: $m_Z\cos\hat\theta_W$ gives
79.95\,GeV and the on-shell route from $\alpha(0)$, $G_F$ and $m_Z$ gives 80.94\,GeV. The measurement lies inside that spread, and only the loop-level
Standard Model reaches its precision. The coupling is born near $1/128$ and runs to $1/137.035999177(21)$~\cite{codata2022} \chk{data:D8.5}.
$m_H=125.11\pm0.11$\,GeV~\cite{atlas2023} lies above the end point of the first-order line, between 70 and 95\,GeV~\cite{kajantie1996}, so the split is the
lattice crossover at $T_c=159.5\pm1.5$\,GeV~\cite{donofrio} \chk{data:D8.6}. \emph{Verdict:} \vd{holds}; it is all the Standard Model's.

\paragraph{The rest.} Quantum content reaches but does not exceed Tsirelson's bound $2\sqrt2$, while the gates give 2 (Section~\ref{sec:holo}). The
best value, $S=2.82759\pm0.00051$~\cite{poh2015}, lies $1.64\sigma$ below the bound, and the loophole-free $S=2.42\pm0.20$~\cite{hensen2015} lies
inside $(2,2\sqrt2]$. The known neutrinos are not the remnant: $\sum m_\nu<0.064$\,eV~\cite{desi2} leaves them below 0.57\% of the dark matter, and
KATRIN's $m_\nu<0.45$\,eV~\cite{katrin2025} leaves at most 12\%. The radiated remnant has $\Omega_{\rm gw}\le1.3\times10^{-8}$ at 1/yr~\cite{nanograv}
and is radiation. A gravity that weighed only the Higgs-given mass would separate titanium and platinum by $\eta\sim10^{-3}$, excluded by
$4\times10^{11}\sigma$; gravity weighs all energy (Hypothesis~1$'$) \chk{data:D9.1-D9.6}.

\subsection{The four inherited tensions}\label{sec:data-tensions}

The four FAILs are real, and each is a tension of the established account that GUID adopts unchanged; GUID adds nothing that moves any of them.
\emph{Evolving dark energy} (D3.3): no combination reaches $5\sigma$, the recalibrated DES supernovae lower their own value to $3.2\sigma$, and the
fitted $w$ crosses $-1$, which no single canonical scalar can do. If the preference holds, the future is not de Sitter and the balanced state of
Section~\ref{sec:cosmo} must be read again; an information scalar rolling with that dark energy would have to couple below $1.7\times10^{-8}$.
\emph{The Hubble constant} (D3.4): $4.9\sigma$ from the Cepheid ladder but $1.5\sigma$ from the TRGB; the disagreement between the local methods is
part of the open question. \emph{Curvature} (D3.7): $2.1\sigma$ from DESI with the CMB, which its
authors call not significant, against flatness to 0.4\% from Planck with BAO.
\emph{The spectral index} (D4.3): at leading order, the plateau inflation Part~I names needs $N\ge63$ for P-ACT-LB and $N\ge55$ for P-ACT, while Planck
alone accepts $N=50$ to 60. The BAO that also drive D3.3 cause part of the shift. How many e-folds belong to the pivot scale depends on reheating,
so the gap of a few e-folds constrains the reheating as much as the potential. The model is in tension; GUID's start, which needs inflation but
not this potential, is not.

\subsection{No data yet}\label{sec:data-nodata}

No measurement reaches the predictions that would tell GUID apart: gravitationally mediated entanglement ($\mathcal N=0$~\cite{bose,mv}, with
decoherence first under Bridge B, Section~\ref{sec:bmv}), the LOCC inequality~\cite{lpp}, the decoherence-diffusion trade-off at equality (only the
window of D6.8 is known), Bridge B's decoherence itself (Section~\ref{sec:data-bridgeB}) and the sign of the drift of $\alpha$. Tensor modes, the Hawking
glow at the window's light edge and proton decay have bounds only; single-graviton clicks do not discriminate; the return of information is
reached by no observation.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.} The two $z_t$ determinations were combined with the scale factor after they were seen to disagree,
and one paper's three parametrisations were merged with their spread as a systematic error; scored alone, either would FAIL, in opposite
directions. Further, DESI is scored on its least significant combination; Planck and P-ACT-LB are scored, and P-ACT, which overlaps both,
is reported; the lunar errors are read as one standard deviation; the Kerr test uses the upper end of each efficiency range; D7.2 carries a
tolerance of a factor of ten; Bridge B passes a run below $\Gamma\tau=0.1$; and the monopole datum is scored once. The model inputs are $f_b=10^{-3}$ as an upper value, 1.8\,Gyr for Oklo, a molecular density of 1300\,kg/m$^3$, and the cluster separation at its
stated lower bound. No verdict except D3.1 depends on these choices.

\paragraph{Caveats.} Several values were read through a web search, because the publisher's page could not be opened. They are Cassini's $\gamma$,
MESSENGER's $\eta_N$, the Particle Data Group values of $m_Z$ and $\sin^2\hat\theta_W$, the 2017 value of $\hat\alpha(M_Z)$, the CODATA $\alpha$, the
interferometer parameters of Fein et al., and the species and separations of Refs.~\cite{kovachy2015,xu2019}. The DESI significances are the
collaboration's conversions of $\Delta\chi^2$ for two extra parameters. Model B's $w_0$-to-speed mapping is exact at $z=0$, but CPL is not a thawing fit. The nucleosynthesis datum uses 2001 abundances; recent analyses reach parts per million but let all couplings vary
together~\cite{martins2021}, which a scalar that sets only $\alpha$ does not do. Spins are measured with Kerr models, and the GW250114 statement is
qualitative.

All numbers in this section come from \texttt{docs/simulation/predictions\_vs\_data.py} (72 rows, 54 of 58 scored confrontations consistent,
6 of 6 method checks, about four seconds on one core).

\paragraph{Verdict.} Of 58 confrontations of GUID with current data, 54 hold at 95\%. The four that do not are tensions of $\Lambda$CDM and of
plateau inflation that GUID inherits unchanged: evolving dark energy, the Hubble constant, a $2\sigma$ curvature and the spectral index. Everything else
holds, but holds equally for general relativity, $\Lambda$CDM and the Standard Model. The information scalar's negative drift is derived and is too small to measure, and Bridge B lies
five to eight orders of magnitude in mass beyond the largest superpositions made. No existing datum tells GUID apart; the experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:exp} can.
