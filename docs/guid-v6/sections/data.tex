\section{Every other prediction against current data}\label{sec:data}

Part~I predicts more than galactic dynamics. It predicts universal free fall with an $\alpha$-setting scalar
(Sections~\ref{sec:fix} and~\ref{sec:alpha}), the post-Newtonian limit of general relativity, and a late universe that settles into de Sitter
expansion after a start that is inflation and that contains the strong split (Section~\ref{sec:cosmo}, Hypothesis~6$'$). It also predicts Kerr
black holes (Section~\ref{sec:bh}), the Di\'osi--Penrose rate through Bridge~B (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}), primordial black holes as the contained
remnant (Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}) and the electroweak split (Section~\ref{sec:matter}). This section confronts every one of these
predictions that a published measurement reaches; rotation curves are treated in Sections~\ref{sec:sparc} and~\ref{sec:remnant}. In one sentence:
of 33 scored confrontations, 30 hold at 95\% and 3 fail. The three FAILs are correlated tensions that GUID inherits unchanged from $\Lambda$CDM, and
only 13 of the 33 test content specific to GUID, none of it at a sensitivity that could tell GUID apart \chk{data:summary}.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:data-method}

A confrontation sets a GUID value $x_G$, with a theory uncertainty $\sigma_G$ where one exists (for example propagated from Planck's
$\Omega_m$), against a datum. For a Gaussian datum $x\pm\sigma$ (one standard deviation; a published 95\% half-width $b$ enters as $\sigma=b/1.96$) we
form the \emph{pull}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pull}
p=\frac{x-x_G}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\sigma_G^2}} .
\end{equation}
Other data are allowed regions, or probabilities and qualitative statements of the experimenters. A test is \textbf{OK} when $|p|<1.96$ or $x_G$
lies inside the allowed region, and a \textbf{FAIL} otherwise. A FAIL stays a FAIL when general relativity, $\Lambda$CDM or the Standard Model
share it. Where a region is quoted at 90\% CL the table says so; passing it implies passing at 95\%. Each of the 74 rows has one of four kinds,
and the reason for every kind other than scored is stated with the row \chk{data:summary}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Scored} (33): a prediction confronted with a datum it was not set from. Only these enter the tally.
\item \emph{Conditional} (2): the same test for the plateau inflation potential, which Part~I names but GUID does not derive; reported apart.
\item \emph{Consistency} (12): the prediction or statement was set from this datum, so it holds by construction. These rows are marked CONS
and are never counted as successes.
\item \emph{INFO} (27): superseded by a newer measurement that includes its data, derived rather than measured, overlapping a scored row,
inconclusive, or without a usable uncertainty; reported, not scored.
\end{itemize}
Were GUID right and the 33 scored data independent, $0.05\times33=1.7$ FAILs would occur by chance. They are not independent: D3.3 and D3.7 both use
DESI DR2 BAO with the CMB, D3.4 rests on the CMB fit of $\Lambda$CDM, and the conditional D4.3 uses the CMB with DESI DR1 BAO. Every measured number
was checked against the arXiv text or abstract of its source; rows read only through secondary sources are listed under Caveats. Six checks of
the script's numerics all pass.

\subsection{D1: the information scalar}\label{sec:data-alpha}

\paragraph{Model.} Hypothesis~\ref{h:info} adds a scalar $\phi$ that sets the fine-structure constant (Section~\ref{sec:alpha}). In units $\hbar=c=1$ with
$\bar M_P^2=1/8\pi G$, Part~I's action gives it the kinetic term $-\tfrac12\bar M_P^2(\partial\phi)^2$, no potential, and the coupling
$\lambda=\dd\ln\alpha/\dd\phi$ \tagH. A body whose mass has electrostatic share $f_A$ then has $\partial\ln m_A/\partial\phi=\lambda f_A$. Exchange of
$\phi$ adds $2\lambda^2f_Af_B$ to the Newtonian force, and two bodies falling towards the Earth differ by $\eta=2\lambda^2f_\oplus(f_1-f_2)$
(Section~\ref{sec:fix};~\cite{dz}) \tagP. The Coulomb term $a_CZ(Z-1)/A^{1/3}$ of the semi-empirical mass formula gives $f_{\rm Ti}=2.02\times10^{-3}$,
$f_{\rm Pt}=4.05\times10^{-3}$, $f_{\rm Be}=4.89\times10^{-4}$ and, for a coarse Earth composition, $f_\oplus=1.71\times10^{-3}$. MICROSCOPE then
measures $\eta=k\lambda^2$ with $k=-6.94\times10^{-6}$ \chk{data:D1.1}. Because $\lambda^2\ge0$, GUID fixes the sign, $\eta\le0$ for Ti/Pt and Be/Ti, so
these tests can fail although $\lambda$ is free.

\paragraph{The coupling.} We profile $\chi^2_{\rm EP}(\lambda)=\sum_i[(k_i\lambda^2-\eta_i)/\sigma_i]^2$ over MICROSCOPE~\cite{microscope} (errors in
quadrature, $2.75\times10^{-15}$) and the E\"ot-Wash Be/Ti balance~\cite{schlamminger2008}, with $\lambda^2\ge0$. The best fit, $|\lambda|=1.47\times10^{-5}$,
is not significant, and $\Delta\chi^2=3.84$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-lambda}
|\lambda|<3.1\times10^{-5}\quad(95\%\ \mathrm{CL})\qquad\chk{data:D1.1},
\end{equation}
set by MICROSCOPE alone. The value $2\times10^{-5}$ of version 5.1 is the one-standard-deviation bound, $|\eta|<2.7\times10^{-15}$.

\paragraph{Model A: the action as written.} Without a potential, the scalar is driven only by the electromagnetic energy of matter, as in the
theories of Bekenstein~\cite{bekenstein82} and of Sandvik, Barrow and Magueijo~\cite{bsbm}. Dark matter, a gravitational remnant, carries none;
baryons carry an electrostatic share $f_b>0$. For pressureless baryons with $\partial\rho_b/\partial\phi=\lambda f_b\rho_b$, the field equation
$\bar M_P^2\Box\phi=\partial\rho_b/\partial\phi$ in a flat Friedmann universe reads, with $N=\ln a$ and $\phi'=\dd\phi/\dd N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-modelA}
\phi''+\Big(3+\frac{\dd\ln H}{\dd N}\Big)\phi'=-3\lambda f_b\,\Omega_b(N),
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_b(N)$ the baryon share of the critical density. In the matter era $\dd\ln H/\dd N=-3/2$, and every solution approaches
$\phi'=-2\lambda f_b\Omega_b/\Omega_m$ (checked numerically). Integrated through flat $\Lambda$CDM~\cite{planck18} to today, it gives
$\phi'_0=-0.141\,\lambda f_b$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-driftA}
\frac{\dot\alpha}{\alpha}\Big|_0=\lambda H_0\phi'_0=-9.70\times10^{-12}\,\lambda^2f_b\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}\le0\qquad\chk{data:D1.3}.
\end{equation}
The scalar rolls towards lower matter energy, and a positive electrostatic share makes that a fall in $\alpha$. GUID's forward statement that
$\alpha$ does not grow is therefore \vd{derived} for the action as written, but it is not observable. The helium Coulomb energy alone gives
$f_b=6.0\times10^{-5}$. Even with $f_b=10^{-3}$, a generous upper value for the electromagnetic self-energy of nucleons, and $|\lambda|$ at
the bound~\eqref{eq:data-lambda}, the drift is $-9.6\times10^{-24}$ per year, four orders of magnitude below the clocks.

\paragraph{Model B: a slowly rolling scalar.} We give the scalar a linear potential $V=V_0-s\phi$ and make it a
thawing dark energy, frozen early by Hubble friction. A canonical scalar has $\rho_\phi+p_\phi=\bar M_P^2\dot\phi^2$, so $\phi'^2=3\Omega_\phi(1+w)$
exactly. For small slopes the history has a universal shape, and each $\alpha$ datum measures the present drift $d=\dot\alpha/\alpha|_0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-G}
\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\alpha}(z)=-\frac{d}{H_0}G(z),\qquad G(z)=\int_{-\ln(1+z)}^{0}\frac{\phi'(N)}{\phi'(0)}\,\dd N .
\end{equation}
$G$ is $0.118$ at Oklo ($z=0.137$, the 1.8\,Gyr implied by the quoted bound and rate of Damour and Dyson~\cite{damour1996}), $0.435$ at $z=1.15$,
$0.514$ at $z=6.3$ and $0.516$ from recombination back; for $1+w_0=0.16$ these values change by less than 2\%. Each datum gives
$d_i=-H_0(\Delta\alpha/\alpha)_i/G(z_i)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:data-alpha}a). The clocks~\cite{filzinger2023}, which include the data of~\cite{lange}, give
$\pm2.5\times10^{-19}$ per year and Oklo~\cite{davis2015} $\pm3.3\times10^{-18}$; the quasars~\cite{espresso,wilczynska2020}, the CMB~\cite{hart2018}
and nucleosynthesis~\cite{avelino2001} are three to six orders weaker. Their weighted mean, $d=(1.79\pm2.49)\times10^{-19}$ per year ($\chi^2=3.05$ for
5 degrees of freedom), is derived and dominated by the clocks, so it is reported, not scored \chk{data:D1.12}.

\paragraph{The sign.} The data say nothing about it. The clocks dominate; their central value is positive at $0.7\sigma$; $P(d<0)=0.24$; and
the 95\% interval $[-3.1,6.7]\times10^{-19}$ per year holds both signs. In Model B, drift and coupling are linked only by the field speed,
$d=\lambda H_0\phi'_0$. If the scalar drove the evolving dark energy DESI prefers, with $1+w_0=0.162$ and so $\phi'_0=0.577$, the clocks would
demand $|\lambda|<1.7\times10^{-8}$, about two thousand times below the free-fall bound ($2.1\times10^{-7}$ for
$1+w_0=10^{-3}$) \chk{data:D1.12} (Fig.~\ref{fig:data-alpha}b). \emph{Verdict:} all eight scored rows hold. Free fall \vd{holds}, and the
coupling is bounded by~\eqref{eq:data-lambda}. The negative drift is \vd{derived} for the action as written and \vd{untested}; where a drift could
be seen, its sign is not predicted.

\subsection{D2: post-Newtonian gravity}\label{sec:data-ppn}

Minimal coupling (Section~\ref{sec:fix}) gives the post-Newtonian limit of general relativity \tagP: $\gamma=\beta=1$, a Nordtvedt parameter
$\eta_N=4(\beta-1)-(\gamma-1)=0$~\cite{fienga2024}, the gravitational redshift of the lapse, and massless classical waves at $c$ with two tensor
polarisations. Of overlapping data, Cassini (D2.1) is scored for $\gamma$ and the ephemeris combination (D2.3) for $\beta$; D2.2 and D2.4 are
INFO. All ten scored rows hold. The largest pull is
$1.06\sigma$ (lunar $\gamma$), and $\chi^2=4.68$ for 8 Gaussian pulls gives $p=0.79$, indicative only because the three lunar rows come from one
fit \chk{data:D2}. The graviton bound is at 90\% credibility, and the GWTC-3 tests also find no non-tensor polarisation~\cite{ligo2025a}.
\emph{Verdict:} \vd{holds}, and adds nothing to general relativity.

\subsection{D3: the balanced state}\label{sec:data-cosmo}

GUID's late universe is flat $\Lambda$CDM settling into de Sitter expansion, $H\to H_\infty=\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}H_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:cosmo}).
Acceleration starts at $z_t=(2\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m)^{1/3}-1$: $0.632\pm0.018$ for Planck's $\Omega_m=0.315\pm0.007$~\cite{planck18}, or $0.664\pm0.009$
for DESI DR2 with the CMB~\cite{desi2}. Its published determinations disagree with each other: $0.4\pm0.1$ from cosmic
chronometers~\cite{moresco2016} ($-2.3\sigma$), $0.72\pm0.05$ or $0.84\pm0.03$ from $H(z)$ for $H_0=68$ or 73.24, the value depending mainly on that
prior~\cite{farooq2017} ($+1.7$, $+6.0\sigma$), and $0.883\pm0.110$ from supernovae with $H(z)$~\cite{jesus2018} ($+2.2\sigma$). The test is
\vd{inconclusive} (INFO) \chk{data:D3.1}.

The direct test is the equation of state. DESI DR2 fits $w=w_0+w_a(1-a)$. With the CMB it finds $w_0=-0.42\pm0.21$ and $w_a=-1.75\pm0.58$, which
disfavours $\Lambda$CDM at $3.1\sigma$; with the three supernova samples it finds 2.8, 3.8 and $4.2\sigma$~\cite{desi2}. A recalibration of the DES
supernovae lowers its value to $3.2\sigma$, with Bayesian odds of about 5:1~\cite{popovic2025}. Scored on the weakest combination, $2.8\sigma$, the
floor FAILs \chk{data:D3.3}. The model's $H_0=67.4\pm0.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$ lies $4.9\sigma$ from the Cepheid ladder,
$73.04\pm1.04$~\cite{riess2022}, and $1.5\sigma$ from the tip of the red giant branch, $70.39\pm1.94$~\cite{freedman2025} \chk{data:D3.4,D3.5}.
Inflation predicts $|\Omega_K|\lesssim10^{-5}$. DESI with the CMB finds $\Omega_K=(2.3\pm1.1)\times10^{-3}$, $2.1\sigma$ from flat, while reporting no
significant preference. Planck with BAO, flat to 0.4\% at 95\%~\cite{planck2020x}, overlaps it through the CMB and is reported as INFO
\chk{data:D3.6,D3.7}.

\subsection{D4: inflation and the start}\label{sec:data-infl}

Part~I's start is an inflation that ends in oscillons and contains the strong split (Hypothesis~6$'$). Part~I names Starobinsky's $R^2$
inflation~\cite{starobinsky80}. It records Higgs inflation~\cite{bezrukov} as an open tension with the proposition that fixes $\gamma=1$

\subsection{D5: black holes}\label{sec:data-bh}

GUID's black holes are Kerr's (Section~\ref{sec:bh}). Christodoulou's formula~\cite{christodoulou} has no solution above $|a|=1$, spin energy is
extracted in the ergosphere, and the area never shrinks classically. Matter reaching the innermost stable orbit of a thin disk radiates
$\epsilon(a)=1-E_{\rm isco}(a)$~\cite{bpt}: $0.0377$ for $a=-1$ (retrograde), $0.0572$ for $a=0$, $0.423$ for $a\to1$ \chk{data:D5}. The Soltan argument
compares the light of all quasars with the mass in black holes today. It gives $\epsilon=0.075$ to $0.094$~\cite{shankar2009} (the
older $0.04$ to $0.16$~\cite{marconi2004} and $>0.1$~\cite{yu2002} rest on similar inputs and are INFO); individual disk fits give a mean of
$0.089$~\cite{davis2011}. Both scored values lie below the Kerr maximum and imply
typical spins of $0.40$ to $0.62$ and $0.58$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:data-kerr}a). The curve bounds the efficiency only from above: radiatively inefficient flows can
fall below $5.7\%$. All 61 measured spins of the compilation of Reynolds~\cite{reynolds2021}, 36 supermassive and 25 stellar, lie in $[-1,1]$ at 90\%.
They are measured with Kerr models capped at $a=0.998$, so this row cannot fail and is a consistency row. The ringdown of GW150914 shows an area
increase with probability 0.97~\cite{isi2021}, and that of GW250114 shows it ``to high credibility''~\cite{ligo2025b} \chk{data:D5.6,D5.7}. \emph{Verdict:} \vd{holds}, with general relativity.

\subsection{D6: Bridge B against the largest superpositions}\label{sec:data-bridgeB}

Bridge B (Section~\ref{sec:bridges}) gives the Di\'osi--Penrose rate~\cite{diosi87,penrose96} $\Gamma=E_\Delta/\hbar$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:data-EDelta}
E_\Delta=\frac G2\iint\frac{[\rho_1(\mathbf x)-\rho_2(\mathbf x)][\rho_1(\mathbf y)-\rho_2(\mathbf y)]}{|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|}\,\dd^3x\,\dd^3y
\end{equation}
for a superposition of the mass densities $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$; Part~I uses its order of magnitude $GM^2/\hbar R$. For a uniform sphere displaced by
$d=xR$, $E_\Delta=(GM^2/R)(x^2/2-3x^3/16+x^5/160)$ for $x<2$ and $(GM^2/R)(6/5-1/x)$ for $x\ge2$. For a mass smeared as a Gaussian of width $s$,
$E_\Delta=GM^2[1/(\sqrt\pi s)-\mathrm{erf}(d/2s)/d]$; both forms agree with Fourier quadrature \chk{data:method}. For each of the largest
superpositions made we take the larger of $GM^2/\hbar R$ and the full $E_\Delta/\hbar$. The full value is the bulk sphere plus every nucleus smeared
over the smallest allowed width $R_0=0.54\times10^{-10}$\,m, because a larger $R_0$ lowers the rate; an atom is its nucleus smeared over $R_0$. A run is
consistent when $\Gamma\tau<0.1$ over its observed coherence time $\tau$.

\paragraph{The smallest decisive experiment.} A uniform sphere separated by more than its size has $\tau_{\rm DP}=\hbar R/(\tfrac65GM^2)\propto
M^{-5/3}$. For silica, $\tau_{\rm DP}$ is 10\,ms at $1.2\times10^{-14}$\,kg, 1\,s at $7.6\times10^{-16}$\,kg and 100\,s at $4.8\times10^{-17}$\,kg \chk{data:D6}.
The first experiment to see Bridge B superposes such a particle by more than its diameter for that long. That is five to eight orders of

\subsection{D7, D8, D9: remnants, splits and the rest}\label{sec:data-rest}

\paragraph{Contained remnants.} The contained route makes dark matter of primordial black holes of $10^{14}$ to $10^{19}$\,kg from the start
(Section~\ref{sec:darkmatter}). For a monochromatic mass function, Gorton and Green~\cite{gortongreen} find the window from about $10^{17}$ to
$10^{22}$\,g open for all of the dark matter; it narrows for extended mass functions. Hawking evaporation products (INTEGRAL/SPI gamma rays,
Voyager~1 electrons and positrons) close it below, Subaru-HSC microlensing of M31 above. Part~I adopted its window from this one (consistency). The mass evaporated within
13.8\,Gyr, $1.7\times10^{11}$\,kg, is a factor 5.8 below the ``about $10^{15}$\,g'' of Carr et al.~\cite{carr2021}, a statement without an uncertainty
(INFO) \chk{data:D7.1-D7.3}. \emph{Verdict:} \vd{live}; the glow predicted at the light edge is what bounds the window.

\paragraph{The splits.} A unification before the start (Hypothesis~6$'$) would still let protons decay, with $\tau\sim M_X^4/(\alpha_G^2m_p^5)$, and
$M_X$ is free. The example $4.6\times10^{35}$\,yr for $M_X=10^{16}$\,GeV and $\alpha_G=1/40$ was chosen above Super-Kamiokande's
$2.4\times10^{34}$\,yr (90\% CL)~\cite{superk}, so D8.1 is a consistency row; Hyper-Kamiokande~\cite{hyperk} is the live test \chk{data:D8.1,D8.2}. At the
electroweak split the W and Z take their masses from $v=(\sqrt2G_F)^{-1/2}=246.22$\,GeV~\cite{mulan2013} \tagP. From
$1/\hat\alpha(M_Z)=127.950\pm0.017$~\cite{erler2017} and $\sin^2\hat\theta_W(M_Z)=0.23129\pm0.00004$~\cite{navas2024}, the tree-level
$m_W=(\pi\hat\alpha/\sqrt2G_F\sin^2\hat\theta_W)^{1/2}=80.22$\,GeV lies $0.18\%$ below $80.369\pm0.013$\,GeV (without CDF)~\cite{lhctev2024}, and
$m_Z=m_W/\cos\hat\theta_W=91.50$\,GeV lies $0.34\%$ above $91.1880\pm0.0020$\,GeV~\cite{navas2024}. Tree level is scheme dependent: $m_Z\cos\hat\theta_W$
gives 79.95\,GeV ($-0.52\%$) and the on-shell route from $\alpha(0)$, $G_F$ and $m_Z$ gives 80.94\,GeV ($+0.71\%$). Part~I's half per cent holds only in
the $\overline{\rm MS}$ route, so D8.3 and D8.4 are tree-level Standard Model values (INFO) \chk{data:D8.3,D8.4}. Part~I's $\alpha\approx1/128$ and its crossover are read from the measured

\subsection{The inherited tensions}\label{sec:data-tensions}

The three scored FAILs and the conditional FAIL are real tensions of established accounts that GUID adopts unchanged, and they share data.
\emph{Evolving dark energy} (D3.3): no combination reaches $5\sigma$, recalibrated DES supernovae give $3.2\sigma$, and the fitted $w$ crosses $-1$,
which no single canonical scalar can do. If the preference holds, the future
is not de Sitter and the balanced state of Section~\ref{sec:cosmo} must be read again. An information scalar rolling with that dark energy would have
to couple below $1.7\times10^{-8}$. \emph{The Hubble constant} (D3.4, $1.5\sigma$ from the TRGB) and \emph{curvature} (D3.7, called not significant by DESI and
built on D3.3's data) are known $\Lambda$CDM discrepancies. \emph{The spectral index} (D4.3, conditional): the gap of a few e-folds to P-ACT-LB comes partly from the BAO that also drive D3.3, and since the
e-folds of the pivot scale depend on reheating, it constrains the reheating as much as the potential. The plateau potential is in tension; GUID's
start, which needs inflation but not this potential, is not tested by it.

\subsection{No data yet}\label{sec:data-nodata}

No measurement reaches what tells GUID apart: mediated entanglement ($\mathcal N=0$~\cite{bose,mv}; Section~\ref{sec:bmv}), the LOCC

Tensor modes, the light-edge Hawking glow and proton decay have bounds only; nothing reaches the return of information.

\paragraph{Choices made after seeing the data.} The kinds were assigned after a first scoring; twenty-four rows scored as OK in that pass,

\paragraph{Caveats.} Read only through secondary sources: Cassini's $\gamma$ (ADS abstract), MESSENGER's $\eta_N$ (journal record), the PDG and CODATA
